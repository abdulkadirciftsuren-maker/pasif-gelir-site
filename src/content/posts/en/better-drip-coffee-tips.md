A drip coffee maker is convenient, but in many kitchens it makes worse coffee than it could. The good news: without buying a new machine, a few simple habits make your coffee noticeably better. Here are 7 ways.

> **Short answer:** Use **55–65 g of coffee per liter** (about 2 tablespoons per 6 oz) and **weigh it**. Grind **fresh and medium**. Use **good water**, **rinse the filter** and **preheat** the machine. Don't leave coffee **on the hot plate** – move it to a thermal carafe. **Clean and descale** regularly.

## 1. The right ratio: weigh your coffee

Most bad coffee is simply **too weak or too strong**. Use a scale instead of a scoop:

- **55–65 g per liter** is a good starting point.
- For a 4-cup pot (about 600 ml / 20 oz): 33–39 g.
- Work out your amount with the [coffee ratio calculator](/en/coffee-ratio-calculator/) and weigh it on a [coffee scale](/en/coffee-scale-guide/).

## 2. Grind fresh and right

- **Buy whole beans** and grind right before brewing – ground coffee goes stale fast.
- **Medium** (a little coarser than sand) suits most machines. Bitter? Slightly coarser. Sour? Slightly finer.
- See the [grinder guide](/en/best-coffee-grinder/) and [how to tell if coffee is fresh](/en/how-to-tell-if-coffee-is-fresh/).

## 3. Use good water

Coffee is about 98% water. If your tap water smells of chlorine or is very hard, use a **filter pitcher** – see [best water for coffee](/en/best-water-for-coffee/).

## 4. Fit and rinse the filter

- Use the **right size and shape** for your basket.
- **Fold the crimped seams** so the filter sits snugly and doesn't tear.
- **Rinse it with hot water** first to remove the papery taste – and pour that water away.

## 5. Preheat the machine and carafe

A cold machine often doesn't reach the right temperature in the first minutes.

- Run **one cycle with water only** – the machine and carafe warm up.
- Rinsing the carafe with hot water helps too.

## 6. Skip the hot plate and brew the right amount

- The **hot plate** keeps "cooking" the coffee – after 20–30 minutes it turns bitter. Move it to a **thermal carafe**, or choose a machine with one – see the [drip coffee maker guide](/en/drip-coffee-maker-guide/).
- Machines usually brew best at **at least half their capacity**. For small batches, use a "small batch" setting if your machine has one.
- **Swirl the carafe** before pouring – the first and last drips have different strengths.

## 7. Clean and descale regularly

- **Daily:** Rinse the basket and carafe – old coffee oils taste bitter.
- **Regularly:** Scale lowers the brewing temperature and makes coffee sour and flat. Descale as the manufacturer recommends – see [how to descale a coffee maker](/en/how-to-descale-a-coffee-maker/).

## Quick troubleshooting

| Problem | Likely cause | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bitter | Hot plate, too fine, too much coffee | Transfer to a carafe, grind a little coarser |
| Sour, flat | Scale, too coarse, cold machine | Descale, preheat, grind a little finer |
| Watery | Too little coffee | 55–65 g per liter |
| Papery taste | Filter not rinsed | Rinse the filter with hot water first |
| Grounds in the cup | Torn filter or wrong size | Right size, fold the seams |

## FAQ

**Brown or white filters?** Either works. Unbleached filters can taste a bit more of paper – rinsing helps.

**Which roast for a drip machine?** A medium roast works very well – see [coffee roast levels](/en/coffee-roast-levels/).

**Is pour-over better than a machine?** It gives you more control, but you have to stand there. Try a [V60](/en/v60-pour-over-guide/) or [Chemex](/en/how-to-use-a-chemex/).
