You don't need an expensive machine for good Turkish coffee – but you do need the right coffee. The most important rule: **Turkish coffee must be ground as fine as powder and be fresh.** Regular filter coffee grounds won't work: they don't create foam and don't settle at the bottom of the cup.

> **Short answer:** Buy **ready-ground Turkish coffee in small packs** (100–250 g), look for the **most recent date** you can find and keep it **airtight** once opened. With a well-known brand like Mehmet Efendi, you can't go far wrong.

## How to recognise good Turkish coffee

1. **Powder-fine grind.** Turkish coffee is as fine as flour – finer than espresso. The pack usually says "Türk Kahvesi" or "Turkish coffee".
2. **Freshness.** Ground coffee loses its aroma quickly. Check the roast or production date and prefer small packs you'll finish within 2–3 weeks.
3. **Roast.** The classic choice is a **medium roast** – not too light, not too dark. A roast that's too dark makes the coffee bitter.
4. **Bean type.** Traditionally it's mostly **Arabica**, often from Brazil. More on that in our guide [Arabica vs Robusta](/en/arabica-vs-robusta/).
5. **Packaging.** Vacuum packs or bags with an aroma valve keep the coffee fresh until opened.

## Well-known brands

The best-known brand is **Kurukahveci Mehmet Efendi** from Istanbul, whose history goes back to the 19th century. You'll find it in most Turkish and Middle Eastern grocery stores and online. There are also many other Turkish brands – from large producers to small roasters.

Our tip: **try two or three brands in small packs** and compare. Taste is personal – some prefer it milder, others stronger.

## Types and variations

| Variation | What is it? | Who is it for? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Classic Turkish coffee** | Pure coffee ground as fine as powder | For the traditional taste |
| **With mastic (Damla Sakızlı)** | Flavoured with mastic resin, fresh and slightly resinous | For the curious |
| **With cardamom** | Spicy note, common in the Middle East | If you like it aromatic |
| **"Ottoman coffee"** | Usually blends with spices, cocoa or mastic | Varies a lot by brand |
| **Dibek coffee** | Traditionally pounded in a mortar, often mixed with cocoa or milk powder | If you like it milder and sweeter – see [Dibek coffee](/en/dibek-coffee/) |
| **Menengiç coffee** | Not real coffee: made from wild pistachios – caffeine-free | If you want to avoid caffeine – see [Menengiç coffee](/en/menengic-coffee/) |

Learn more about regional specialities in our guide to [types of coffee](/en/types-of-coffee/).

## Where to buy

- **Turkish and Middle Eastern grocery stores:** The widest selection and usually the freshest stock, because it sells quickly.
- **Large supermarkets:** Many have an international aisle with one or two well-known brands.
- **Online:** Convenient, with a wide selection, including specialities like mastic or menengiç coffee. Look for reviews that mention freshness.

## Storing it properly

- After opening, transfer it to an **airtight, opaque canister**.
- **Don't keep it in the fridge:** moisture forms when you take it out, and the coffee absorbs odours.
- Store it somewhere cool, dry and dark – a kitchen cupboard, not next to the stove.
- Use it within **2–3 weeks**. After that, the foam gets noticeably weaker.

## FAQ

**Can I grind Turkish coffee myself?** Only with a grinder that can grind to a powder – many hand grinders can't. For most people, ready-ground coffee is the easier and better choice. More in our guide to the [best coffee grinder](/en/best-coffee-grinder/).

**Is Turkish coffee made in a moka pot?** No. Turkish coffee is simmered with the grounds in a cezve and served unfiltered. The moka pot is a different method – see [How to use a moka pot](/en/how-to-use-a-moka-pot/).

**How do I make it?** Step by step in our guide [How to make Turkish coffee](/en/how-to-make-turkish-coffee/). The right pot is covered in [How to choose a cezve](/en/how-to-choose-a-cezve/).
