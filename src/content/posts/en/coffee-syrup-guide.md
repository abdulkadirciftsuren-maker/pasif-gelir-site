Vanilla latte, caramel macchiato, hazelnut cappuccino: in many coffee shops, a splash of syrup is the reason the coffee tastes "somehow better". That's easy to recreate at home. Here you'll learn which flavors exist, how much syrup goes into a cup and what to look for when buying.

> **Short answer:** Start with **vanilla or caramel** – they go with almost any milk coffee. Use about **10–15 ml (1–2 pumps, roughly ½ oz) per cup**, put the syrup in the cup **first** and pour coffee and milk on top. If you want to cut sugar, go for a **sugar-free syrup**.

## The most popular flavors

| Flavor | Taste | Goes well with |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vanilla | Mild, sweet, rounded | Latte, cappuccino, iced coffee |
| Caramel | Sweet, slightly toasty | Latte macchiato, flat white |
| Salted caramel | Sweet and salty | Latte, iced coffee |
| Hazelnut | Nutty, warm | Cappuccino, drip coffee |
| Cinnamon / gingerbread | Spicy, wintry | Winter drinks, the holidays |
| Coconut | Tropical | Iced coffee, cold brew |
| Chocolate / mocha | Cocoa-like | Mocha, hot chocolate with espresso |

We explain the different milk drinks in [Latte vs. cappuccino vs. flat white](/en/latte-vs-cappuccino-vs-flat-white/).

## How much to use

- **Latte (300 ml / 10 oz):** 15–20 ml syrup
- **Cappuccino (180 ml / 6 oz):** 10–15 ml
- **Espresso:** 5 ml is enough
- **Iced coffee:** 15–20 ml, because cold mutes the flavor

One pump usually dispenses about 7–10 ml – check the pump for the exact amount. Start with less and add more if needed.

## How it tastes best

1. **Syrup in the cup first:** It mixes in by itself as you pour.
2. **Espresso on top**, give it a quick stir.
3. **Frothed milk** last. With a [milk frother](/en/milk-frother-guide/) you can do this without an espresso machine.
4. **Don't add syrup to boiling milk:** Some syrups can turn slightly bitter at very high temperatures or make the milk curdle.

## What to look for when buying

1. **Ingredients:** Good syrups use natural flavorings. It's worth checking the ingredient list.
2. **Sugar or sugar-free:** Classic syrup contains a lot of sugar. Sugar-free versions are sweetened with sweeteners – try a small bottle first.
3. **Glass or plastic:** Glass bottles feel more premium; plastic bottles are lighter and won't break.
4. **Pump:** Avoids mess and gives consistent amounts. Make sure it fits the bottle size.
5. **Shelf life:** Once opened, syrup usually keeps for a few weeks to months – check the label. Store it somewhere cool and dark.
6. **Sets:** Sampler sets with several small bottles are ideal for trying flavors – and make a nice [coffee gift](/en/coffee-gift-ideas/).

## Make your own syrup

Vanilla syrup is ready in 10 minutes:

1. Put **200 g (1 cup) sugar** and **200 ml (about ¾ cup + 1 tbsp) water** in a saucepan.
2. Add a **vanilla bean**, split open.
3. Bring to a boil and stir until the sugar dissolves, then simmer gently for 5 minutes.
4. Let it cool, pour into a clean bottle and keep it in the fridge. Use within about 2 weeks.

For cinnamon syrup, use 2 cinnamon sticks instead of vanilla. For caramel, first melt the sugar until golden brown, then carefully add hot water.

## Other coffee add-ins

- **Cinnamon or cocoa** for dusting the milk foam
- **Cardamom:** A classic in Arabic and Turkish coffee – more in [How to make Turkish coffee](/en/how-to-make-turkish-coffee/)
- **Plant-based milks:** Oat or barista editions froth well – more in [Oat milk for coffee](/en/oat-milk-for-coffee/)
- **Creamer or coffee whitener:** Quick, but no match for frothed milk in taste

## FAQ

**Can you add syrup to black coffee?** Yes, but the flavor stands out more. Start with 5–10 ml.

**Does syrup work with coffee from a super-automatic machine?** Yes, just put it in the cup first. Tips on the machine itself are in the [super-automatic espresso machine guide](/en/super-automatic-espresso-machine-guide/).

**Which beans go best with syrup drinks?** Medium to dark roasts with chocolatey notes. More in [How to buy coffee beans](/en/how-to-buy-coffee-beans/).
