Dibek coffee takes its name from the **dibek**, a large stone or wooden mortar in which coffee used to be pounded. It's brewed in a cezve like Turkish coffee, but it's **milder** and usually **foamier**. Today, however, most dibek coffees on sale are **blends**.

> **In short:** Traditional dibek coffee is pounded in a stone mortar and is milder than Turkish coffee. Many ready-made products also contain **cocoa, milk powder, mastic or spices**. If you want pure coffee, check the ingredient list.

## What is a dibek?

A dibek is a large, hollowed-out mortar made of stone or wood. Before grinders were widespread, roasted beans were pounded with a heavy pestle in many parts of Anatolia.

**Why milder?** Pounding warms the coffee less than a fast-running grinder – which protects the aromas. The particles are also less uniform, which gives dibek coffee its distinctive texture.

## How it differs from Turkish coffee

| | Turkish coffee | Dibek coffee |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How the powder is made | Ground as fine as powder | Traditionally pounded |
| Taste | Stronger | Milder |
| Ingredients | Usually just coffee | Often a blend |
| Foam | Plenty | Often even creamier |
| Brewed with | Water | Water or milk |

## What to look for when buying

1. **Read the ingredients.** If it only says "coffee", it's pure dibek coffee. Cocoa, milk powder, sugar, mastic, carob or spices mean it's a blend.
2. **Sugar and milk powder.** Some blends are already sweetened. If you'd rather sweeten it yourself, choose an unsweetened version.
3. **Caffeine.** Blends with a low coffee content contain less caffeine.
4. **Freshness.** Buy small packs and store them airtight – just like [Turkish coffee](/en/how-to-buy-turkish-coffee/).

## How to make it

1. **Into the cezve:** Per cup, one heaped teaspoon of dibek coffee and one Turkish coffee cup of cold water (or milk).
2. **Add sugar if you like** and stir well before heating.
3. **Heat on a low flame.** When the foam rises, take it off the heat – don't let it boil.
4. **Share out the foam**, then slowly pour in the rest.

The tricks are the same as for Turkish coffee – see [How to make Turkish coffee](/en/how-to-make-turkish-coffee/). For the right pot, see [How to choose a cezve](/en/how-to-choose-a-cezve/).

### Dibek coffee with milk

Use the same amount of **cold milk** instead of water. Milk boils over faster – so use the lowest setting and stay with it. It's especially popular in winter.

## FAQ

**Can I make dibek coffee in a Turkish coffee machine?** Yes, with water it usually works without problems. For the milk version, the machine needs a milk mode – see the [Turkish coffee machine guide](/en/turkish-coffee-machine-guide/).

**Does dibek coffee have less caffeine?** Pure dibek coffee doesn't. Blends with cocoa or milk powder contain less coffee per cup and therefore less caffeine.
