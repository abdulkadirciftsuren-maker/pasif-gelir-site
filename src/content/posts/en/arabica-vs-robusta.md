Almost all the coffee in the world comes from two species: **Arabica** (*Coffea arabica*) and **Robusta** (*Coffea canephora*). You've probably seen "100% Arabica" on bags – but does that automatically mean better? In short: **Arabica is more aromatic and smoother; Robusta is stronger, more caffeinated and cheaper.** Which suits you depends on how you drink your coffee.

> **Short answer:** For filter, V60 or black coffee – **Arabica**. For milk drinks, moka pot or espresso with a full body and thick crema, a **blend with some Robusta** can be the better choice.

## The differences at a glance

| | Arabica | Robusta |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Taste | Sweet, fruity, floral, pleasant acidity | Strong, bitter, earthy, woody, nutty |
| Caffeine | Lower | About twice as much |
| Body and crema | Lighter | Fuller, thick crema in espresso |
| Bean shape | Oval, S-shaped crease | Round, straight crease |
| Grown | High altitude, cool climate | Low altitude, hot climate |
| Hardiness | Sensitive to disease and pests | Resistant – hence the name |
| Price | Higher | Lower |
| Share of world production | Around 60% | Around 40% |

## Arabica

Arabica originates in **Ethiopia**. It grows slowly at high altitude in cool, shady conditions, which lets more sugars and aromatic compounds develop in the bean – that's why Arabica can taste of fruit, chocolate, caramel or flowers. Because it's disease-prone and lower-yielding, it costs more. Nearly all specialty coffee is Arabica.

**Top producers:** Brazil, Colombia, Ethiopia, Honduras, Peru.

## Robusta

Robusta comes from **Central and West Africa**. It grows in hot lowlands, resists disease and yields well. Its high caffeine is actually a natural defence against insects – and makes it taste more bitter. Robusta's bad reputation comes mostly from cheap, carelessly processed lots. Carefully grown, high-quality Robusta exists and is deliberately used in Italian espresso blends for **thick crema and full body**. Most instant coffee also contains Robusta.

**Top producers:** Vietnam (by far), Brazil, Indonesia, Uganda.

## How to tell in the shop

1. **Read the label.** "100% Arabica" means no Robusta. "Coffee", "blend" or no species at all usually means a mix. Brands that state the ratio are more transparent.
2. **"Espresso", "intense" or "crema" bags** are usually dark-roasted and often contain Robusta. A high intensity rating hints at more Robusta.
3. **Very cheap bags** are probably Robusta-heavy. Not necessarily bad – but expect a harsher taste.
4. **Look at the beans.** Oval with an S-crease is Arabica; round with a straight crease is Robusta. Blends contain both.
5. **Check the roast date.** Whatever the species, stale coffee won't taste good. Prefer bags with a **roast date** rather than just a best-before date.

## Which coffee for which method?

| Method | Our recommendation |
| --- | --- |
| V60, Chemex, filter | 100% Arabica, light to medium roast |
| French press | Arabica, medium roast |
| Moka pot | Arabica or a blend with a little Robusta |
| Espresso and milk drinks | Blend (for body and crema) |
| Turkish coffee | Usually Arabica-heavy, depends on the brand |
| Cold brew | Arabica; chocolatey coffees work very well |

## Are there other species?

Yes, but they're rare. **Liberica** has large, irregular beans and a woody, fruity taste, and is drunk mainly in the Philippines and Malaysia. **Excelsa** is now classified as a variety of Liberica, known for a tart, fruity flavour.

## FAQ

**Is Arabica really better?** It's usually more aromatic and less bitter, so most high-quality coffee is Arabica. But "better" is personal – if you like strong, creamy coffee, a good Robusta blend may make you happier.

**Which keeps you more awake?** Robusta – about twice the caffeine. Caffeine per cup also depends on method and serving size.

**Is Turkish coffee Arabica?** Traditionally Turkish coffee is mostly Arabica, often Brazilian. Some brands use blends – check the label.
