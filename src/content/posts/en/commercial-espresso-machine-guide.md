The two most expensive and most important tools in a café are the **espresso machine** and the **grinder**. Choose badly and you get queues at rush hour, inconsistent coffee and frequent repairs. This guide explains in plain language **how many groups** you need, the **boiler types**, what to plan for **power and water**, how to choose a **grinder**, which **brands** are common and what they roughly **cost**.

> **Short answer:** For most neighborhood cafés, a **2-group**, **volumetric** machine with an **on-demand** espresso grinder is the right start. Don't skimp on the grinder: a **good grinder + solid machine** beats an **expensive machine + cheap grinder**. Above all, pick a brand with **service near you**.

*Note: Prices are rough 2026 ranges in Europe, excluding VAT. Always get quotes from several suppliers.*

## 1. How many groups do you need?

The **group head** is where the portafilter locks in. The number of groups decides how many coffees you can brew at once.

| Coffees per day | Recommendation | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **50–150** (kiosk, small café) | 1 or 2 groups | If you plan to grow, start with 2 |
| **150–400** (neighborhood café) | **2 groups** | The most common choice; two baristas work comfortably |
| **400+** (busy street, station) | 3 groups or 2 machines | A second grinder is a must |

**Tip:** Plan for your **busiest hour**, not the average. If you sell 60 coffees between 8 and 9 a.m., there shouldn't be a queue in that hour.

## 2. Boiler type: heat exchanger or multi-boiler?

- **Heat exchanger (HX):** One large steam boiler; the brew water is heated through a tube running through it. Robust, common and affordable. The temperature moves a little, but it's fine for most cafés.
- **Multi-boiler:** Each group has its own boiler with its own temperature. **More consistent espresso** and different temperatures for different beans. Ideal for specialty cafés, but pricier.

## 3. Volumetric or manual?

- **Volumetric:** The machine measures the water for each shot and stops by itself, so the barista can steam milk meanwhile. **The right choice for most cafés.**
- **Semi-automatic (manual):** The barista starts and stops each shot. Needs an experienced team; mistakes go up at rush hour.
- **Gravimetric:** A scale in the drip tray measures the espresso **in grams**. The most consistent, but only on high-end machines.

## 4. Steam, hot water and daily use

- **Steam power:** If you sell lots of milk drinks, the steam boiler size (liters) matters. A small boiler weakens when you make several lattes in a row.
- **Auto steam wand:** Stops at the set milk temperature. Saves time and milk, especially with new baristas.
- **Height under the group:** To-go cups need to fit underneath.
- **Cup warmer and hot water tap:** A separate hot water tap speeds up Americanos and tea.

## 5. Power, water and drain

Sort these out with your landlord and tradespeople **before you buy**:

- **Power:** Most 2- and 3-group machines need a **three-phase** connection (400 V in Europe). If it's missing, talk to an electrician and budget for it. In the US, check the required voltage and amperage with the supplier.
- **Plumbed-in water:** Commercial machines connect directly to the water line. Low pressure means you need a pump.
- **Water filter:** Scale is the most common cause of repairs. A **filter system** matched to your water is a must. See [best water for coffee](/en/best-water-for-coffee/) and [how to descale a coffee maker](/en/how-to-descale-a-coffee-maker/).
- **Drain:** The drip tray needs a drain connection under the counter.

## 6. The grinder: half of the flavor

For home grinders, see our [best coffee grinder](/en/best-coffee-grinder/) guide. In a café, other things matter:

- **On-demand:** Grinds fresh for each order. Old-style **doser** grinders grind ahead of time and the aroma fades.
- **Burr size:** Bigger burrs (64 → 75 → 83 mm) grind faster and heat up less. Busy cafés usually go for **75 mm or more**.
- **Retention:** Coffee that stays inside the grinder. Less retention means less waste when you change the setting.
- **Time or weight?** Timed grinders are cheaper; **grind-by-weight** models are more consistent.
- **Second grinder:** If you sell **decaf** or **filter coffee**, you need a second, maybe third grinder. For filter, a dedicated grinder (e.g. Mahlkönig EK43) and a **batch brewer** help a lot at rush hour.

## 8. New, used, rented or leased

- **New:** Comes with warranty and installation. The safest but most expensive route.
- **Used / refurbished:** Often half the new price. Check the **service history**, **scale build-up** and **spare parts**; ideally have a technician inspect it.
- **Rent / lease / rent-to-own:** Saves cash at the start. Compare the total cost with buying.
- **Machine from your roaster:** Some roasters provide the machine and grinder free or cheap if you commit to **buying a set amount of coffee for a set time**. Attractive for a start, but read the **term, minimum purchase and exit terms** carefully.

## 9. Maintenance: make it last

| When? | What? |
| --- | --- |
| **After every shot** | Flush the group briefly, wipe the portafilter |
| **Every evening** | **Backflush** with a blind basket and cleaner, clean the steam wands |
| **Weekly** | Remove shower screens and baskets, soak in cleaning solution |
| **Monthly** | Brush the grinder burrs or use grinder cleaning pellets |
| **Every 3–12 months** | Replace the water filter cartridge (depending on use) |
| **Yearly** | Technician service: gaskets, valves, pressure and temperature |

## Checklist before you buy

- [ ] I know how many coffees I'll sell in my **busiest hour**.
- [ ] **Power**, **plumbed-in water** and a **drain** are in place or planned.
- [ ] There's **service** and **spare parts** for the brand near me.
- [ ] Part of the budget is set aside for the **grinder** (and a second one if needed).
- [ ] The **water filter** is included in the quote or planned separately.

## FAQ

**Can I open a café with a home espresso machine?** Not a real café. Home machines are too slow for constant use and break quickly. For home use: [home espresso machine guide](/en/home-espresso-machine-guide/).

**Is a super-automatic machine OK for a café?** Where coffee is a side business (bakery, office, hotel breakfast), yes. If coffee is your main business, a portafilter machine makes the difference in both taste and presentation.

**Which brand is best?** The one with **good service near you**. Even the best machine is useless if no technician shows up on the day it breaks.

**How long does a commercial machine last?** Well maintained, **10–15 years**. A water filter and yearly service are the key.
