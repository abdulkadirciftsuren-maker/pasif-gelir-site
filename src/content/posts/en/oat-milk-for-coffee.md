Oat milk has become one of the most popular dairy alternatives in coffee. It tastes mild and slightly sweet and goes well with espresso. But not every oat milk froths equally well, and some curdle in coffee. Here's what matters.

> **Short answer:** For cappuccinos and lattes, use a **barista edition** – it froths finer and rarely curdles. Froth it **well chilled** and **shaken**, and don't heat it **above about 65 °C (150 °F)**. For black coffee with a splash of oat, regular oat milk is fine.

## Barista edition or regular oat milk?

| | Regular oat milk | Barista edition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Foam | Coarse, collapses quickly | Fine, stable, latte art possible |
| Curdling in coffee | Can happen | Rare |
| Taste | Light, oaty | A little creamier, fuller |
| Price | Cheaper | A little more expensive |
| Ideal for | Cereal, black coffee | Cappuccino, latte, flat white |

Barista versions usually contain a little **plant oil** for creaminess and an **acidity regulator** so they don't curdle in acidic coffee.

## Why does plant milk curdle in coffee?

Coffee is slightly acidic and hot. When acidity and big temperature differences meet the protein in plant milk, it can curdle. It's harmless, but doesn't look nice. How to avoid it:

1. Use a **barista edition**.
2. **Warm or froth the milk first**, then add the coffee – don't pour cold milk into very hot coffee.
3. **Milder roasts:** Very light, acidic coffees make plant milks more likely to curdle. Tips on roasts in [How to buy coffee beans](/en/how-to-buy-coffee-beans/).

## How to froth it properly

1. **Cold from the fridge:** Cold oat milk froths better.
2. **Shake well:** Ingredients settle in the carton.
3. **55–65 °C (130–150 °F):** Above that, the foam gets coarse and the taste suffers. The pitcher should feel warm, but not too hot.
4. **With a steam wand,** hold the tip just below the surface until the volume increases a little, then dip it deeper and let the milk swirl.
5. **Without an espresso machine:** An electric [milk frother](/en/milk-frother-guide/) works very well with barista oat milk.

How cappuccino, latte and flat white differ is explained in [Latte vs cappuccino vs flat white](/en/latte-vs-cappuccino-vs-flat-white/).

## Everyday tips

- **Opened cartons** belong in the fridge and should be used within a few days – check the label.
- **Stocking up:** Unopened shelf-stable cartons usually keep for a long time. Multipacks are often cheaper per liter.
- **Unsweetened or sweetened:** For coffee, an unsweetened or lightly sweetened version is usually best. If you like it sweet, add a splash of [coffee syrup](/en/coffee-syrup-guide/) instead.
- **Super-automatic machines:** Many machines froth oat milk well. Rinse the milk system after every use. More in the [super-automatic espresso machine guide](/en/super-automatic-espresso-machine-guide/).

## FAQ

**Can I use oat milk in a capsule machine with a milk system?** Yes, if the machine uses fresh milk. It won't work with milk capsules (powder). More in the [capsule coffee machine guide](/en/capsule-coffee-machine-guide/).

**Is oat milk healthier than cow's milk?** It depends on your needs. Oat milk is lactose-free and plant-based, but contains less protein. Check the ingredients list.

**Why does my oat cappuccino taste sweet?** During production, some of the oat starch is converted into sugar. That's why oat milk tastes naturally a little sweet – even without added sugar.
