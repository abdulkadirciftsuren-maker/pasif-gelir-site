There's no single "best coffee maker": the perfect machine for someone who drinks a pot of black coffee each morning can be the worst choice for someone who has two cappuccinos a day. In this guide we put the **types of coffee makers** side by side: price, **cost per cup**, effort and who each one suits. At the end you'll find **specific picks** for every type.

> **Short answer:** If you mostly drink **black coffee**, a good **drip coffee maker** gives you the most flavor for your money. For **speed and zero mess**, a **capsule machine**; for daily **milk drinks**, a **super-automatic**; if you enjoy the craft, an **espresso machine plus a grinder**. If you love Turkish coffee, a dedicated **Turkish coffee machine** is the easiest way.

## First, ask yourself 3 questions

1. **What do you drink?** Drip coffee, espresso, cappuccino or Turkish coffee? That's half the decision.
2. **How many cups a day?** For heavy drinkers, cost per cup matters more than the price of the machine.
3. **How much effort do you want?** Press a button and go, or play with grind size and brew time?

## Our picks by type

### Drip coffee maker: for black coffee drinkers

A machine that brews at the right temperature (195–205 °F / 92–96 °C) tastes very different from the bitter or watery coffee of cheap models. The **Moccamaster** is the classic here, but there are good cheaper options too. More: [drip coffee maker guide](/en/drip-coffee-maker-guide/).

### Capsule machine: the fastest, cleanest way

Pop in a capsule, press the button, done. The **Nespresso Essenza Mini** fits any small kitchen; the **Vertuo** line also makes large mugs. Downsides: cost per cup and capsule waste. More: [capsule coffee machine guide](/en/capsule-coffee-machine-guide/).

### Super-automatic: cappuccino at the touch of a button

It grinds the beans itself and makes espresso and (depending on the model) milk. The **De'Longhi Magnifica S** is a solid start for black coffee; if you drink cappuccino every day, an easy-to-clean milk system like **Philips LatteGo** is worth it. More: [super-automatic espresso machine guide](/en/super-automatic-espresso-machine-guide/).

### Espresso machine: for people who enjoy the craft

With a portafilter machine you control everything: grind, dose, tamping, milk foam. The **De'Longhi Dedica** is an affordable start, and the **Breville Bambino Plus** heats up in seconds and froths milk automatically. But remember: a **good grinder** matters at least as much as the machine. More: [home espresso machine guide](/en/home-espresso-machine-guide/) and [best coffee grinder](/en/best-coffee-grinder/).

### Turkish coffee machine: no boil-overs, lots of foam

For anyone who doesn't want to stand over a cezve. The **Arzum Okka Minio** is small and practical; there are multi-cup models for bigger families. More: [Turkish coffee machine guide](/en/turkish-coffee-machine-guide/).

### No machine: lots of flavor for little money

You don't need an electric machine. With a [French press](/en/how-to-use-a-french-press/), [moka pot](/en/moka-pot-buying-guide/), [AeroPress](/en/how-to-use-an-aeropress/) or V60 you can make excellent coffee for $20–60. Freshly ground beans make the biggest difference here too.

## Which situation, which machine?

- **I live alone and want a quick espresso in the morning:** capsule machine or a small moka pot.
- **There are two of us and we drink a pot in the morning:** drip coffee maker.
- **Everyone at home drinks something different, lots of cappuccino:** super-automatic with a milk system.
- **Coffee is my hobby:** espresso machine plus a good grinder.
- **We often have guests and Turkish coffee is a must:** Turkish coffee machine.
- **I'm a student on a budget:** French press or AeroPress.

## Example: cost per year

For someone who drinks **2 cups a day**, roughly:

| | Per cup | Per year (730 cups) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Capsules** | ~$0.55 | **~$400** |
| **Whole beans (super-automatic, drip, espresso)** | ~$0.25 | **~$180** |

So a capsule machine is cheap to buy but costs more over the years. If you drink a lot of coffee, a bean-to-cup machine pays for itself within a few years. To work out your own recipe, try our [coffee ratio calculator](/en/coffee-ratio-calculator/).

## Checklist before you buy

- [ ] **Water and scale:** Is your water hard? Scale is every machine's worst enemy. See [best water for coffee](/en/best-water-for-coffee/) and [how to descale a coffee maker](/en/how-to-descale-a-coffee-maker/).
- [ ] **Cleaning:** Are the brew unit and milk system easy to clean?
- [ ] **Grinder:** Buying an espresso machine? Budget for a grinder too.
- [ ] **Size:** Measure the height under your wall cabinets.
- [ ] **Noise:** Early riser? Pay attention to the grinder noise.
- [ ] **Service and parts:** Are service and spare parts easy to get for the brand?

## FAQ

**What is the best coffee maker?** It depends on what you drink: drip for black coffee, super-automatic for milk drinks, capsule for speed, espresso machine as a hobby.

**Capsule or super-automatic?** At less than one cup a day, capsules are practical and good enough. From two cups a day, a super-automatic pays for itself in a few years and tastes better with fresh beans.

**Does an expensive machine make better coffee?** Not always. Most of the flavor comes from **fresh beans**, the **right grind** and a **clean machine**. Pricier models mostly add convenience.

**Which one makes a good gift?** If you don't know what they drink, a small capsule machine or a French press is a safe choice. More ideas: [coffee gift ideas](/en/coffee-gift-ideas/).
