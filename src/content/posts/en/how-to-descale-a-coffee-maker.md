Scale (limescale) is the hidden enemy of every coffee maker. It builds up in the tubes and heating element, the machine takes longer and gets louder – and the coffee tastes flat and no longer gets properly hot. Tap water is hard in many places, so regular descaling extends the life of your machine and improves the taste.

> **Short answer:** Descale with a **coffee maker descaler** or with **citric acid in cold water**. **Vinegar** isn't a good idea for most machines. With hard water, descale roughly **every 4–6 weeks**; with soft water, every few months. For super-automatic machines, always use the **descaling program** and the product the manufacturer recommends.

## Signs it's time

- The coffee runs through **more slowly** than usual.
- The machine gets **louder**, gurgles or hisses more.
- The coffee is **no longer really hot**.
- You see **white spots or flakes** in the water tank or carafe.
- The coffee tastes **flat or slightly metallic**.
- On super-automatic machines: the **descale indicator** lights up.

## How hard is your water?

Your **local water utility** usually publishes water hardness on its website or in its annual water quality report – searching for "water hardness" plus your town is often enough. Simple test strips also work. Hardness is often given in mg/L (ppm) or grains per gallon (gpg).

| Hardness | Approx. mg/L (ppm) | Descale roughly every … |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Soft | under 150 (under ~9 gpg) | 3–4 months |
| Medium | 150–250 (~9–15 gpg) | 6–8 weeks |
| Hard | over 250 (over ~15 gpg) | 4–6 weeks |

If you brew several pots a day, descale more often. On super-automatic machines you can set the water hardness in the menu – the machine will then remind you itself.

## What to descale with: the options compared

| Product | Pros | Cons | Suitable for |

| **Coffee maker descaler** | Made for the machines, gentle on seals, clear dosage | More expensive | All machines, especially super-automatics |
| **Citric acid** | Inexpensive, odorless | Don't use hot – it can form new deposits | Drip machines, kettles, moka pots, cezves |

| **Baking soda** | Good for cleaning | Barely removes scale | Better for carafes and mugs |

**Important:** For super-automatic machines, espresso machines and many pod machines, the manufacturer often specifies a particular descaler. Using something else may put your **warranty** at risk if the machine is damaged. Check the user manual.

## Step by step: descaling a drip coffee maker

1. **Mix the descaling solution.** Mix the descaler with **cold water** as directed on the package. With citric acid: dissolve the powder in cold water as directed on the package.
2. **Fill the water tank.** About halfway, or up to the mark for a full carafe.
3. **Run it without coffee or a paper filter.** Put the carafe in place and switch the machine on.
4. **Pause halfway.** Switch the machine off for 10–15 minutes halfway through so the solution can work. Then let the rest run through.
5. **Pour the solution away.**
6. **Rinse twice with clean water.** Fill the tank with fresh water each time and run a full cycle. This removes any residue and taste.
7. **Wash the carafe and filter basket** with warm water and dish soap.

## Other machines

- **Super-automatic espresso machine:** Always start the **descaling program** from the menu and follow the on-screen instructions. Use the manufacturer's product or one it explicitly approves. Remove any water filter first.
- **Pod machine:** Usually has its own descaling procedure (a button combination). Follow the manual.
- **Electric kettle:** Fill with citric acid in cold water, let it sit, pour it out, rinse thoroughly, then boil one batch of fresh water and discard it.
- **Moka pot:** Fill the water chamber with citric acid solution, let it sit, then rinse thoroughly. More on care in our [moka pot guide](/en/how-to-use-a-moka-pot/).
- **Turkish coffee machine:** Descale models with a water tank according to the manufacturer's instructions – more in our [Turkish coffee machine guide](/en/turkish-coffee-machine-guide/).
- **Cezve:** Occasionally wipe it out with citric acid or a little vinegar and rinse well. With copper, protect the tin lining – see [How to choose a cezve](/en/how-to-choose-a-cezve/).

## How to prevent scale

- **Use filtered water.** A water filter pitcher reduces scale – and coffee often tastes cleaner too.
- **Don't leave water standing in the tank.** Fill it with fresh water every day.
- **Let the machine dry after use** and leave the tank lid open.
- **Set the water hardness correctly on super-automatic machines** so the reminder comes on time.

**Cleaning beyond descaling:** A daily, weekly and monthly plan for coffee oils, milk systems and brew units: [how to clean a coffee maker](/en/how-to-clean-a-coffee-maker/).

## FAQ

**Can I descale with vinegar?** It's possible, but most manufacturers don't recommend it. Vinegar can attack rubber seals and metal parts, and the smell lingers for a long time. Citric acid or a dedicated descaler is the better choice.

**Why only use citric acid cold?** When citric acid is heated strongly, it can form hard-to-dissolve deposits with the scale that clog the tubes. So mix it with cold or lukewarm water, and for machines that heat water to high temperatures, use a dedicated descaler instead.

**What happens if I never descale?** The machine heats less efficiently, uses more electricity, the coffee turns lukewarm and flat – and eventually tubes clog or the heating element fails.

**Does scale affect the taste?** Yes. Very hard water makes coffee taste flat and dulls the aromas. If you use filtered water, you'll often taste a clear difference with a [V60](/en/v60-pour-over-guide/).
