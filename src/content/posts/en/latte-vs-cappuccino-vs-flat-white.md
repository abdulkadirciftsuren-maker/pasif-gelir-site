The three most popular café drinks are made from the same two ingredients: **espresso and milk.** What sets them apart is how much milk is used and how much it is foamed. Once you've seen the difference, you'll never hesitate at the menu again.

> **Short answer:** **Latte** is the milkiest and mildest. **Cappuccino** has less milk and a thick, airy foam. **Flat white** is the smallest and most coffee-forward, with a fine, velvety foam.

## Cappuccino

The classic of Italian coffee culture, traditionally described as three roughly equal layers: **espresso, hot milk and thick foam.** The airy foam makes the first sip light and fluffy. Often dusted with cocoa or cinnamon. In Italy it's mostly a morning drink.

## Flat white

Generally said to have emerged in the 1980s in the coffee cultures of Australia and New Zealand – both countries claim it. Made in a smaller cup, usually with a **double espresso** and a **little very finely textured milk (microfoam)**. The foam is so fine the surface looks flat and glossy – hence *flat*. Less milk and more espresso means the coffee flavour comes first.

## Latte

*Caffè latte* is Italian for "coffee with milk": **lots of steamed milk** with a **thin layer of foam**. The biggest and mildest of the three. Flavoured lattes (vanilla, caramel, hazelnut) and the iced latte build on it. Tip: order just a "latte" in Italy and you may get a glass of milk – ask for a "caffè latte".

## Caffeine and calories

**Caffeine** depends on the **number of espresso shots**, not the milk. A single-shot latte and a single-shot cappuccino have about the same caffeine. A flat white is usually made with a double shot, so despite its small size it can contain more.

**Calories** come almost entirely from the milk, so a latte is usually the most caloric. Plant milks or reduced-fat milk lower calories but change the foam.

## How to make them at home

1. **Brew strong coffee.** Without an espresso machine, the best option is a [moka pot](/en/how-to-use-a-moka-pot/). Very strong French press coffee also works.
2. **Heat the milk** to about 60 °C – too hot to hold comfortably, but not boiling. Boiled milk loses sweetness and foams poorly.
3. **Froth it** with a handheld or electric frother. No frother? Shake the milk in a jar with a lid for 30 seconds, or pump a French press plunger up and down quickly.
4. **Combine:**
   - **Cappuccino:** some milk, lots of foam on top.
   - **Latte:** lots of milk, a thin layer of foam.
   - **Flat white:** a little milk, froth only briefly, then tap and swirl the jug to remove big bubbles.

> **Plant milk tip:** "Barista" oat milks usually give the best foam. Regular almond milk foams easily but collapses quickly.

## FAQ

**Which is strongest?** The flat white – less milk and usually a double shot.

**Where do macchiato and cortado fit?** A macchiato is espresso with a spoonful of foam; a cortado is espresso with roughly equal warm milk. Both are smaller and stronger. All other types: [Types of coffee](/en/types-of-coffee/).

**Which one is best for latte art?** You need fine, glossy microfoam, so latte and flat white are easiest; a cappuccino's thick foam makes it harder.
