Got a new espresso machine but your shots taste **sour**, **bitter** or **watery**? Don't worry, that's completely normal. Espresso changes a lot with tiny adjustments. This guide covers **the basic recipe**, **a step-by-step method** and **how to fix the most common problems**.

> **Short answer:** Pull about **36 g** of espresso from **18 g** of finely ground coffee in **25–30 seconds** (a **1:2** ratio), with water at **195–200 °F (92–94 °C)**. If the shot is **sour and fast**, grind **finer**; if it's **bitter and slow**, grind **coarser**. Change **one thing at a time**.

## The basic recipe

| Setting | Value |
| --- | --- |
| **Dose (coffee in)** | 18 g (double shot; 14–16 g in a smaller basket) |
| **Yield (espresso out)** | 36 g (about 1.3 oz / 40 ml with crema) |
| **Ratio** | 1:2 |
| **Time** | 25–30 seconds (from when the pump starts) |
| **Water temperature** | 195–200 °F / 92–94 °C (if adjustable) |
| **Grind** | Fine; between flour and table salt |

**Why a scale?** Espresso is measured in **grams**, not ounces or ml, because crema throws off the volume. A scale with a timer makes it far easier. See [coffee scale guide](/en/coffee-scale-guide/).

## Step by step

1. **Heat the machine:** The machine and portafilter need to be properly hot: 30 seconds to 20 minutes depending on the model. Leave the portafilter locked in so it heats too.
2. **Warm your cup:** Rinse it with hot water or keep it on top of the machine.
3. **Weigh and grind:** Grind 18 g of beans right before brewing.
4. **Distribute:** Spread the coffee evenly in the basket. Stirring with a fine needle tool (WDT) breaks up clumps.
5. **Tamp:** Hold the tamper level and press firmly. Being **level** matters more than force.
6. **Flush the group head:** Run water for a few seconds to clear old grounds and stabilize the temperature.
7. **Lock in and start right away:** Lock in the portafilter, put the cup on the scale, zero the scale and timer, and start the shot.
8. **Stop at 36 g:** Check the time. Around 25–30 seconds? Great.
9. **Taste it:** Drink it straight first. Sweet, balanced, a little fruity? Sour? Bitter?
10. **Clean up:** Knock out the puck and rinse the portafilter.

## Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Cause | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Sour, watery, under 20 seconds** | Under-extraction; water runs through too fast | Grind **finer** or add 0.5–1 g of coffee |
| **Bitter, dry, over 35 seconds** | Over-extraction; water runs too slowly | Grind **coarser** or use slightly less coffee |
| **Sour and bitter at once** | Channeling: water finds a path and rushes through one spot | Distribute better (WDT), tamp level |
| **Fast, pale flow at the edges** | Channeling | Same: distribute and tamp level |
| **No or little crema** | Stale beans or grind too coarse | Use fresh beans roasted 1–4 weeks ago |
| **Not even dripping** | Far too fine or too much coffee | Grind coarser, reduce the dose |
| **Flat and dull** | Very dark roast or water too hot | Try a medium roast, lower the temperature by 2–3 °F |

**Golden rule:** Change **one thing at a time**. First hit the time with the grind, then adjust the ratio to taste.

## How to dial in

Every new bag of beans needs a fresh setting, which baristas call **"dialing in"**:

1. Start with 18 g in, stop at 36 g out.
2. **Under 20 seconds:** set the grinder a few steps **finer**.
3. **Over 35 seconds:** a few steps **coarser**.
4. At 25–30 seconds, taste:
   - Still a bit sour? Stretch the ratio to **1:2.2–1:2.5** (e.g. 40–45 g).
   - A bit bitter or too intense? Shorten it to **1:1.8** (e.g. 32 g).
5. Write down the setting you like.

**The grinder matters:** Espresso needs a grinder that goes very fine with precise steps. You can't dial in with pre-ground coffee. See [best coffee grinder](/en/best-coffee-grinder/).

## Espresso variations

| Drink | Ratio | Time | Taste |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Ristretto** | 1:1–1:1.5 | 15–20 s | Very intense, sweet, less bitter |
| **Espresso** | 1:2 | 25–30 s | Balanced |
| **Lungo** | 1:3 | 30–40 s | Lighter, more bitter |

## Using a pressurized basket?

Many entry-level machines come with a **pressurized (dual-wall) basket**. It makes "crema" even with pre-ground coffee, but flavor stays limited and dialing in barely helps. If you have a good grinder, switch to a **standard (single-wall) basket**; that's when the settings above really work. ## FAQ

**How many ml is an espresso?** A single shot is about 25–30 ml (1 oz), a double 40–60 ml. Crema makes volume misleading, so **grams** are more accurate.

**How hard should I tamp?** Firmly and level, until the coffee won't compress any further. Extra force barely changes the taste; **level** matters more.

**Which beans are best for espresso?** To start, a **medium roast** blend or chocolatey coffees from Brazil or Colombia, roasted **1–4 weeks ago**. See [how to buy coffee beans](/en/how-to-buy-coffee-beans/).

**Does a moka pot make espresso?** No, its pressure is far too low. It makes strong coffee, but not espresso. See [moka pot vs French press vs pour over](/en/moka-pot-vs-french-press-vs-pour-over/).
