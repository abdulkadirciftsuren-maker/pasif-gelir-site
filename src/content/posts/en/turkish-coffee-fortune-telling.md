In many Turkish homes, turning the coffee cup over onto its saucer is the best part of the visit. **Coffee cup reading** ("kahve falı") is a centuries-old tradition: people see birds, roads and hearts in the grounds and spin stories from them. Here's **how the cup is turned and read**, and what the best-known **symbols traditionally mean**.

> **Note:** Coffee cup reading is a **tradition for fun**. It doesn't tell you anything real about the future, so please don't base important decisions on it. The real magic is the conversation over a cup of coffee.

## The right coffee

Good patterns need a good Turkish coffee first: lots of foam and enough grounds at the bottom.

1. Use **fresh, powder-fine Turkish coffee**. See [how to buy Turkish coffee](/en/how-to-buy-turkish-coffee/).
2. Per cup: **1 demitasse cup of cold water** and **1 heaped teaspoon of coffee**. Less water gives denser grounds.
3. Heat **slowly over low heat** and don't let it boil. Full recipe: [how to make Turkish coffee](/en/how-to-make-turkish-coffee/).
4. **Sip slowly** and don't stir up the grounds. A sip of thick coffee stays at the bottom.

**Does the cezve matter?** Yes: a pot that spreads heat evenly gives better foam and neater grounds. See [how to choose a cezve](/en/how-to-choose-a-cezve/).

## How to turn the cup

1. **Make a wish:** after the last sip, think of a wish or a question.
2. **Place the saucer on top of the cup.**
3. Gently swirl cup and saucer a few times **toward yourself** (clockwise in some regions) so the grounds coat the inside.
4. **Flip it over** in one smooth move.
5. Some people put a **ring or coin** on the upturned cup to help it cool.
6. **Wait until the cup is cold**, usually **5–10 minutes**. If the bottom feels cool, it's ready.

## Zones of the cup

Traditionally, each part of the cup tells a different story:

| Zone | Traditional meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Rim** | The near future, things coming soon |
| **Bottom** | The past, the distant future, or deep feelings |
| **Handle side** | The person themselves, their home and loved ones |
| **Opposite the handle** | The outside world, work, strangers and faraway places |
| **Right side** | Positive developments (in some readings) |
| **Left side** | Things left behind, unfinished business |
| **Saucer** | Wishes, and whether they'll come true |

**Light and dark areas:** clean white areas are read as relief and "an open road"; thick, dark grounds as worries, tiredness or a busy time.

**The secret of reading:** symbols are read **together**. A bird near the handle with a letter next to it might mean "news is coming soon from someone whose name starts with that letter."

## The "heart" (the wish)

At the end, the reader traditionally presses a **thumb** into the bottom of the cup and lifts it. The print is read as the "heart":

- **Clean and clear:** a peaceful heart; the wish will come true
- **Smudged and runny:** worries inside; the wish needs more time

## Reading the saucer

The grounds that ran onto the saucer are read too: a **clean** saucer means the wish comes easily, a **full, messy** one that the road is a little longer.

## A little etiquette

- You don't read **your own** cup; someone else reads it, as part of the conversation.
- Customs vary from region to region and family to family.
- Good things are highlighted, bad things said gently, so the conversation stays fun.
- As a Turkish saying goes: "Don't believe in fortune telling, but don't go without it either." 🙂

## FAQ

**How long should I wait before opening the cup?** Until it's cold, usually 5–10 minutes. Opened too early, the grounds run and the shapes blur.

**Why can't I see any shapes?** The coffee was too watery or swirled too hard. Use less water, a finer grind, and sip slowly.

**Which coffee works best?** Fresh, powder-fine Turkish coffee. Coarser [dibek coffee](/en/dibek-coffee/) also leaves lovely patterns. [Menengiç coffee](/en/menengic-coffee/) is usually made with milk, so it's less suited.

**Does it work with a Turkish coffee machine?** Yes. Machines make good foam and even grounds. See our [Turkish coffee machine guide](/en/turkish-coffee-machine-guide/).
