A coffee scale is the cheapest accessory that improves your coffee the most. Depending on the roast and grind, a measuring scoop can easily hold 30% more or less coffee – no wonder the same coffee sometimes tastes too weak and sometimes too bitter. With a scale, you make the same good cup every day.

> **Short answer:** For pour-over, French press and drip machines, an **inexpensive scale with 0.1 g resolution, a timer and tare** is enough. For an **espresso machine**, you also need a **slim, compact and fast-responding** scale that fits under the portafilter.

## Why use a coffee scale?

1. **Consistent taste:** Always the same ratio of coffee to water.
2. **Targeted improvements:** If the coffee tastes too strong or too weak, you change one number – not "a bit more of a scoop".
3. **Follow recipes:** Almost all recipes use grams, not scoops.
4. **Timer:** Time matters for pour-over and espresso. A scale and timer in one device makes it easy.

## What to look for when buying

1. **0.1 g accuracy:** Important for espresso, nice to have for filter coffee. A kitchen scale with 1 g steps is too coarse for espresso.
2. **Built-in timer:** Ideal when weight and time are visible at the same time.
3. **Tare function:** Resets the display to zero with the vessel on it – standard on almost every scale.
4. **Response time:** Cheap scales show the weight with a delay. You'll notice it while pouring.
5. **Size:** For pour-over the scale can be larger; for espresso it must be slim and small enough for the drip tray. **Measure before you buy!**
6. **Maximum capacity:** At least 2 kg (about 4.4 lb), so the server, dripper and water all fit.
7. **Power:** A USB-rechargeable battery is more convenient than disposable batteries.
8. **Water protection:** Spills happen. A silicone mat protects the scale.

## How to use the scale

### Pour-over (V60)

1. Put the dripper and server on the scale and press tare.
2. Weigh in **15 g** of coffee, then tare again.
3. Start the timer and pour **250 g** of water in stages.
4. Goal: finished in about **2:30–3:30 minutes**. The full recipe is in the [V60 pour-over guide](/en/v60-pour-over-guide/). For a calm, steady pour, a [gooseneck kettle](/en/gooseneck-kettle-guide/) helps.

### Espresso

1. Put the portafilter on the scale, tare, and grind in **18 g** of coffee.
2. Put the cup on the scale under the portafilter, tare, and start the timer.
3. Goal: about **36 g** of espresso in **25–30 seconds**. Too fast → grind finer; too slow → grind coarser. More in the [home espresso machine guide](/en/home-espresso-machine-guide/).

### French press and drip coffee maker

- French press: **60–65 g of coffee per liter**, see [How to use a French press](/en/how-to-use-a-french-press/).
- Drip coffee maker: **55–65 g per liter**, see the [drip coffee maker guide](/en/drip-coffee-maker-guide/).
- For any number of cups, the [coffee ratio calculator](/en/coffee-ratio-calculator/) works out the amounts.

## Which scale for whom?

| You use … | Recommendation |
| --- | --- |
| French press, drip machine | Budget scale, 0.1 g is plenty, timer optional |
| Pour-over (V60, Chemex) | Scale with timer, can be larger |
| Espresso machine | Slim, compact, fast-responding scale with timer |
| All of the above | One slim scale with timer that fits under the portafilter |

## FAQ

**Is a regular kitchen scale enough?** For French press and drip machines, yes, as long as it reads in single grams. Not for espresso – there, tenths of a gram matter.

**How many grams of coffee per cup?** For filter coffee about 15 g per 250 ml (about 8 oz) of water; for a double espresso 16–18 g.

**Do I need a grinder too?** A scale pays off most with freshly ground beans. For the right grinder, see [Best coffee grinder](/en/best-coffee-grinder/); for the right beans, see [How to buy coffee beans](/en/how-to-buy-coffee-beans/).
