"Does a cup of Turkish coffee have more caffeine than a mug of drip coffee?" The answer surprises a lot of people. This article covers **approximate caffeine amounts by drink**, **what determines caffeine** and the **generally recommended daily limits**.

> **Short answer:** A single espresso has about **60–80 mg**, an 8 oz (250 ml) mug of drip coffee **100–150 mg**, and a cup of Turkish coffee **50–80 mg** of caffeine. A small cup may seem "stronger", but the **big mug** usually has more caffeine in total. For healthy adults, up to **400 mg a day** is generally considered safe.

> **Note:** This article is general information, not medical advice. If you're pregnant, breastfeeding, have a health condition or take medication, **talk to your doctor** about caffeine.

## What determines caffeine?

1. **How much coffee you use:** The biggest factor. A drink made with 18 g of coffee has roughly twice the caffeine of one made with 9 g. Work out your own recipe with the [coffee ratio calculator](/en/coffee-ratio-calculator/).
2. **The type of coffee:** **Robusta** has about **twice** the caffeine of arabica. Cheap blends and some Italian espresso blends contain a lot of robusta. See [arabica vs robusta](/en/arabica-vs-robusta/).
3. **Brew time and grind:** The longer water and coffee are in contact, the more caffeine is extracted. That's why French press and cold brew can be strong.
4. **Serving size:** Espresso is **concentrated** but small; a big mug of drip coffee delivers more caffeine **in total**.
5. **Roast level:** Contrary to popular belief, dark roast does **not** have more caffeine; the difference is tiny. See [coffee roast levels](/en/coffee-roast-levels/).

## Do milk or water reduce caffeine?

No. Milk or water make a drink **milder**, but they don't change the **total caffeine**. A single-shot latte has about as much caffeine as a single espresso; it's just bigger.

## How much caffeine per day?

Based on the European Food Safety Authority (**EFSA**), general guidelines are:

| Who? | Per day | At once |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Healthy adults** | up to 400 mg | up to 200 mg |
| **Pregnant and breastfeeding women** | up to 200 mg | – |
| **Children and teens** | much less, by body weight (about 3 mg per kg) | – |

The US FDA also cites about **400 mg a day** for healthy adults.

**What does 400 mg look like?** Roughly **3 mugs of drip coffee**, **5 single espressos** or **6–7 cups of Turkish coffee**. Cola, tea and chocolate through the day count too.

Sensitivity to caffeine varies a lot from person to person. If you notice a racing heart, restlessness or trouble sleeping, cut back and talk to a doctor if needed.

## Caffeine and sleep

Caffeine's **half-life** in adults is on average **3–6 hours**. So half the caffeine from a 4 p.m. coffee can still be in your body at 9 or 10 p.m.

- If you're a light sleeper, try to have your last coffee **at least 6 hours before bed**.
- For an evening coffee, **decaf** is a good choice. See our [decaf coffee guide](/en/decaf-coffee-guide/).
- Completely caffeine-free: [menengiç coffee](/en/menengic-coffee/), made from terebinth berries.

## FAQ

**Does espresso or drip coffee have more caffeine?** **Per ounce**, espresso is far more concentrated. **Per cup**, though, a mug of drip coffee usually has more caffeine than a single espresso.

**Is Turkish coffee strong?** It tastes intense and full-bodied, but because the serving is small, the caffeine per cup is **moderate** (50–80 mg).

**Does dark roast have more caffeine?** It tastes more bitter and bold, but the caffeine is **almost the same**.

**Is decaf caffeine-free?** Not quite: usually **2–15 mg** per cup.

**Does tea or coffee have more caffeine?** Dry tea leaves contain more caffeine than coffee beans, but a cup of tea uses far fewer leaves, so **a cup of tea** usually has **less** caffeine than a cup of coffee.
