You don't need an expensive machine to make great coffee at home. The **moka pot**, the **French press** and the **V60 pour over** are cheap, durable and much loved, but they taste completely different. This guide compares all three on **taste, effort, time and price**, and ends with a quick quiz to help you **pick the right one**.

> **Short answer:** For **strong, intense** coffee or milk drinks without a machine, get a **moka pot**. For the **easiest** method and brewing for several people, a **French press**. For **clean, aromatic** filter coffee and specialty beans, a **V60**. Can't decide? Start with a French press: it's the most forgiving.

## All three at a glance

| | Moka pot | French press | V60 pour over |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Taste** | Strong, intense, espresso-like | Full-bodied, oily, smooth | Clean, light, aromatic |
| **How it works** | Steam pressure pushes water through the coffee | Coffee steeps in water, then is filtered | Water flows through coffee in a paper filter |
| **Time** | 5 minutes | 4–5 minutes | 3–4 minutes |
| **Difficulty** | Medium | Very easy | Medium to hard |
| **Grind** | Fine (a bit coarser than espresso) | Coarse | Medium |
| **Cups** | 1–6 small cups, by size | 1–8 cups | 1–2 cups |
| **With milk** | Very good (latte, cappuccino stand-in) | OK | Best black |
| **Extra gear** | None | None | Gooseneck kettle and scale recommended |
| **Cleanup** | Easy (water only) | Dumping grounds is a bit messy | Very easy; toss the filter |
| **Price (approx.)** | $20–50 | $15–45 | $15–30 (+ kettle) |

For amounts and ratios, try the [coffee ratio calculator](/en/coffee-ratio-calculator/).

## Moka pot: for strong-coffee lovers

A moka pot heats water in the bottom chamber, and the steam pressure **pushes it up** through the grounds. The result is **strong and intense**, but it isn't true espresso: the pressure is only about 1–2 bar versus 9 bar.

- **Pros:** Cheap, durable, milk drinks without a machine.
- **Cons:** Too much heat makes it bitter. Aluminum pots don't work on induction.
- **Best for:** People who like strong coffee, lattes or cappuccinos.

How to: [how to use a moka pot](/en/how-to-use-a-moka-pot/). Which one: [moka pot buying guide](/en/moka-pot-buying-guide/).

## French press: the easiest method

Coffee and hot water steep for **4 minutes** in a glass or steel carafe, then the metal mesh is pressed down. Because the mesh lets coffee oils through, the cup is **full-bodied and smooth**.

- **Pros:** Hard to mess up, no extra gear, several cups at once.
- **Cons:** Some sediment in the cup. Left in the press it turns bitter, so pour it all out.
- **Best for:** Anyone who wants it easy, families and guests.

How to: [how to use a French press](/en/how-to-use-a-french-press/).

## V60 pour over: shows the flavor most clearly

The coffee sits in a paper filter and hot water is poured **slowly in circles**. The paper holds back oils and fines, so the coffee is **clean and clear**. Fruity or floral notes come through best this way.

- **Pros:** Cheap, light, brings out what makes specialty coffee special.
- **Cons:** Takes technique. Without a gooseneck kettle and a scale, results are hard to repeat.
- **Best for:** Coffee hobbyists and anyone who wants to try different beans.

How to: [V60 pour over guide](/en/v60-pour-over-guide/). Gear: [gooseneck kettle guide](/en/gooseneck-kettle-guide/) and [coffee scale guide](/en/coffee-scale-guide/).

## Quick quiz: which one suits you?

1. **Do you take your coffee with milk?** Yes → **moka pot**.
2. **Want as little effort as possible in the morning?** Yes → **French press**.
3. **Often making coffee for more than 3 people?** Yes → **French press** (or a big moka pot).
4. **Want to taste coffees from different countries?** Yes → **V60**.
5. **Strong or light?** Strong → **moka pot**; light → **V60**; in between → **French press**.

## What about the AeroPress?

The **AeroPress** sits somewhere in between: fast (2 minutes), can go strong or light, is unbreakable and great for travel. Ideal for one person, but it makes one cup at a time. See [how to use an AeroPress](/en/how-to-use-an-aeropress/).

## Tips for all three

- Use **fresh beans** and grind **right before brewing** if you can. See [best coffee grinder](/en/best-coffee-grinder/).
- **Water temperature:** 195–205 °F (92–96 °C). Let boiling water sit for 30 seconds.
- **Water quality** changes the taste a lot. See [best water for coffee](/en/best-water-for-coffee/).
- If your coffee is **bitter** or **sour**, adjust the grind and time. See [why is my coffee bitter](/en/why-is-my-coffee-bitter/).

## FAQ

**Which makes the strongest coffee?** The **moka pot**. It gives the most concentrated coffee per cup.

**Which has the most caffeine?** Per cup the difference is small; how much coffee you use matters more. See [how much caffeine is in coffee](/en/how-much-caffeine-in-coffee/).

**Can I use the same ground coffee for all three?** You can, but it's not ideal: moka pot fine, V60 medium, French press coarse. Pre-ground **drip** coffee suits the V60; **moka/espresso** grind suits the moka pot.

**Which is best for beginners?** The **French press**. Move to a V60 later if you get curious. Starter ideas: [coffee starter kit](/en/coffee-starter-kit/).
