The Chemex is an hourglass-shaped glass coffee maker that has been made since 1941. With its wooden collar and leather tie it's a design classic shown in several museums. But its real secret is the **very clean, sweet** cup – thanks to a paper filter that's noticeably **thicker** than other pour-over filters.

> **Short answer:** **30 g** of medium-coarse coffee and **500 g** of water at about **94 °C (200 °F)**. Bloom with **60 g** of water for 45 seconds, then pour the rest slowly in stages. Total time **4–5 minutes**. Put the **triple-folded side** of the filter **against the spout**.

## What makes the Chemex special?

- **Thick filter:** Holds back coffee oils and fines – a clean, clear, light-bodied cup with no sludge.
- **Glass:** Doesn't absorb smells or flavors.
- **All in one:** Brewer and carafe in one – you serve straight from the Chemex.

It's similar to a V60, but with a thicker filter and a slightly coarser grind – see the [V60 guide](/en/v60-pour-over-guide/).

## What you need

- A Chemex and Chemex filters
- 30 g of coffee, medium-coarse (like coarse sea salt)
- 500 g of water at about 94 °C / 200 °F (wait 30 seconds after boiling), plus extra for rinsing
- A [coffee scale](/en/coffee-scale-guide/), and ideally a [gooseneck kettle](/en/gooseneck-kettle-guide/)

## Step by step

1. **Place the filter:** Open the folded filter and set it with the **three-layer side against the spout** – this keeps the spout clear so air can escape.
2. **Rinse:** Wet the whole filter with hot water; the Chemex warms up too. Pour the water out without moving the filter.
3. **Add coffee:** 30 g, shake gently to level. Tare the scale.
4. **Bloom:** Pour 60 g of water until all the grounds are wet. Wait **45 seconds** while the coffee swells and releases gas.
5. **Pour:** Add the rest in 2–3 stages, in slow circles from the center outward. Don't fill to the very top of the filter.
6. **Finish:** Stop at 500 g. Draining fully takes about **4–5 minutes** in total.
7. **Serve:** Remove the filter, give the Chemex a gentle swirl and pour.

## Ratio and size

A ratio of **1:15 to 1:17** is typical. For stronger coffee, go toward 1:15.

| Chemex size | Coffee | Water | About cups |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3-cup | 20 g | 320 g | 1–2 |
| 6-cup | 30–40 g | 500–650 g | 2–4 |
| 8-cup | 45–50 g | 750–800 g | 4–5 |

Note: a Chemex "cup" is small (about 5 oz / 150 ml). For other amounts, use the [coffee ratio calculator](/en/coffee-ratio-calculator/).

## Dialing in the taste

| Coffee tastes… | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Bitter, drains longer than 6 minutes | Grind coarser |
| Sour, drains faster than 3 minutes | Grind finer |
| Watery | Use more coffee |
| Filter clogs | Grind too fine or too many fines |

More in [why is my coffee bitter or sour?](/en/why-is-my-coffee-bitter/). An even grind needs a good grinder – see the [grinder guide](/en/best-coffee-grinder/).

## Cleaning

Wash with warm water and a little dish soap – a long bottle brush reaches the bottom. Remove the wooden collar first and keep it out of the water. For coffee stains, swirl with diluted vinegar now and then and rinse well.

## FAQ

**Can I use regular coffee filters?** Not recommended. They're thinner and don't fit the shape, so coffee runs faster and tastes less clean.

**Chemex or V60?** The Chemex is great for several cups and an especially clean taste. The V60 is handier for one cup and brings out flavors a bit more.

**Which coffee?** Light to medium roasts with fruity or floral notes shine in a Chemex – see [how to buy coffee beans](/en/how-to-buy-coffee-beans/). For a quick single cup, try the [AeroPress](/en/how-to-use-an-aeropress/).
