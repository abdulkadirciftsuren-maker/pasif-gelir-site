The cezve – also called an ibrik – is the most important tool for Turkish coffee. It looks simple, but **material, size and your stove** decide whether your coffee gets a thick foam or boils over.

> **Short answer:** For **gas or electric**, a **tin-lined copper cezve** is the best choice. For **induction**, you need an **induction-ready stainless steel cezve**. Choose the size for the number of cups you usually make – smaller is better than too big.

## First, check your stove: induction or not?

- **Copper and aluminium don't work on induction** – the hob won't detect them.
- For induction you need a cezve with a **magnetic base** (usually stainless steel), labelled "induction-ready" or with a coil symbol.
- Quick test at home: if a fridge magnet sticks to the base, it works on induction.
- Workaround for copper: an **induction adapter plate**. It works, but reacts slowly – it's easier to miss the moment the foam rises.

## Materials compared

| | Copper (tin-lined) | Stainless steel | Enamel / aluminium |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Heat distribution | Very even | Medium | Medium |
| Foam control | Best | Good, with practice | Good |
| Induction | No | Usually yes | Usually no |
| Care | More demanding | Easy | Easy |
| Lifespan | Very long | Long | Medium |
| Price | Medium to high | Low | Low |

**Important with copper:** The inside should be **tin-lined**. Coffee shouldn't come into direct contact with bare copper. If after years the tin wears off and reddish copper shows through, have it re-tinned or replace the cezve.

## The right size

A cezve works best when it is **about half to two-thirds full**. In a cezve that's too big, the foam spreads out and gets thin.

| You usually make … | Size |
| --- | --- |
| 1 cup | approx. 100–150 ml |
| 2 cups | approx. 200–250 ml |
| 3–4 cups | approx. 300–400 ml |

A Turkish coffee cup holds about 60–70 ml. If you often have guests, buy **two sizes**: a small one for every day and a larger one for visitors.

## What else to look for

1. **Narrow neck:** A cezve that is narrower at the top gathers the foam and lets it rise slowly.
2. **Spout:** A small lip helps share the foam neatly between cups.
3. **Handle:** Long enough to keep your hand away from the flame. Wooden handles stay cool but shouldn't go in the dishwasher.
4. **Thick base:** Spreads heat more evenly and prevents scorching.

## Care

- **Rinse with warm water** after each use and dry well.
- Copper: **no dishwasher** and no abrasive cleaners – they damage the tin lining.
- Tarnished copper on the outside can be polished with lemon and salt (outside only).
- With hard water, descale occasionally with a little citric acid and rinse thoroughly.

## FAQ

**Which cezve is best for beginners?** A small one for 1–2 cups: tin-lined copper on gas or electric, stainless steel on induction.

**What coffee do I need?** Turkish coffee ground as fine as powder. How to make it: [How to make Turkish coffee](/en/how-to-make-turkish-coffee/). Prefer a machine? See the [Turkish coffee machine guide](/en/turkish-coffee-machine-guide/).
