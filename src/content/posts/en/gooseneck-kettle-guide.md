Once you start making pour-over, you quickly notice: a regular kettle pours water in a thick, unsteady gush. A **gooseneck kettle**, on the other hand, pours a thin, even stream – exactly where you want it. Is it just a pretty accessory, or does it really make better coffee?

> **Short answer:** For **pour-over (V60, Chemex, Kalita)**, a gooseneck makes a noticeable difference because you control the amount, speed and placement of the pour. Ideally, choose a model with **temperature control (about 92–96 °C / 198–205 °F)**. For **French press, drip machines or super-automatics**, you don't need one.

## Why a gooseneck?

1. **Controlled stream:** You pour slowly and evenly, so the coffee bed gets wet evenly.
2. **Targeted pouring:** Pour in circles and avoid the edges – less water bypasses the coffee along the filter wall.
3. **Repeatable:** Together with a [coffee scale](/en/coffee-scale-guide/) you can repeat any recipe exactly.
4. **Less channeling:** A calm stream doesn't churn up the coffee bed. Result: more even extraction, less bitter or sour.

## What's the right temperature?

Boiling water (100 °C / 212 °F) is usually a little too hot for coffee. Good guidelines:

| Roast | Temperature |
| --- | --- |
| Light (fruity) | 94–96 °C (201–205 °F) |
| Medium | 92–94 °C (198–201 °F) |
| Dark | 88–92 °C (190–198 °F) |

No temperature control? Bring the water to a boil and wait about **30–60 seconds**.

## What to look for when buying

1. **Spout:** The stream should be thin, calm and easy to steer – this matters most.
2. **Temperature control:** Electric models you can set to the exact degree are the most convenient.
3. **Keep-warm:** Holds the temperature while you grind and prepare.
4. **Capacity:** 0.6–1 liter is enough for 1–3 cups. For more cups, go for 1 liter or more.
5. **Handle and weight:** The kettle should sit comfortably in your hand when full and not feel top-heavy.
6. **Material:** Stainless steel inside, ideally without plastic in contact with the water.
7. **Electric or stovetop:** Stovetop kettles are cheaper and long-lasting; electric models are more convenient and precise.
8. **Descaling:** Kettles build up scale too. Tips in [How to descale a coffee maker](/en/how-to-descale-a-coffee-maker/).

## How to pour properly (V60)

1. Rinse the filter, add **15 g** of medium-fine ground coffee, tare the scale.
2. **Bloom:** pour 30–40 g of water, wait 30–45 seconds.
3. Pour in **slow circles** from the center outward and back, without hitting the filter wall directly.
4. **250 g** of water in total, done after about **2:30–3:30 minutes**.

The full recipe with troubleshooting is in the [V60 pour-over guide](/en/v60-pour-over-guide/).

| --- | --- |

| Drip machine, super-automatic, espresso machine | No |

## FAQ

**Can I make pour-over with a regular kettle?** Yes, but the stream is harder to control. A cheap trick: pour the water into a milk pitcher with a pointed spout first.

**Electric or stovetop?** Electric with temperature control is the most convenient. A stovetop kettle is cheaper and works fine if you let the water cool briefly.

**Which beans for pour-over?** Freshly roasted coffee, light to medium roast. More in [How to buy coffee beans](/en/how-to-buy-coffee-beans/); the right grinder is in [Best coffee grinder](/en/best-coffee-grinder/).
