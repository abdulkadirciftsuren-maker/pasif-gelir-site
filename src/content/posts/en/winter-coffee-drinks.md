When it gets cold, café menus change: pumpkin spice lattes, cinnamon coffees, gingerbread drinks. The good news: you can make all of them **at home, for a fraction of the price**. Here are **5 winter coffee drinks** plus the **homemade syrups** cafés use.

> **Short answer:** A winter latte = **espresso (or strong coffee) + hot foamed milk + spiced syrup**. Make the syrup once, keep it in the fridge, and your café-style latte is ready in 3 minutes every morning.

## The basics: 3 building blocks

1. **Coffee:** one shot of espresso. No machine? Strong coffee from a [moka pot](/en/how-to-use-a-moka-pot/) or an [AeroPress](/en/how-to-use-an-aeropress/) (about 50–60 ml) works great.
2. **Milk:** 200–250 ml hot milk. Foam it with a [milk frother](/en/milk-frother-guide/) or shake it in a jar: [how to froth milk without a frother](/en/how-to-froth-milk-without-a-frother/). Plant-based? Barista oat milk foams best: [oat milk for coffee](/en/oat-milk-for-coffee/).
3. **Syrup:** one of the syrups below. Store-bought works too: [coffee syrup guide](/en/coffee-syrup-guide/).

**Order:** syrup in the mug, espresso on top, stir. Then add the hot milk and spoon the foam on top, so every sip tastes the same.

## 1. Pumpkin spice latte

The café star of autumn. With real pumpkin it tastes more natural and less sugary.

**Pumpkin spice blend (one jar):**

- 3 tbsp ground cinnamon
- 2 tsp ground ginger
- 2 tsp grated nutmeg
- 1 tsp ground cloves or allspice

Mix and keep in a lidded jar. It is great in cookies and cakes too.

**Pumpkin syrup (6–8 lattes):**

- 100 g pumpkin purée (canned, or mashed roasted pumpkin)
- 100 g sugar (brown sugar tastes better)
- 150 ml water
- 1½ tsp pumpkin spice blend
- a little vanilla, if you like

**Method:**

1. Put everything in a small pan and bring to a boil, stirring.
2. Simmer gently for **10 minutes** until slightly thick.
3. For a smooth syrup, press it through a fine sieve.
4. Let it cool and pour into a jar. Keeps **5–7 days** in the fridge.

**Latte:** stir **2 tbsp syrup** into **1 shot of espresso**, add **hot foamed milk**. Top with whipped cream and a pinch of spice.

## 2. Cinnamon latte

The easiest winter coffee, made with what is already in your cupboard.

**Cinnamon syrup:**

1. Put **150 ml water**, **100 g sugar** and **2 cinnamon sticks** (or 1 tsp ground cinnamon) in a pan.
2. Bring to a boil and simmer for **8–10 minutes**.
3. Let cool and remove the sticks. Keeps **2 weeks** in the fridge.

**Latte:** 1–2 tbsp syrup + espresso + hot milk, dusted with cinnamon.

**Sugar-free:** sprinkle **a pinch of cinnamon** on the ground coffee before brewing. The aroma goes into the coffee, no syrup needed.

## 3. Gingerbread latte

Christmas market in a mug: ginger, cinnamon and a touch of molasses.

**Gingerbread syrup:**

- 150 ml water
- 100 g brown sugar
- 1 tbsp **molasses** (or grape molasses)
- 1 tsp ground ginger (or 3–4 slices of fresh ginger)
- ½ tsp cinnamon, a pinch of cloves

Simmer for **10 minutes**, strain if you used fresh ginger. Keeps **1 week** in the fridge.

**Latte:** 2 tbsp syrup + espresso + hot milk. Lovely with whipped cream and crushed gingerbread cookies.

## 4. Hazelnut mocha

Chocolate meets coffee, perfect for grey afternoons.

1. Stir **1 tbsp cocoa**, **1 tbsp sugar** and **1 tbsp hot water** into a smooth paste in the mug.
2. Add **1 tsp hazelnut butter** (or hazelnut syrup) if you like.
3. Pour in the **espresso** and stir.
4. Top up with **hot foamed milk** and sprinkle chopped roasted hazelnuts.

## 5. Turkish coffee with cardamom and cinnamon

A spiced Turkish coffee for winter. The base is our guide on [how to make Turkish coffee](/en/how-to-make-turkish-coffee/).

1. For each cup, put **1 demitasse cup of cold water** and **1 heaped teaspoon of Turkish coffee** into the cezve.
2. Add **1 lightly crushed cardamom pod** per cup (or a pinch of ground cardamom), plus a little cinnamon if you like.
3. Add sugar to taste, stir and heat slowly until it foams.
4. Share the foam between the cups, warm the rest briefly and pour.

Cardamom gives a fresh, slightly lemony aroma. Another special one: [menengiç coffee](/en/menengic-coffee/).

## Tips

- **How much syrup:** 1–2 tbsp is enough for a 250 ml latte. Start small and add more.
- **Milk temperature:** milk tastes sweetest at about 60–65 °C (140–150 °F). If it boils, the foam collapses.
- **Which coffee:** medium to dark roasts with chocolate and nutty notes go best with spices. See [coffee roast levels](/en/coffee-roast-levels/).
- **Less sugar:** you can cut the sugar by up to 30%, but the syrup will keep for less time.
- **Gift idea:** a small bottle of homemade pumpkin syrup and a bag of coffee make a lovely winter gift. More ideas: [coffee gift ideas](/en/coffee-gift-ideas/).

## FAQ

**Is there real pumpkin in a pumpkin spice latte?** In many cafés, only the spice flavor. Our homemade version uses real pumpkin purée, so it tastes more natural.

**I don't have an espresso machine. What now?** Use a moka pot, AeroPress or strong drip coffee. A **capsule machine** works too: [capsule coffee machine guide](/en/capsule-coffee-machine-guide/).

**Can I have winter lattes iced?** Yes. Mix the syrup with cold milk and ice, then pour espresso on top. See our [iced latte recipe](/en/iced-latte-recipe/).

**Can I make them decaf?** Of course, same recipes with decaf beans: [decaf coffee guide](/en/decaf-coffee-guide/). Perfect for the evening.
