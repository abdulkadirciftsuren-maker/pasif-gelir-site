Mırra is the bitter, deep-dark coffee of southeastern Anatolia, especially **Mardin and Şanlıurfa**. It's boiled for hours, unsweetened and drunk in tiny sips only. Mırra is more than a drink: it stands for hospitality, mourning, reconciliation and a promise kept.

> **In short:** To make Mırra, dark-roasted coffee is **boiled with water for hours, strained and reduced again**. It's **unsweetened and very bitter**. It's served in small handleless cups – just **one or two sips**. The cup isn't set down but handed back to the host.

## Where is Mırra drunk?

- **Mardin:** The best-known Mırra city. Served at weddings, condolence visits and to guests.
- **Şanlıurfa:** Mırra is everywhere, especially at the traditional sıra nights with music. Ready-made Mırra is sold in many places here.
- **Surrounding areas:** Also in Gaziantep, Diyarbakır, Batman, Siirt, Adıyaman and parts of Hatay. Taste and preparation differ from region to region.

Many families from these regions now live abroad – and the Mırra tradition has travelled with them.

## Mırra and Arabic coffee

Mırra is the Anatolian form of the Arabic coffee tradition (*qahwa*). The Arabic coffee drunk in the Gulf region is on the **UNESCO list of Intangible Cultural Heritage**.

| | Mırra | Gulf Arabic coffee (qahwa) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Roast | Dark | Usually light |
| Taste | Very bitter, intense | Lighter, spiced |
| Spices | Usually none, sometimes cardamom | Usually cardamom, sometimes saffron |
| Preparation | Hours long, boiled several times | Shorter |
| Served with | Water | Often dates |

## How is Mırra made?

Every family has its own method. Roughly, it goes like this:

1. **Roasting:** The beans are roasted dark.
2. **Pounding or grinding:** Coarser than Turkish coffee.
3. **First boil:** The coffee is boiled with plenty of water in a large pot for hours.
4. **Resting and straining:** The grounds settle and the clear part is poured off.
5. **Second boil:** The strained coffee is reduced again with fresh coffee and concentrated – often several times. This is what gives Mırra its strength.
6. **Storing and serving:** It's kept sealed and warmed before serving.

**To try it:** The easiest way is **ready-made bottled Mırra**. Just warm it and serve in small cups.

## How do you drink Mırra?

- **Served standing:** Usually a younger family member pours with the right hand.
- **Pour only a little:** Just one or two sips. Filling the cup is considered impolite.
- **Don't set the cup down:** You hand the cup back to the host. According to tradition, otherwise you take on an obligation towards the host.
- **Water alongside:** Before or after the Mırra.

## For which occasions?

- **Condolence visits:** Mırra in a house of mourning is an old tradition.
- **Weddings and engagements.**
- **Reconciliation:** A Mırra drunk together seals the word given.
- **Guests of honour:** A sign of respect.

## FAQ

**Is Mırra the same as Turkish coffee?** No. Turkish coffee is ground as fine as powder, brewed briefly and drunk with its grounds. Mırra is boiled for hours, strained and has no grounds – it's much darker and more bitter.

**Is Mırra drunk with sugar?** Traditionally not. The bitterness is part of it.

**Where can I buy Mırra?** Some Turkish and Middle Eastern grocery stores and online shops sell ready-made Mırra, Mırra coffee or matching cups.
