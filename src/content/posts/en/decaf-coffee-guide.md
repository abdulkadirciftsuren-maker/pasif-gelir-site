You love coffee, but it keeps you up at night – or you've been told to cut back on caffeine? That's what **decaf** is for. But how is it made, is it really caffeine-free and does it taste like regular coffee? Here are the answers.

> **Short answer:** Decaf is made by removing most of the caffeine from **green, unroasted coffee**. It's not quite zero: a cup usually has **a few milligrams** of caffeine, while regular coffee has dozens. The methods are **water (Swiss Water)**, **CO₂** and **solvent** processes. Freshly roasted good decaf tastes very close to regular coffee.

## How is caffeine removed?

Caffeine is removed while the coffee is **still green**, before roasting. The beans are swelled with water or steam, then the caffeine is extracted in different ways:

| Method | How it works | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Water process (e.g. Swiss Water)** | Only water and filters separate out the caffeine | No chemical solvents; often highlighted on the bag |
| **CO₂ process** | Pressurized carbon dioxide selectively pulls out caffeine | Preserves flavor compounds well |
| **Ethyl acetate ("natural")** | Uses a compound also found in fruit and sugarcane | Often called the "sugarcane process" |
| **Other solvents** | A solvent binds the caffeine and is then removed | Residues are limited by regulation |

The beans are then dried and roasted like any other coffee.

## Is decaf really caffeine-free?

**Not completely.** In the EU, decaf roasted coffee may contain no more than 0.1% caffeine, and in the US decaf generally has at least 97% of its caffeine removed. In practice a cup of decaf usually has **just a few milligrams** of caffeine – a regular cup of drip coffee has **dozens**. So the difference is big.

## Does it taste different?

Decaf used to have a reputation for tasting flat. Today, well-processed, **freshly roasted** decaf tastes very close to regular coffee. What matters:

- **Freshness:** Decaf sells more slowly and can sit on shelves for a long time. Check the **roast date** – see [how to store coffee beans](/en/how-to-store-coffee-beans/).
- **Method:** Water and CO₂ methods generally keep flavors well.
- **Roast:** Decaf beans are more delicate to roast. Medium roast is a safe start – see [coffee roast levels](/en/coffee-roast-levels/).

## Who is it for?

- Anyone who wants an evening coffee without **sleep problems**
- People **sensitive** to caffeine (racing heart, jitters)
- Anyone trying to **cut down** on caffeine
- Anyone with a caffeine limit from their doctor (e.g. during pregnancy) – always **ask your doctor**

## How to brew decaf

Just like regular coffee:

- [Turkish coffee](/en/how-to-make-turkish-coffee/): decaf Turkish coffee foams too.
- [V60](/en/v60-pour-over-guide/), [French press](/en/how-to-use-a-french-press/), [AeroPress](/en/how-to-use-an-aeropress/)
- [Espresso](/en/home-espresso-machine-guide/) and [capsules](/en/capsule-coffee-machine-guide/): decaf capsules exist for many systems.
- **Super-automatic:** With a bypass doser you can make a decaf without emptying the bean hopper – see the [super-automatic guide](/en/super-automatic-espresso-machine-guide/).

## Naturally caffeine-free alternatives

- **[Menengiç coffee](/en/menengic-coffee/):** Made from wild pistachios, contains no coffee and is caffeine-free.
- **Kenger coffee:** Made from the seeds of a thistle-like plant, caffeine-free – see [types of coffee](/en/types-of-coffee/).
- **Half-caf:** Mixing regular and decaf half and half is a nice middle ground.

**How much caffeine is in each drink?** From espresso and Turkish coffee to tea and cola: [how much caffeine is in coffee](/en/how-much-caffeine-in-coffee/).

## FAQ

**Is decaf unhealthy?** For approved methods, residues are limited by regulation. If you'd rather avoid solvents, choose "water process" or "CO₂".

**Why does decaf cost more?** Decaffeination is an extra production step.

**Why does Robusta have more caffeine?** Robusta contains roughly twice as much caffeine as Arabica. Even switching to 100% Arabica lowers your caffeine – see [Arabica vs Robusta](/en/arabica-vs-robusta/).
