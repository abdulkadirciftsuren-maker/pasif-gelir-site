Café-style espresso made at home: that's the promise of a manual (semi-automatic) espresso machine. Unlike a [super-automatic espresso machine](/en/super-automatic-espresso-machine-guide/), you do everything yourself here – grinding, tamping, brewing, steaming milk. It takes a few weeks of practice, but it rewards you with full control over the taste. This guide shows what matters when you buy and where the marketing exaggerates.

> **Short answer:** Plan your budget for the **machine and grinder together** – the grinder is at least as important. Look for **stable temperature (PID)**, a **usable steam wand** and, if possible, a **58 mm portafilter**. You can ignore the bar number on the box.

## Espresso machine, super-automatic or capsule?

| | Capsule | Super-automatic | Manual espresso machine |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Operation | Insert capsule, press button | One button | You grind, tamp and brew |
| Learning curve | None | Very little | A few weeks |
| Control over taste | Very little | Medium | Full |
| Cost per cup | High | Low | Low |
| Milk drinks | Separate frother | Usually at the push of a button | With a steam wand, takes practice |
| Who is it for? | Quick and easy | Convenience and milk drinks | Coffee as a hobby |

## The bar myth

Many boxes proudly say **"15 bar"** or **"20 bar"**. But more isn't better: around **9 bar** at the coffee puck is considered ideal for good espresso. The number on the box is just the pump's maximum pressure and says very little about taste. Look at temperature stability, baskets and the grinder instead.

## The most important part: the grinder

With an espresso machine, **the grinder decides the taste more than the machine does**. Espresso needs very fine, even grounds – even small changes in grind size change the shot time and the flavor.

- **Pre-ground coffee** only works to a limited extent: it goes stale quickly and isn't dialed in for your machine.
- **Split your budget:** a mid-range machine with a good grinder makes better espresso than an expensive machine with a cheap grinder.
- We explain the different grinder types in [Best coffee grinder](/en/best-coffee-grinder/).

## Pressurized or standard basket?

Many entry-level machines come with **pressurized (double-wall) baskets**. They produce a "crema" even with pre-ground coffee – forgiving, but limited in taste. Once you have fresh beans and a good grinder, a **standard (single-wall) basket** makes the real difference. Often both are included, or they're inexpensive to buy separately.

## 6 things to check before you buy

1. **Heating system and temperature:**
   - **Thermoblock / thermojet:** heats up in seconds, ideal for beginners.
   - **Single boiler:** stable temperature, but you wait between espresso and steam.
   - **Heat exchanger / dual boiler:** espresso and milk at the same time – for advanced users, and much more expensive.
   - **PID control** keeps the temperature constant and gives more consistent results.
2. **Steam wand:** Simple "panarello" wands make foam easily; a real steam wand produces fine microfoam for latte art. More in [Latte vs cappuccino vs flat white](/en/latte-vs-cappuccino-vs-flat-white/).
3. **Portafilter size:** 58 mm is the café standard – tampers, baskets and accessories are available everywhere. Smaller sizes have less choice.
4. **Water tank and scale:** The tank should be easy to remove. In areas with hard water, regular descaling is a must – see [How to descale a coffee maker](/en/how-to-descale-a-coffee-maker/).
5. **Size:** The machine lives on your countertop permanently. Measure the height under your wall cabinets too.
6. **Spare parts and service:** Gaskets, baskets and the shower screen should be easy to get.

## First steps after buying

1. **Get fresh beans** with a roast date – medium to dark roast. Tips in [How to buy coffee beans](/en/how-to-buy-coffee-beans/).
2. **Starting recipe:** 18 g of coffee in a double basket, about 36 g of espresso in the cup, in 25–30 seconds.
3. **Change only the grind size:** Runs too fast and tastes sour → grind finer. Runs too slow and tastes bitter → grind coarser.
4. **Use a scale:** A simple coffee scale with a timer makes dialing in much easier – see the [coffee scale guide](/en/coffee-scale-guide/).

**Accessories:** For a scale, tamper, milk pitcher and more, see our [espresso accessories](/en/espresso-accessories/) guide.

## FAQ

**Can I make Turkish coffee with an espresso machine?** No. Turkish coffee is ground as fine as powder and brewed in a cezve – in an espresso machine it clogs the basket. More in [How to make Turkish coffee](/en/how-to-make-turkish-coffee/).

**Which beans for espresso?** Freshly roasted beans with a roast date, usually medium to dark roast or an arabica-robusta blend. See [Arabica vs robusta](/en/arabica-vs-robusta/).

**Is capsule coffee "real" espresso?** It's brewed under pressure and comes close to espresso, but it can hardly be adjusted and costs much more per cup. A comparison of the systems is in the [capsule coffee machine guide](/en/capsule-coffee-machine-guide/).
