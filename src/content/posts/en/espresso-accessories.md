You've got your espresso machine; now it's time for **accessories**. There are hundreds of gadgets out there, but not all of them are worth it. This guide shows **what to buy first**, **how to pick the right tamper size** and which tools **actually make a difference**.

> **Short answer:** Four things are enough to start: a **scale**, a **tamper that fits your basket**, a **milk pitcher** and a **knock box**, about **$60–120** in total. If your espresso is still inconsistent, add a **WDT tool** and a **dosing funnel**. A **bottomless portafilter** and a **precision basket** are for later.

For choosing a machine, see our [home espresso machine guide](/en/home-espresso-machine-guide/). And remember: before any accessory, the most important upgrade is a **good grinder**: [best coffee grinder](/en/best-coffee-grinder/).

## 1. The scale: buy this first

Two numbers matter in espresso: **how many grams of coffee** go in and **how many grams of espresso** come out. A typical start: **18 g coffee → 36 g espresso** in about **25–30 seconds**. Without a scale you can't dial in your grinder properly.

- **0.1 g precision** and a **timer**.
- **Slim and small** enough to fit on the drip tray.
- Water resistance is a big plus.

More: [coffee scale guide](/en/coffee-scale-guide/).

## 2. The tamper: get the size right first

The tamper has to **fit the basket exactly**. A gap at the edge means the coffee isn't compressed evenly. Find the size in your manual or measure the inside diameter of the basket.

| Machine (examples) | Typical size |
| --- | --- |
| De'Longhi Dedica, La Specialista | 51 mm |
| Breville Bambino, Barista Express | 54 mm |
| Gaggia Classic, Rancilio Silvia, most E61 and commercial machines | 58 mm |

- **Calibrated tamper:** It "clicks" at a set pressure, so you press the same every time. Great for beginners.
- **Weight and grip:** A heavy stainless base and a comfortable handle.
- **How hard to press?** Firm and even is enough; pressing harder doesn't improve the taste. Tamping **level** matters more than force.

## 3. Milk pitcher

- **12 oz (350 ml):** One cappuccino or latte.
- **20 oz (600 ml):** Two drinks or a large latte.
- A **sharp spout** helps with latte art; **markings inside** show how much milk you've poured.
- Fill to about **one third**; the milk grows as you steam it.

More on milk and drinks: [milk frother guide](/en/milk-frother-guide/) and [latte vs cappuccino vs flat white](/en/latte-vs-cappuccino-vs-flat-white/).

## 4. Knock box

Knock the used puck out of the portafilter in **one tap**. The **rubber-covered bar** protects your portafilter. Drawer-style models save counter space. Empty it daily; the grounds make good plant fertilizer.

## 5. WDT tool and dosing funnel

Freshly ground coffee has **small clumps**. They make water flow unevenly and cause **channeling**: espresso that tastes sour and bitter at the same time.

- **WDT tool:** Stir the grounds in **small circles** with fine needles to break up the clumps. It takes 10–20 seconds and you'll taste the difference right away.
- **Dosing funnel:** Sits on the portafilter so nothing spills while you grind and stir.

## 6. Advanced: bottomless portafilter and precision basket

- **Bottomless portafilter:** Open at the bottom, so you can see **how the espresso flows**. Spurts from one spot mean channeling. Great for learning, though a bit messy at first.
- **Precision basket:** More even holes, cleaner flow and clearer taste. Pick the right size for your machine.
- **Distributor (leveler):** Levels the surface. Usually unnecessary if you use WDT.

## 7. For cleaning

- **Group head brush:** Brush the gasket and shower screen daily.
- **Microfiber cloth:** Keep a separate cloth for the steam wand and wipe it after every use.
- **Blind basket and cleaner:** Backflush weekly only if your machine has a **3-way valve**. If not, don't; check your manual.
- **Scale:** Regular descaling is the most important care. See [how to descale a coffee maker](/en/how-to-descale-a-coffee-maker/).

## Common mistakes

- **Wrong tamper size:** Measure the basket first.
- Trying to dial in **without a scale**.
- **Fancy accessories instead of a better grinder:** Grinder first.
- Changing everything at once: Change one thing, then taste.

## FAQ

**Is the plastic tamper that came with my machine enough?** To start, yes, but it's often smaller than the basket and too light. A good metal tamper is one of the cheapest, most effective upgrades.

**Does WDT really make a difference?** Yes, especially with grinders that clump. It's cheap, so it's worth a try.

**What first: scale or tamper?** The **scale**. Without measuring, you can't tell what changed.

**How do I find my tamper size?** Check the manual or measure the inside diameter of your basket. Tampers are sold by basket size (e.g. "58 mm") and are a few tenths of a millimeter smaller.
