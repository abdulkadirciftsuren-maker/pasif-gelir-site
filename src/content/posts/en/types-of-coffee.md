Looking at a café menu can be overwhelming: latte, flat white, cortado, macchiato… Yet most of these drinks are made from the same two ingredients – **espresso and milk** – just in different amounts. In this guide we group coffee into four families: espresso drinks, brewed coffee, the regional coffees of Türkiye, and traditional coffees from around the world.

> **In short:** Milky and mild – **latte**. Milky but clearly coffee – **flat white**. Short and intense – **espresso** or **Turkish coffee**. Long and light – **filter coffee** or **Americano**.

## Espresso drinks

Espresso is a concentrated shot pulled from finely ground coffee under high pressure in about 25–30 seconds. It is the base of most café drinks.

| Drink | What's in it | Taste and feel |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Espresso** | Single or double shot | Intense, full-bodied, with crema |
| **Ristretto** | Shorter shot, less water | Sweeter, more concentrated, less bitter |
| **Lungo** | Longer shot, more water | Lighter, can be more bitter |
| **Americano** | Espresso + hot water | Close to filter coffee |
| **Macchiato** | Espresso + a spoon of milk foam | Espresso, slightly softened |
| **Cortado** | Espresso + roughly equal warm milk | Small, balanced, coffee-forward |
| **Cappuccino** | Espresso + milk + thick foam | Light and foamy, a breakfast classic |
| **Flat white** | Double espresso + a little silky milk | Velvety, coffee-forward |
| **Latte** | Espresso + lots of milk + thin foam | The mildest and creamiest |
| **Mocha** | Espresso + chocolate + milk | Sweet, for chocolate lovers |
| **Affogato** | Espresso over a scoop of ice cream | Half coffee, half dessert |

The difference between the three most confused drinks is explained, with a graphic, in [Latte vs Cappuccino vs Flat White](/en/latte-vs-cappuccino-vs-flat-white/).

For ratios, cup sizes and the difference between a macchiato and a latte macchiato, see [espresso drinks explained](/en/espresso-drinks-explained/).

## Brewed coffee

No pressure here: hot water flows through the coffee or steeps with it. The result is lighter than espresso, with clearer flavours.

- **Pour-over (V60, Chemex):** Clean and bright; fruity and floral notes shine. See our [V60 guide](/en/v60-pour-over-guide/).
- **French press:** The coffee steeps in the water – full, rich body. See our [French press guide](/en/how-to-use-a-french-press/).
- **Cold brew:** Coffee steeps in cold water for 12–24 hours. Low acidity, smooth and naturally sweet.
- **Drip machine:** The easiest option. With freshly ground coffee and the right ratio, much better than its reputation.

## Regional coffees of Türkiye

Turkish coffee culture is more than a single cup. Each region has coffees with their own story.

### Turkish coffee

Coffee ground as fine as flour is slowly heated with water (and sugar if you like) in a cezve and served **with its grounds**. Turkish coffee culture and tradition is listed by UNESCO as Intangible Cultural Heritage. A good cup has a thick, lasting foam and is served with a glass of water. Step by step: [How to make Turkish coffee](/en/how-to-make-turkish-coffee/).

### Mırra (Mardin and southeastern Türkiye)

**Mırra**, from Mardin, Şanlıurfa and the surrounding region, is the strongest coffee in Türkiye. Roasted, pounded beans are boiled with water for a long time, strained and boiled down again – a process that can take hours, sometimes days. The result is very dark, unsweetened and distinctly bitter.

- **How it's drunk:** Only a sip or two, from small handleless cups. The cup is not put down but handed straight back to the host.
- **Tradition:** Mırra stands for hospitality, mourning and keeping one's word. It is said that placing the empty cup on the table creates an obligation towards the host – which is why it is always handed back.
- **Taste:** Surprisingly bitter at first, followed by a long-lasting coffee flavour. Some families add cardamom.

### Dibek coffee

Named after the stone or wooden mortar (*dibek*) in which the coffee is pounded. Because it heats up less than in a grinder, it tastes softer. Many packaged dibek coffees are **blends** that may contain cocoa, milk powder or mastic – check the label if you want pure coffee.

### Menengiç coffee (Gaziantep)

Not actually coffee: it's made from the roasted fruit of the terebinth (wild pistachio) tree, is **caffeine-free**, is cooked with milk and tastes nutty and rich – a lovely option if you avoid caffeine.

### Kenger coffee

Made from the seeds of the kenger plant, a thistle from southeastern Türkiye. Also caffeine-free, with an earthy, mild taste.

### Mastic and "Ottoman" coffee

Variations on Turkish coffee. **Mastic coffee** adds the fresh, resinous note of mastic gum. Products sold as **"Ottoman coffee"** are usually blends with spices, mastic or cocoa – recipes vary by brand.

## Traditional coffees from around the world

| Coffee | Country | What makes it special |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Cà phê sữa đá** | Vietnam | Strong drip coffee with sweetened condensed milk and ice |
| **Frappé** | Greece | Instant coffee, water and sugar shaken to a foam, served iced |
| **Café de olla** | Mexico | Brewed in a clay pot with cinnamon and piloncillo |
| **Buna** | Ethiopia | A ceremony where beans are roasted in front of guests |
| **Qahwa** | Arabian Peninsula | Lightly roasted coffee with cardamom, served with dates |
| **Irish coffee** | Ireland | Hot coffee, whiskey, sugar and cream |
| **Café bombón** | Spain | Half espresso, half condensed milk, in layers |

## Which coffee is right for you?

| If you … | try |
| --- | --- |
| are new to coffee and like it milky | Latte |
| like milk but want to taste the coffee | Flat white or cortado |
| want something short and pure | Espresso or Turkish coffee |
| want to sip for a while | Filter coffee or Americano |
| want something cold in summer | Cold brew or iced latte |
| avoid caffeine | Menengiç coffee or decaf |
| want a real experience | A cup of Mırra in Mardin |

## FAQ

**Which coffee is the strongest?** In taste, Mırra. Caffeine per cup depends on the method and serving size: a large mug of filter coffee can contain more caffeine than a small espresso.

**Which has fewer calories, latte or cappuccino?** The calories come almost entirely from the milk. A cappuccino has less milk, so it is usually lighter.
