Opening your own coffee shop is a dream for many coffee lovers. With a solid plan it can become a realistic business; without one, the first year is often tough. This guide goes **from concept to opening day**: the **equipment list**, **rough prices**, and how to work out what a cup of coffee really costs you.

> **Short answer:** First decide your **concept** (kiosk, neighborhood café, specialty coffee bar) and **budget**. The biggest equipment items are the **commercial espresso machine**, **grinder**, **water filter**, **refrigeration** and **dishwasher**. Always add **3–6 months of operating reserve**: most cafés are not profitable in their first months.

*Note: prices are rough 2026 ranges in euros (dollar prices are similar). Always get current quotes.*

## 1. Pick your concept

Everything depends on it: space, equipment, menu and prices.

| Concept | Size | What it sells | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Kiosk / to-go** | 10–25 m² | Espresso drinks, a few snacks | Lowest investment, needs heavy foot traffic |
| **Neighborhood café** | 40–80 m² | Coffee, tea, cakes, breakfast | Regulars, cozy atmosphere |
| **Specialty coffee bar** | 30–70 m² | Filter coffee, single origins, retail beans | Needs quality gear and trained baristas |
| **Turkish coffee café** | 30–60 m² | Turkish coffee, menengiç, dibek, Turkish delight | A distinctive concept outside Turkey |

**Tip:** Visit the cafés nearby before you decide. What is missing? An early-opening café? Good filter coffee? A quiet place to work? Build your concept around that gap.

## 2. Budget items

Split your budget into:

1. **Rent and deposit:** usually 2–3 months' rent as a deposit.
2. **Fit-out and decor:** electrics, plumbing, flooring, counter, lighting, furniture. Often the item that **surprises people most**.
3. **Equipment:** see the list below.
4. **Opening stock:** coffee, milk, syrups, cups, napkins, cleaning supplies.
5. **Permits, accounting, insurance.**
6. **Launch marketing:** sign, menu, social media, opening offer.
7. **Operating reserve:** rent, wages and bills for **3–6 months**.

## 3. Equipment list with rough prices

| Equipment | Why you need it | Rough price (new) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Commercial espresso machine (2 groups)** | The heart of the bar; two baristas can work at once | €6,000–15,000 (used: €3,000–7,000) |
| **Espresso grinder (on-demand)** | Half of the flavor comes from the grinder | €1,000–3,000 |
| **Second grinder** | For decaf or filter coffee | €500–2,000 |
| **Water filter / softener** | Scale is the machine's worst enemy | €300–900 + cartridges |
| **Batch brewer** | Fast, consistent filter coffee | €800–2,500 |
| **Under-counter fridge** | Milk belongs right at the bar | €800–2,000 |
| **Commercial dishwasher** | Fast, hygienic washing | €1,500–4,000 |
| **Ice machine** | For iced coffee (summer sales) | €500–1,500 |
| **Turkish coffee machine** | If Turkish coffee is on the menu | €300–1,500 |
| **Blender** | Frappés, smoothies | €150–600 |
| **Barista tools** | Scale, tamper, pitchers, knock box, cloths | €150–500 |
| **POS system** | Orders, payments, stock | Tablet + monthly subscription |
| **Cups, glasses, to-go cups** | Presentation that reflects your brand | €300–1,500 |

**Where to save, and where not to:**

- **Don't save:** on the grinder and the water filter. An expensive machine with a cheap grinder makes average coffee. Without a filter the machine scales up and repairs cost far more than the filter. See [the best water for coffee](/en/best-water-for-coffee/).
- **You can save:** on a well-maintained **used espresso machine** (check the service history), furniture and decor.
- **Service:** Is there a **technician** and spare parts nearby? A broken machine means a closed café.

## 4. Sourcing coffee

- **Work with a local roaster:** fresh coffee, samples and often barista training. Ask several roasters for **samples** and taste them blind.
- **At least 2 coffees:** an **espresso blend** for milk drinks and a **single origin** for filter, plus **decaf**. See [how to buy coffee beans](/en/how-to-buy-coffee-beans/) and [what is specialty coffee](/en/what-is-specialty-coffee/).
- **Freshness:** use coffee 1–4 weeks after roasting; order small and often. See [how to tell if coffee is fresh](/en/how-to-tell-if-coffee-is-fresh/).
- **Turkish coffee:** a good brand or your own freshly ground blend can really set you apart.

## 5. What does a cup of coffee cost you?

Before setting prices, work out the **cost per cup**. Example (rounded):

| Item | Latte (250 ml) |
| --- | --- |
| Coffee (18 g at €25/kg) | €0.45 |
| Milk (200 ml at €1.20/l) | €0.24 |
| Cup/lid or washing | €0.10–0.25 |
| **Total ingredient cost** | **about €0.80–0.95** |

At a selling price of €3.50–4.50, ingredient cost is about **20–25%**. The rest covers rent, wages, energy, taxes and profit. Rule of thumb: for drinks, aim to keep ingredient cost under **20–30%** of the net selling price.

**Formula:** ingredient cost ÷ net selling price × 100 = cost percentage.

## 6. Modern café design tips

- **Bar flow:** order → pay → prepare → hand over, all in one direction. Guests and baristas shouldn't cross paths.
- **Lighting:** warm white (about 2,700–3,000 K), pendant lights over the bar, as much daylight as possible.
- **Materials:** wood, stone, plants. Few but good. Calm base colors plus 1–2 accent colors.
- **Seating:** a mix: bar stools at the window, tables for two, one long communal table.
- **Outlets and Wi-Fi:** laptop guests stay longer; plan outlets accordingly.
- **Acoustics:** hard surfaces make it loud. Rugs, curtains and acoustic panels help.
- **A photo spot:** a nice wall, your logo or a neon sign. Guests' photos are free advertising.
- **Menu board:** short and readable. 8–12 drinks is enough; long menus slow people down and create waste.

## 7. Permits and paperwork (general info)

Rules differ a lot by country and city. **Check with your local authority and an accountant before you start.** Typically you will need:

- **Business registration** and tax registration
- **Food business registration** or a health permit from the local authority
- **Food hygiene training** for everyone who handles food
- A **change-of-use** or building permit if the premises were used differently before
- A **music license** if you play music (e.g. through your country's collecting society)
- Signage permission, insurance and, if you sell alcohol, a separate license

## 8. Pre-opening checklist

- [ ] Concept and target customers written down
- [ ] Budget + 3–6 months' reserve
- [ ] Lease (is café use allowed?)
- [ ] Permits done
- [ ] Equipment installed, **water filter** fitted, service partner confirmed
- [ ] Roaster chosen, menu and prices calculated
- [ ] Barista training (fixed recipes, working with a scale)
- [ ] POS and card payments
- [ ] Google Business Profile, social media accounts
- [ ] Soft opening: invite friends and neighbors for free coffee and find the bottlenecks

## FAQ

**How much does it cost to open a coffee shop?** It depends heavily on the concept and city. A small kiosk can be done for a few tens of thousands of euros, while a full café with fit-out and furniture costs much more. The biggest items are usually fit-out and equipment. You only get real numbers from quotes for a specific location.

**Is a used espresso machine a good idea?** Often, yes. Check the service history, scale and spare parts availability, ideally with a technician.

**What's the most common mistake?** Too little reserve and the wrong location. Great coffee is hard to sell on a street nobody walks down.

**Can I start with a home machine?** Not for a café: home machines are too slow for constant use and break quickly. For home use, see our [home espresso machine guide](/en/home-espresso-machine-guide/).

**How do I add Turkish coffee to the menu?** A [Turkish coffee machine](/en/turkish-coffee-machine-guide/) makes fast service much easier. Served with Turkish delight and a glass of water, guests love it.
