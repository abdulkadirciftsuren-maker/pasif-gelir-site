The aroma of freshly roasted coffee can disappear within days if it's stored badly. Storing it well is simple and almost free. Here's what harms coffee, the truth about the fridge myth and how to keep your coffee fresh for longest.

> **Short answer:** Keep coffee **airtight and away from light** in a **cool, dry** cupboard. **Not in the fridge.** Buy **whole beans** and grind just before brewing. Only buy what you'll use in **2–4 weeks**. For longer storage, **freeze** it in portions.

## Coffee's 4 enemies

1. **Air (oxygen):** Breaks down aroma compounds and makes coffee stale – the biggest enemy.
2. **Light:** Sunlight in particular damages coffee fast. Clear jars look nice but are bad for coffee.
3. **Heat:** Next to the stove, oven or coffee machine, coffee ages faster.
4. **Moisture:** Harms coffee and makes it absorb odors.

## The fridge myth

Lots of people keep coffee in the fridge. **Not a great idea:**

- Every time you open it, **moisture condenses** on the cold coffee.
- Coffee **absorbs smells** – cheese, onions, leftovers.
- Cold coffee meeting warm room air goes stale faster.

**The freezer is different:** Coffee you want to keep for a long time can be frozen in **weekly portions, sealed airtight**. Take out one portion, let it come to room temperature unopened (so no condensation forms) and don't refreeze it. Don't keep moving your daily coffee in and out of the freezer.

## How long does coffee stay fresh?

| Coffee | Unopened | After opening |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Whole beans** | A few months after roasting (valve bag) | Best within 2–4 weeks |
| **Ground coffee** | A few months (vacuum-packed) | 1–2 weeks |
| **Turkish coffee** | A few months (vacuum-packed) | 2–3 weeks |
| **Frozen beans** | A few months in airtight portions | 1–2 weeks after thawing |

Note: freshly roasted beans release **CO₂** for several days. Most coffees taste best between a few days and a few weeks after roasting – that's why the roast date matters. More in [how to buy coffee beans](/en/how-to-buy-coffee-beans/).

## The best place to store coffee

1. **The original valve bag:** Good coffee comes in bags with a one-way valve – gas out, air not in. Squeeze out the air, roll it tight and clip it.
2. **An airtight, opaque canister:** Stainless steel or ceramic.
3. **A vacuum canister:** Reduces the air inside – useful if a bag lasts you more than a few weeks.
4. **A cool, dark cupboard:** Away from the stove and sunlight.

## Ground or whole beans?

Ground coffee has a much larger surface area, so it starts losing aroma within **minutes to hours**. For the best taste, buy whole beans and grind right before brewing – see the [grinder guide](/en/best-coffee-grinder/). Turkish coffee is best bought in small packs and used quickly – see [how to buy Turkish coffee](/en/how-to-buy-turkish-coffee/).

## Is your coffee stale?

- **Little aroma** when you open the bag.
- **No bloom** when brewing a V60 or Chemex.
- Less **crema** on espresso, less **foam** on Turkish coffee.
- A **flat, cardboard-like** or bitter taste.

If so, check how you store it and buy smaller bags. For taste problems, see [why is my coffee bitter or sour?](/en/why-is-my-coffee-bitter/).

## FAQ

**Can I store coffee in a glass jar?** If it seals tightly, yes – but **in a dark cupboard**. A clear jar on a sunny counter is a bad idea.

**Is it OK to leave beans in my super-automatic's hopper?** Only a few days' worth. Hoppers usually aren't airtight or lightproof – see the [super-automatic guide](/en/super-automatic-espresso-machine-guide/).

**Can you drink expired coffee?** It's usually not a health issue, but it tastes noticeably weaker. If there's mold or a musty smell, throw it out.
