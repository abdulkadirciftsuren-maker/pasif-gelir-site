On a hot day, nothing beats a cold coffee. The good news: café-quality cold coffee is easy at home – even without an espresso machine. Here's the recipe for an **iced latte** plus **5 more cold coffee drinks**.

> **Short answer:** Fill a glass with **ice**, add **cold milk** and pour a **double espresso** (or strong moka pot coffee or AeroPress concentrate) on top. Because ice dilutes, the coffee should be **stronger than usual**. To sweeten, use **syrup** instead of sugar – sugar barely dissolves in cold drinks.

## 4 golden rules for cold coffee

1. **Strong coffee:** Melting ice waters it down. Brew stronger or use more coffee.
2. **Coffee ice cubes:** Freeze leftover coffee in an ice tray – your drink won't get watery.
3. **Syrup, not sugar:** Use [coffee syrup](/en/coffee-syrup-guide/) or homemade simple syrup.
4. **Fresh coffee:** Cold mutes flavor, so quality and freshness matter more. See [how to tell if coffee is fresh](/en/how-to-tell-if-coffee-is-fresh/).

## Iced latte

**Ingredients (1 glass):**

- A double espresso (about 40 ml / 1.4 oz) or 60 ml (2 oz) of strong moka pot coffee / AeroPress concentrate
- 150–200 ml (5–7 oz) cold milk (or barista oat milk)
- Plenty of ice
- 10–15 ml (2–3 tsp) syrup, optional

**Method:**

1. Fill a tall glass with **ice**.
2. Add **syrup** if you like.
3. Pour in the **cold milk**.
4. Slowly pour the coffee over the top – you'll get **layers**. Stir before drinking.

**No espresso machine?** A [moka pot](/en/how-to-use-a-moka-pot/) or [AeroPress](/en/how-to-use-an-aeropress/) (18 g coffee, 90 g water) works great, and so do [capsules](/en/capsule-coffee-machine-guide/). For foam, shake cold milk in a jar: [how to froth milk without a frother](/en/how-to-froth-milk-without-a-frother/).

## 1. Japanese iced coffee

Hot-brewed coffee drips straight onto ice and chills instantly – aromatic, fresh and clear.

1. Put **140 g of ice** in the carafe under your V60.
2. Add **20 g** of medium-fine coffee to the filter.
3. Brew as usual with **160 g** of hot water (about 94 °C / 200 °F).
4. The coffee drips onto the ice. Swirl and pour into a glass with ice.

See the [V60 guide](/en/v60-pour-over-guide/).

## 2. Frappé (Greek style)

The foamy classic made with instant coffee.

1. Put **2 tsp instant coffee**, sugar to taste and **50 ml (1.7 oz) cold water** into a jar or shaker.
2. Shake hard for **30 seconds** – you'll get a thick foam.
3. Pour into a glass with ice and top up with cold water or milk.

## 3. Dalgona coffee

Whipped coffee foam on cold milk.

1. Put **2 tbsp instant coffee**, **2 tbsp sugar** and **2 tbsp hot water** in a bowl.
2. **Whip** until creamy and pale (2–3 minutes with a hand mixer, longer by hand).
3. Spoon it onto a glass of cold milk over ice.

## 4. Affogato

The Italian dessert: ice cream and hot espresso.

1. Put **1–2 scoops of vanilla ice cream** in a bowl.
2. Pour **a shot of hot espresso** (or moka pot coffee) over it.
3. Serve right away.

## 5. Espresso tonic

Fresh and bittersweet – a summer café favorite.

1. Fill a glass with ice.
2. Add **120–150 ml (4–5 oz) tonic water**.
3. Slowly pour **a shot of espresso** on top – it foams with the tonic.
4. Add an orange slice if you like.

## What about cold brew?

**Cold brew** steeps cold for hours and tastes smoother; iced coffee is brewed hot and then chilled. Both are delicious – the recipe is in [how to make cold brew](/en/how-to-make-cold-brew/).

## FAQ

**Which coffee for an iced latte?** Medium to dark roasts with chocolate notes pair well with milk – see [coffee roast levels](/en/coffee-roast-levels/).

**Why is my iced coffee watery?** The coffee isn't strong enough or the ice melts too fast. Use more coffee and coffee ice cubes.

**Can I use plant milk?** Yes – barista oat milk works very well. See [oat milk for coffee](/en/oat-milk-for-coffee/).
