Whole beans are the easiest way to better coffee: ground coffee loses its aroma within minutes to days, while whole beans keep it for weeks. But the shelf holds dozens of options – espresso, breakfast blend, 100% Arabica, "barista", dark roast. Which of these actually matters?

> **Short answer:** Look at the **roast date** first (no older than 1–2 months) and only buy as much as you'll use in **3–4 weeks**. For espresso and super-automatics, a **darker roast** works well, ideally with a little Robusta. For drip, pour-over and French press, a **light to medium roast** of 100% Arabica is usually the better choice.

## 6 things to check before you buy

1. **Roast date, not best-by date:** Supermarket coffee often only shows a best-by date, which can be up to two years after roasting. Beans taste best roughly 1–6 weeks after roasting. Roasters print the roast date on the bag.
2. **Roast level:**
   - **Light:** fruity, bright acidity, complex – ideal for pour-over and drip machines.
   - **Medium:** balanced, nutty, chocolatey – works with almost everything.
   - **Dark:** bold, bittersweet chocolate, low acidity – the classic for espresso and milk drinks.
3. **Arabica or Robusta?** Arabica is more aromatic and milder; Robusta is stronger and more bitter, with more caffeine and a denser crema. Many espresso blends combine both. We explain the difference in [Arabica vs. Robusta](/en/arabica-vs-robusta/).
4. **One-way valve:** The small round valve lets carbon dioxide out but no air in. A good bag has one.
5. **Origin:** Single origin beans show the typical flavors of one country – berry-like from Ethiopia or chocolatey from Brazil, for example. Blends are often rounder and more consistent.
6. **Bag size:** A 2 lb (1 kg) bag is cheaper, but only makes sense if you'll finish it in about a month – or store it in airtight portions.

## Supermarket, online or roaster?

- **Supermarket:** cheap and available everywhere, but usually without a roast date and often stored for a long time. Perfectly fine for milk drinks from a super-automatic.
- **Online (e.g. Amazon):** huge selection, including roaster coffee with a roast date. Read the reviews and look for the roast date in the description.
- **Local roaster:** the freshest, with advice included – but usually the most expensive.

## How to store beans

1. **Airtight:** In the original bag with a valve (tightly closed) or in a storage canister.
2. **Dark and cool:** Not next to the stove and not in the sun.
3. **Not in the fridge:** Moisture and odors harm the aroma. Freezing only works in portions, in well-sealed bags – and don't refreeze the beans.
4. **Grind just before brewing:** This is where the flavor is won or lost. To find the right grinder, see [Best coffee grinder](/en/best-coffee-grinder/).

## How many beans do I need?

A cup of drip coffee (250 ml / about 8 oz) takes roughly 15 g, a double espresso 16–18 g. If you drink two cups a day, you'll need around 1 kg (about 2 lb) a month. For your exact amount, try the [coffee ratio calculator](/en/coffee-ratio-calculator/).

## FAQ

**What does "breakfast blend" mean?** Usually a light to medium roast designed for a mild, easy-drinking cup of drip coffee. Labels like this aren't standardized, so check the roast level and roast date.

**Are expensive beans always better?** No. Freshness and the right roast level matter more than price. Freshly roasted mid-priced beans often beat expensive but stale ones.

**Can I use espresso beans for drip coffee?** Yes. The coffee will be stronger and more bitter. If you like fruity flavors, a lighter roast is the better choice. More on coffee styles in [Types of coffee](/en/types-of-coffee/).
