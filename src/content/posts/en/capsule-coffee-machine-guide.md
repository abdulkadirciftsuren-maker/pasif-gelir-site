Pop in a capsule, press a button, done: a capsule machine is the easiest way to get espresso at home. It's small, cheap to buy and almost maintenance-free. But which system suits you – and how much will it cost over time? This guide compares the main systems.

> **Short answer:** If you mostly drink **espresso** and want to save money, go for **Nespresso Original** – it has the widest choice of affordable compatible capsules. If you like **large mugs**, **Nespresso Vertuo** is the better fit. If you want **milk drinks from a capsule**, look at **Dolce Gusto**. If you drink many cups a day, a [super-automatic espresso machine](/en/super-automatic-espresso-machine-guide/) works out cheaper in the long run.

Availability of each system differs from country to country, so check which capsules are easy to buy where you live.

## What does a cup cost?

The machine is cheap; the capsules are where the money goes. As a rough guide (prices vary by country and brand):

- **Compatible capsules (Original):** the cheapest capsule option
- **Nespresso-brand Original capsules:** roughly one and a half to two times the price of compatibles
- **Vertuo capsules:** usually the most expensive, and large cups cost more
- **For comparison – super-automatic or espresso machine with whole beans:** typically well under half the cost of branded capsules

If you drink two cups a day, capsules can easily add up to **a few hundred dollars a year**. At that point a super-automatic machine can pay for itself after a while.

## What to look for when buying

1. **Choose the system first:** Capsules are not interchangeable between systems. Decide based on the drinks you want and the capsule price, not the machine price.
2. **Water tank:** Small machines often hold less than 1 liter (about 34 oz) – annoying if several people use it.
3. **Cup height:** Does your latte glass fit under the spout? Many machines have a fold-down cup tray.
4. **Milk:** Some models have a built-in or bundled milk frother, but a separate [milk frother](/en/milk-frother-guide/) works just as well.
5. **Programmable cup size:** Handy so your espresso and lungo come out the same every time.
6. **Descaling:** Capsule machines build up scale too. How to do it: [How to descale a coffee maker](/en/how-to-descale-a-coffee-maker/).

## Capsules and the environment

- **Aluminum capsules** are recyclable in principle, but whether they're accepted in household recycling depends on where you live – check your local recycling rules. Nespresso also runs its own capsule return programs in many countries.
- **Compostable capsules** are available for the Original system. Check whether they can go in your food or yard waste collection – this varies by municipality.
- **Refillable stainless steel capsules** avoid waste altogether and are the cheapest option. You'll need ground coffee and a little patience.

## Tips for better capsule coffee

1. **Preheat:** Run some hot water through without a capsule – it warms up both the machine and the cup.
2. **Warm your cup:** Espresso cools down quickly in a cold cup.
3. **Don't use too much water:** Too much water makes an espresso bitter and thin. Use lungo capsules for bigger cups instead.
4. **Syrup and milk:** For lattes and cappuccinos, just froth some milk – add a [coffee syrup](/en/coffee-syrup-guide/) and it tastes like the café.

## FAQ

**Are compatible capsules bad for the machine?** Usually not. Make sure they're explicitly labeled for your system. If you have problems during the warranty period, it's worth checking the manufacturer's terms.

**Is capsule coffee real espresso?** It's brewed under pressure and comes close to espresso. But you have much less control over the taste than with an espresso machine.

**What about pod machines (like Senseo)?** Soft pods are cheaper than capsules and make mild coffee in large cups – but not espresso.
