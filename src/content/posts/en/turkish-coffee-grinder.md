The smell of freshly ground Turkish coffee can't compare with coffee from a packet. But there's a catch: Turkish coffee has to be ground **powder-fine**, and **most grinders can't get there**. This guide explains **how to grind Turkish coffee at home**, **which mill or grinder** works and **how to test the grind**.

> **Short answer:** The classic, affordable way is a **traditional brass Turkish coffee mill**. If you want one grinder for Turkish **and** espresso or filter, choose a **quality hand grinder with a Turkish setting**. Cheap blade grinders and most electric home grinders can't grind fine enough. No grinder? Have a **Turkish or Middle Eastern grocer or roaster** grind it fresh and buy small amounts.

## How fine should Turkish coffee be?

Turkish coffee is **finer than espresso**: between your fingers it should feel like **flour or powdered sugar**, with no grit. That's roughly **100–200 microns**.

**What happens if it's too coarse?**

- Little, patchy foam.
- The grounds don't settle properly and end up in your mouth.
- The coffee tastes thin and weak.

On buying the coffee itself: [how to buy Turkish coffee](/en/how-to-buy-turkish-coffee/).

## 1. The traditional brass mill

Cylindrical **brass (or copper) Turkish coffee mills** have been used for centuries and are still one of the best options. Their steel burrs grind coffee **powder-fine**.

- **Adjusting:** Use the screw at the bottom. Close it fully, then open in quarter turns and test.
- **Speed:** About **1–2 minutes** of cranking for one cup (7 g).
- **Watch out:** Cheap models sometimes have soft burrs; look for steel burrs and good reviews.
- **Bonus:** It looks great in the kitchen and makes a **much-loved gift**.

## 2. Hand grinders with a Turkish setting

Some high-end hand grinders (e.g. the **1Zpresso J series** or **Comandante**) can reach Turkish fineness. The upside: the same grinder handles **espresso, filter and French press**.

- Make sure the description clearly mentions a **"Turkish" setting**.
- Grinding very fine takes a little longer.
- For choosing a grinder in general: [best coffee grinder](/en/best-coffee-grinder/).

## 3. Electric grinders

Most electric **home** grinders don't go finer than espresso. Models that do Turkish are mostly **commercial** grinders and expensive. Before buying, check that a Turkish setting is listed.

A **blade grinder** (like a spice grinder) can make fine dust, but **unevenly**: chunks and powder mixed, and the motor heats the coffee. In a pinch, grind in **short pulses** and sift through a fine strainer.

## Step by step: grinding with a brass mill

1. **Weigh the beans:** About **7 g** per cup (a heaped teaspoon of ground coffee).
2. **Set it as fine as it goes:** Close the mill, then open it just a touch.
3. **Grind:** Turn steadily and calmly.
4. **Test:** Rub a pinch between your fingers. If it feels **like flour**, you're there; if you feel grit, go finer.
5. **Brew right away:** Ground coffee loses aroma fast. Recipe: [how to make Turkish coffee](/en/how-to-make-turkish-coffee/).

## Which beans?

- **Classic:** Medium roast, a Brazil-heavy blend; chocolatey and smooth.
- **Fruitier:** Light roast from Ethiopia or Colombia; livelier and lighter.
- **Dibek style:** Grind a little coarser and use a mixed roast. See [dibek coffee](/en/dibek-coffee/).
- To try specialty beans: [where to buy specialty coffee](/en/where-to-buy-specialty-coffee/).

## Care

- **Don't wash the mill with water**: brass tarnishes and the burrs can rust. Clean it with a dry brush.
- Grind **grinder cleaning pellets** once a month to remove old coffee oils.
- Caring for other grinders and machines: [how to clean a coffee maker](/en/how-to-clean-a-coffee-maker/).

## Fresh Turkish coffee without a grinder

- **Have it ground:** Many Turkish and Middle Eastern grocers and roasters grind beans fresh for Turkish coffee. Buy **3–8 oz (100–250 g)** a week.
- **Small packs:** If you buy it pre-ground, choose small packs with the **freshest date**.
- When brewing in a **cezve** or a **Turkish coffee machine**, the grind matters even more. See [how to choose a cezve](/en/how-to-choose-a-cezve/) and [Turkish coffee machine guide](/en/turkish-coffee-machine-guide/).

## FAQ

**Can I use a regular coffee grinder for Turkish coffee?** Usually not; most can't grind fine enough. A Turkish setting needs to be clearly listed.

**Brass mill or modern hand grinder?** If you only drink Turkish coffee, a **brass mill** is ideal and beautiful. If you also make filter and espresso, a **hand grinder with a Turkish setting** makes more sense.

**How long does ground Turkish coffee stay fresh?** About **1–2 weeks** in an airtight container. Freshly ground, it smells best in the first few minutes.

**Does the grind matter for cup reading?** Yes. Fine, fresh coffee gives better foam and more even grounds. See [Turkish coffee fortune telling](/en/turkish-coffee-fortune-telling/).
