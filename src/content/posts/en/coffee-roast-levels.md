The same green coffee bean can taste fruity and lively or chocolatey and bold depending on how it's roasted. **Roast level** is one of the biggest factors in how your coffee tastes. Here's the difference between light, medium and dark – and which one suits you.

> **Short answer:** **Light roasts** are fruity and bright – great for V60 and Chemex. **Medium roasts** are balanced and work almost everywhere; Turkish coffee is usually medium roast too. **Dark roasts** are bold, chocolatey and low in acidity – the classic for espresso and milk drinks. The caffeine difference is much smaller than most people think.

## What happens during roasting?

Green coffee barely smells like coffee. During roasting, sugars caramelize, hundreds of aroma compounds form and the bean turns brown.

1. **Drying:** The bean loses water and turns yellowish.
2. **Browning:** Sugars caramelize; you smell bread and nuts.
3. **First crack:** The beans pop like popcorn. A light roast is done shortly after.
4. **Development:** Acidity drops, sweetness and body rise – this is medium roast territory.
5. **Second crack:** A quieter crackle. Oils come to the surface and dark roast begins.

## The three roast levels compared

| | Light | Medium | Dark |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Color | Light brown, dry | Medium brown, dry | Dark brown, often oily |
| Flavor | Fruity, floral, tea-like | Nuts, caramel, chocolate | Dark chocolate, roasty, smoky |
| Acidity | High | Medium | Low |
| Body | Light | Medium | Full |
| Origin character | Very clear | Clear | Roast flavor dominates |
| Best for | V60, Chemex, AeroPress | Drip, French press, Turkish coffee | Espresso, moka pot, milk drinks |

## The caffeine myth

"Dark roast is stronger, so it has more caffeine" – **not true.** Caffeine barely changes during roasting. Dark beans are a little lighter and larger, so if you measure by the **scoop**, light roast gives you slightly more caffeine; by **weight**, the difference is very small. The amount of coffee and the bean type matter far more – see [Arabica vs Robusta](/en/arabica-vs-robusta/).

## Reading the label

- **"Light / medium / dark"** is the clearest.
- **"Espresso roast"** usually means medium-dark, but it isn't standardized – every roaster defines it differently.
- **"Filter roast"** or "omni roast" is usually light to medium.
- **Names like City, Full City, Vienna, French or Italian** run roughly from medium to very dark.
- **Roast date:** As important as roast level – more in [how to buy coffee beans](/en/how-to-buy-coffee-beans/).

## Roast level and taste problems

- Always **sour** with a light roast? Grind finer or switch to a medium roast.
- Always **bitter or smoky** with a dark roast? Use slightly cooler water or try a medium roast.

## FAQ

**Which roast is best?** There's no single right answer. Fruity: light. Balanced: medium. Bold and with milk: dark.

**Are oily beans bad?** No, it's normal for dark roasts. But they can clog the grinder in super-automatic machines. A very oily light roast may be old.

**Can you roast coffee at home?** Yes, but it's smoky and smelly, and an even roast takes practice. Freshly roasted beans from a roaster are easier to start with.
