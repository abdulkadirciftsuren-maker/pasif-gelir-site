Nothing shapes the taste in your café as much as the **beans**. Even the best machine can't save stale or badly roasted coffee. This guide covers **where to buy your coffee**, what **prices per kilo** to expect, **how much coffee** you need each month, how to **test roasters** and what to watch for in **contracts**.

> **Short answer:** For most new cafés, a **local roaster** is the best choice: fresh coffee, a personal relationship and barista training. Ask at least **3 roasters for samples** and test them **as espresso and cappuccino** on your own machine with your own water. Read **long machine contracts** that tie you to one roaster very carefully.

*Note: Prices are rough 2026 wholesale prices in Europe, excluding VAT. Use the same method to compare your own quotes.*

## 1. Where can you buy your coffee?

| Option | Pros | Cons |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Big brand (e.g. Italian espresso brands)** | Known name, consistent taste, often machine and signage support | Less fresh, doesn't set you apart, long contracts |
| **Local roaster** | Fresh coffee, personal contact, training and dial-in help, a "local" story | Small roasters can vary in capacity and consistency |
| **Specialty roaster** | Top quality, single origins, seasonal coffees | Higher price per kilo, needs more knowledge on your team |
| **Roasting yourself** | Full control, strong brand | Roaster, space, permits and expertise; not recommended in year one |

Many cafés use **two sources**: a steady **espresso blend** for milk drinks and rotating **single origins** for filter and a "guest coffee". More: [what is specialty coffee](/en/what-is-specialty-coffee/) and [arabica vs robusta](/en/arabica-vs-robusta/).

## 2. Prices per kilo

| Type of coffee | Approx. wholesale price (per kg, excl. VAT) |
| --- | --- |
| **Big brand / commercial espresso blend** | €12–18 |
| **Good espresso blend from a local roaster** | €18–26 |
| **Specialty single origin (filter or espresso)** | €26–45 |
| **Decaf** | €2–5 above the regular price |
| **Turkish coffee (ground)** | €12–22 |

Prices usually drop for larger volumes and longer agreements. (1 kg is about 2.2 lb.)

## 3. How much coffee do you need each month?

Count on **about 14 g** of coffee per drink (singles and doubles mixed). With a blend at €22/kg:

| Drinks per day | Coffee per day | Coffee per month (30 days) | Cost per month |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **100** | ~1.4 kg | ~42 kg | ~€920 |
| **200** | ~2.8 kg | ~84 kg | ~€1,850 |
| **400** | ~5.6 kg | ~168 kg | ~€3,700 |

Add **50–100 g a day** for dialing in the grinder. To see your cost per cup, use the [coffee shop cost calculator](/en/coffee-shop-cost-calculator/).

## 4. How to test roasters

1. **Pick 3–5 roasters** and ask each for a **1 kg sample**. Many give cafés samples free or at a discount.
2. **Test on your own machine with your own water.** What tastes great in the roaster's showroom may taste different on your counter.
3. **Taste it as espresso and cappuccino.** Most customers drink milk drinks; the blend has to shine with milk too.
4. **Taste blind:** Number the bags and ask your team and a few regulars which they like best.
5. **Use it for a week:** Does it taste the same every day? Do you keep re-adjusting the grinder?
6. Ask the roaster for a **cupping**: the best way to taste coffees side by side.

## 5. Questions to ask a roaster

- **Roast date:** Is the roast date on the bag? How often do they roast?
- **Delivery:** How often do they deliver? Minimum order? Shipping costs?
- **Consistency:** How do they keep the blend the same through the seasons?
- **Training and support:** Barista training, grinder dial-in help, menu ideas?
- **Packaging:** 1 kg bags with a one-way valve? Recyclable?
- **Payment:** Upfront or 14–30 day terms?
- **Certifications:** Organic, Fairtrade or direct trade? A good story for your menu.
- **Decaf and Turkish coffee:** Can you get them from the same roaster?

## 6. Machine contracts: be careful!

Some roasters and big brands supply the **espresso machine and grinder free or cheap** if you commit to buying **a set amount of coffee** for **a set time** (often 3–5 years). That saves cash at the start, but:

- **Minimum purchase:** How many kg a month? What happens if sales are lower?
- **Price per kilo:** If the machine is "free", its cost is usually built into the coffee. Work out the total.
- **Term and exit:** What does leaving early cost? Who owns the machine?
- **Service:** Who comes when it breaks, and how fast?

For machine choice and buying, renting or leasing, see our [commercial espresso machine guide](/en/commercial-espresso-machine-guide/).

## 7. Freshness and storage

- **Rest for espresso:** Waiting **5–10 days** after roasting usually gives a more balanced espresso. For filter, 3–5 days is enough.
- **Shelf life:** Use unopened valve bags within **4–6 weeks** of roasting and opened bags within **a week**.
- **Ordering:** Once or twice a week, enough for **1–2 weeks**.
- **Storage:** Cool, dark and dry, not in the fridge. Only put as much in the grinder hopper as you'll use **that day**.

More: [how to tell if coffee is fresh](/en/how-to-tell-if-coffee-is-fresh/) and [how to store coffee beans](/en/how-to-store-coffee-beans/).

## 8. Extra income: selling beans in your café

Selling beans in **250 g (8 oz) bags** brings extra income and works as advertising. Retail prices are usually **1.5–2 times** the wholesale price. Some roasters will pack the coffee **under your café's name** (private label), a powerful branding tool. Add a short note on grinding and storage for customers.

## FAQ

**How many roasters should I work with?** At the start, **one main roaster** is enough. A second one for filter or a "guest coffee" adds variety to your menu.

**Does cheaper coffee mean more profit?** A little, in the short term. But the difference per cup is usually **a few cents**, and customers taste it right away. Saving on coffee is the most expensive saving there is.

**Should I roast my own coffee?** Not in the first year. You need a roaster, ventilation, permits and experience. Once the café is running well, you can think about it.

**How often should I switch roasters?** If taste and service are good, don't: customers love consistency. Bring variety with a "guest coffee" instead.
