You walk into a café in Naples. The man ahead of you leaves money for two coffees, drinks one and walks out. The barista makes a note: **"One sospeso."** A few hours later, someone with empty pockets looks in and asks: *"C'è un sospeso?"*, "Is there a suspended coffee?" And they get their espresso.

This is one of Naples' loveliest traditions: the **caffè sospeso**, the **suspended coffee**.

## The kindness of a cup of coffee

The idea is simple: someone pays for **one extra coffee** along with their own. That coffee is "suspended", and later a stranger who can't afford it drinks it. Nobody says thank you, nobody learns a name. The good deed is as quiet as a cup of coffee.

For Neapolitans, coffee isn't a luxury but part of the day: drunk standing at the bar in a few sips, with a glass of water on the side. So the thought that "everyone deserves a coffee" fits the city perfectly.

## Where did it come from?

Nobody knows exactly. Most accounts place it in **Naples' working-class neighborhoods** during the hard years of the 20th century: someone who'd had a good day shared their luck with a stranger. The custom had nearly faded when Neapolitan writer **Luciano De Crescenzo** brought it back into the spotlight with his 2008 book *Il caffè sospeso*.

## Around the world

With the economic crisis of the early 2010s, the idea spread on social media. As **"suspended coffee"**, cafés from Europe to the Americas, from Russia to Australia, started similar schemes, some adding a sandwich or a bowl of soup.

## Its Turkish cousin: bread on the hook

In Turkey the idea is old. With **"askıda ekmek"** ("bread on the hook"), you pay the baker for two loaves and "hang" one for someone in need. The custom is traced back to Ottoman times and lives on in many bakeries. Today, some Turkish cafés also have an **"askıda kahve"** (suspended coffee) board. A different name, the same spirit: **give without seeing, take without shame.**

## Next time you order a coffee

- **In Naples:** Just say "**Un caffè e un sospeso**" (one coffee and one suspended) at the register. Some historic cafés still keep the tradition.
- **At home:** Ask your local café about a suspended-coffee board. If there isn't one, suggest it: a board, a pen and a few slips are all it takes.
- **Grace:** We don't question or watch someone who takes a suspended coffee. The beauty of it is that no one feels in debt.

## The coffee of this story: espresso the Naples way

In Naples, espresso is **short, dark and hot**, often preceded by a sip of **water** to clear the palate. In some bars the sugar goes into the cup before the coffee, forming a sweet foam on top.

## FAQ

**What does caffè sospeso mean?** Italian for "suspended coffee": a coffee paid in advance and left for a stranger.

**Who can take a suspended coffee?** Anyone who needs one. No one asks for an explanation.

**Is it a promotion?** No, it's a tradition. Some cafés organize it with boards, but at heart it's entirely voluntary.
