A super-automatic espresso machine (also called a bean-to-cup machine) does it all: beans in, press a button – espresso, a long coffee or a cappuccino, freshly ground in your cup. But there are big differences between an entry-level model and a premium one that costs several times as much, and not every expensive feature is worth it.

> **Short answer:** If you mostly drink **black coffee**, a solid entry-level model with a **removable brew group** is enough. If you drink a **cappuccino or latte** every day, an **automatic milk system that's easy to clean** is worth it. Spending more mainly buys convenience – the taste depends heavily on **fresh beans** and **regular care**.

## 7 things to check before you buy

1. **Milk system:** This is the biggest difference.
   - **Steam wand (manual):** You froth the milk yourself in a pitcher. Cheaper, but takes a little practice.
   - **Automatic with a tube:** Milk is drawn straight from the carton or a container. Convenient, but the tube and spout need rinsing every day.
   - **Automatic with a carafe or tube-free:** Parts go in the dishwasher or rinse off quickly – the most hygienic option.
2. **Brew group:** If it's **removable**, you can rinse it under the tap once a week. Machines with a fixed brew group clean themselves, but need cleaning tablets and occasional servicing.
3. **Grinder:** Conical burr grinders made of ceramic or steel are standard. What matters is that the **grind size has several settings**.
4. **Coffee strength and temperature:** Good machines let you set the amount of beans, water and the temperature. With a **double-shot function** (grinding twice), a large cup doesn't turn watery.
5. **Bypass or ground-coffee chute:** With a bypass, the machine adds hot water separately – so a large cup of coffee gets stronger instead of bitter. A ground-coffee chute lets you use decaf in between.
6. **Size and water tank:** Measure the space under your wall cabinets – with the bean hopper lid open! A water tank of around 1.5 liters (about 50 oz) or more is comfortable for two people.
7. **Noise and controls:** Grinders differ noticeably in volume. A clear display or buttons matter more in daily use than an app.

## Which price tier suits you?

| Price tier | Typical features | Who is it for? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Entry level | Steam wand, simple controls | Espresso and black coffee drinkers |
| Mid-range | Automatic milk system, display, more drinks | Daily cappuccino and latte drinkers |
| Premium | User profiles, two bean hoppers, automatic milk cleaning, quieter | Families, high expectations for convenience |

Prices vary a lot – many models are much cheaper on sale. Watch the price for a few weeks before you buy.

## Running costs: what does a cup cost?

Per cup, a super-automatic is much cheaper than capsules (see the [capsule coffee machine guide](/en/capsule-coffee-machine-guide/)). Count on about 8–10 g of beans per espresso, so the cost per shot is only a small fraction of the price of a capsule. On top of that come water filters, descaler and cleaning tablets – a modest yearly amount, depending on the model and your water hardness.

## Care: how to keep the coffee tasting good

1. **Daily:** Empty the grounds container and drip tray, rinse the milk system.
2. **Weekly:** Rinse a removable brew group under lukewarm water and let it dry.
3. **Regularly:** Descale when the machine tells you to – a water filter extends the intervals. How to do it: [How to descale a coffee maker](/en/how-to-descale-a-coffee-maker/).
4. **As instructed:** Cleaning tablets against coffee oils in the brew group.

Machine acting up (no coffee, milk won't froth)? See [super-automatic espresso machine problems and fixes](/en/super-automatic-troubleshooting/).

## The right beans for a super-automatic

**Medium roasts** work best – look for blends sold for espresso or for bean-to-cup machines. Very dark, shiny, oily beans can gum up the grinder. What to look for when buying beans: [How to buy coffee beans](/en/how-to-buy-coffee-beans/). The difference between the species: [Arabica vs robusta](/en/arabica-vs-robusta/).

## Super-automatic or an alternative?

- **Manual espresso machine:** more control and often better espresso, but more hands-on work and practice – see the [home espresso machine guide](/en/home-espresso-machine-guide/).
- **Drip coffee maker:** cheaper and ideal for big pots of black coffee – see the [drip coffee maker guide](/en/drip-coffee-maker-guide/).
- **Moka pot + milk frother:** the cheapest way to make a cappuccino at home – see [How to use a moka pot](/en/how-to-use-a-moka-pot/) and the [milk frother guide](/en/milk-frother-guide/).

## FAQ

**How long does a super-automatic espresso machine last?** With good care, often 5–10 years. Regular descaling and cleaning is the most important factor.

**Why does my coffee from a super-automatic taste bitter?** Common reasons: beans that are too dark, a grind that's too fine, too much water for one cup (without bypass) or a dirty brew group. Set the grind slightly coarser and reduce the water amount.

**Is a super-automatic worth it for one person?** Yes, if you drink several cups a day. For one or two cups on the weekend, a moka pot or a [French press](/en/how-to-use-a-french-press/) is cheaper.
