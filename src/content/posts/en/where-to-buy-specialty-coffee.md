Want to try specialty coffee but don't know where to start? You'll rarely find it at the supermarket, and online there are hundreds of roasters and cryptic labels: "washed", "natural", "jasmine, bergamot"... This guide covers **where to buy specialty coffee**, **how to read what's on the bag** and **what to choose the first time**.

> **Short answer:** Your best bet is a **local or online roaster**: the bag should show the **roast date**, **origin** (country, region, farm) and **process**. For a first try, buy a **12 oz (250–340 g) bag**, tell them how you brew (filter or espresso) and get **whole beans**. If you're wondering what specialty coffee actually is, start with [what is specialty coffee](/en/what-is-specialty-coffee/).

## Where to buy

| Where? | Pros | Cons |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Local roaster / specialty café** | Freshest coffee, taste before you buy, advice | Not in every town |
| **Roaster's own web shop** | Wide choice, often roasted to order, clear roast date | Shipping time and cost |
| **Coffee subscription** | New coffees every month, regular and fresh | You'll get some you don't love; check cancellation terms |
| **Marketplaces (e.g. Amazon)** | Easy, fast, reviews | Roast date not always clear, stock may sit in a warehouse |
| **Supermarket** | Everywhere | Specialty is rare, roast dates often missing |

**Tip:** On marketplaces, check that **the roaster itself** is selling and that a **roast date** is listed. More on freshness: [how to tell if coffee is fresh](/en/how-to-tell-if-coffee-is-fresh/).

## Reading the bag

A good specialty coffee bag tells you:

| Label | What it tells you | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Origin** | Country, region, sometimes farm or co-op | Ethiopia, Yirgacheffe |
| **Process** | How the fruit was removed; has a big effect on taste | Washed, natural, honey |
| **Varietal** | The variety of coffee plant | Heirloom, Bourbon, Caturra, Geisha |
| **Altitude** | Higher usually means livelier acidity | 6,200–6,900 ft (1,900–2,100 m) |
| **Roast date** | The most important freshness sign | 10/12/2026 |
| **Roast profile** | Which brew method it suits | Filter, espresso, omni |
| **Tasting notes** | The coffee's own flavors (nothing added) | Jasmine, bergamot, peach |
| **Score** | On the SCA scale; 80+ is specialty | 86 |

Flavor differences by region: [coffee growing regions](/en/coffee-growing-regions/). Roast levels: [coffee roast levels](/en/coffee-roast-levels/).

## Process: the label that changes the taste most

| Process | How? | Taste |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Washed** | The fruit is removed with water, then the bean dries | Clean, bright, clear acidity, floral and citrusy |
| **Natural** | The whole cherry dries in the sun | Fruity (strawberry, blueberry), sweet, full-bodied |
| **Honey** | Some fruit stays on the bean as it dries | Sweet, round, in between |
| **Experimental (anaerobic etc.)** | Special fermentation | Very intense and unusual; not for everyone |

In other words: a **washed process** coffee tastes cleaner, a **natural process** coffee fruitier, and a **honey process** coffee sits in between.

## What to choose the first time

| What do you like? | Try | Tasting notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Chocolate, nuts, caramel** | Brazil or Colombia, medium roast | Chocolate, nuts, sweet |
| **Floral and light, almost tea-like** | Washed Ethiopian, light roast | Jasmine, lemon, bergamot |
| **Fruity and sweet** | Natural Ethiopian | Blueberry, strawberry |
| **Balanced and smooth** | Guatemala or El Salvador | Cocoa, red apple |
| **For milk drinks** | Espresso blend (medium roast) | Chocolate, caramel |

**Tell the roaster how you brew:** Light to medium roasts suit filter ([V60](/en/v60-pour-over-guide/), French press), medium to darker roasts suit espresso. Roasters usually label coffees "filter" or "espresso".

## Prices: is it worth it?

In Europe, 250 g of specialty coffee usually costs **€9–18**, special micro-lots **€20 and up**. In the US, a 12 oz bag typically runs **$16–25**.

**Per cup:** A 250 g bag at 15 g per cup makes about **16 cups**. A €14 bag works out to **about €0.90 a cup**, roughly a third of a filter coffee at a café. To work out your own recipe: [coffee ratio calculator](/en/coffee-ratio-calculator/).

## Buying online: checklist

- [ ] Is there a **roast date**? "Roasted to order" is ideal.
- [ ] **Whole bean** or **ground**? Buy beans and grind at home if you can. See [best coffee grinder](/en/best-coffee-grinder/).
- [ ] Buying ground? Pick your **brew method** (Turkish, filter, espresso).
- [ ] Order enough for **2–3 weeks**; more goes stale.
- [ ] What's the **delivery time** and the free-shipping threshold?
- [ ] For subscriptions: can you **pause** and **cancel**?

## After you buy

- **Let it rest:** **3–5 days** after roasting for filter and **5–10 days** for espresso usually taste more balanced.
- **Store it right:** In its valve bag or an airtight container, somewhere cool and dark; not in the fridge. See [how to store coffee beans](/en/how-to-store-coffee-beans/).
- **Take notes:** Which coffee did you like with which method? It makes the next purchase easier.
- **Not tasting as expected?** It's usually the brewing. See [why is my coffee bitter](/en/why-is-my-coffee-bitter/).

## As a gift

Specialty coffee makes a great gift for coffee lovers. A **tasting set** (three origins in small bags) or a one-month **subscription** go down especially well. More ideas: [coffee gift ideas](/en/coffee-gift-ideas/).

## FAQ

**Can I use specialty coffee for Turkish coffee?** Yes. Ask the roaster to grind it powder-fine or grind it very fine at home. Light roasts make a fruitier, lighter Turkish coffee.

**Are tasting notes added flavors?** No. Notes like "strawberry" or "chocolate" describe the coffee's **own** flavor; nothing is added.

**Is expensive coffee always better?** No. Price rises with rarity, farm and process. For most people, a **fresh, mid-priced single origin** is the best start.

**Ground or whole bean?** **Whole bean** if you can. Ground coffee loses its aroma within days. Without a grinder, buy small bags and finish them within 1–2 weeks.
