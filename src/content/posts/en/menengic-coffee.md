Menengiç coffee is called "coffee", but it **isn't coffee at all**. It's made from the roasted and crushed fruits of the **terebinth tree** (menengiç), which grows wild in southeastern Anatolia. It's **caffeine-free**, cooked with milk and tastes nutty, slightly resinous and creamy – ideal for evenings and for anyone avoiding caffeine.

> **Short answer:** Per cup, stir **1–2 teaspoons of menengiç paste** into **one Turkish coffee cup of milk** in a cezve, heat it on a **low flame** while stirring, and take it off the heat as soon as the foam rises. If you like, use half milk, half water and sweeten to taste.

## What is menengiç?

Menengiç (Pistacia terebinthus) belongs to the same family as the pistachio. Regionally it's also called **çitlembik** or **bıttım**. Its small fruits turn reddish to purple when ripe, then they're roasted and crushed until they form an oily paste. Menengiç coffee is especially common around **Gaziantep, Adıyaman, Kahramanmaraş, Mardin and Diyarbakır**.

## What does it taste like?

- **Nutty and oily** – reminiscent of pistachios and roasted nuts.
- **Slightly resinous** – with its own aroma, a little like mastic.
- **Smooth and creamy** – with milk it makes plenty of foam.
- **Not bitter** – and caffeine-free, so it's fine in the evening too.

## How to make it

**For 1 cup:** 1–2 teaspoons of menengiç paste (or 2 teaspoons of powder), 1 Turkish coffee cup of milk (or half milk, half water), sugar to taste.

1. **Stir cold:** Mix the menengiç and milk thoroughly in the cezve before turning on the heat.
2. **Lowest setting:** Milk gets too hot quickly, scorches and boils over.
3. **Stir:** Stir now and then at the start so the paste doesn't settle.
4. **Wait for the foam:** When the foam rises, share it between the cups.
5. **Let it rise once more** and pour in the rest.
6. **Serve hot**, traditionally with a glass of water.

A slightly **wider cezve** helps prevent boiling over – see [How to choose a cezve](/en/how-to-choose-a-cezve/).

## What to look for when buying

1. **Ingredients:** Ideally pure menengiç paste, or just menengiç with sugar. Some blends contain coffee, cocoa or other additives. If you want it caffeine-free, check the ingredient list.
2. **Paste or powder:** Paste is more traditional and aromatic, powder more practical.
3. **Small pack:** After opening, store it cool, dry and tightly closed, and use it within a few months.
4. **Allergy note:** Menengiç belongs to the pistachio family. If you're allergic to **pistachios or nuts**, be careful.

## FAQ

**Does menengiç coffee contain caffeine?** Pure menengiç coffee doesn't, because it contains no coffee beans. Some ready-made blends do contain coffee, though – so check the ingredients.

**Can I make it with oat milk?** Yes, that works. Barista versions foam best – more in [Oat milk for coffee](/en/oat-milk-for-coffee/).
