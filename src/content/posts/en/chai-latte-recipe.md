On a cold winter day, a hot glass that smells of cinnamon, cardamom and ginger: the **chai latte** comes from India's **masala chai** and is the star of every café's winter menu. The good news: it's easy to make at home and **far tastier** than ready-made powders. Here's a recipe for **chai concentrate**, a **quick tea-bag version**, **dirty chai** with espresso and **iced chai**.

> **Short answer:** Simmer water with **cinnamon, cardamom, cloves, ginger, peppercorns and star anise** for 10 minutes, take it off the heat, steep **black tea** for 4–5 minutes, strain and sweeten. Mix half a glass of this **concentrate** with **hot frothed milk**. The concentrate keeps for **5 days** in the fridge.

## What is chai?

In India, **"chai"** simply means "tea". The spiced tea-and-milk drink most of us know is **masala chai**: black tea, milk, sugar and spices cooked together. A **chai latte** is the café version made with **frothed milk**. There's no coffee in it; add a shot of **espresso** and it becomes a **dirty chai**.

## The spices

| Spice | What it adds |
| --- | --- |
| **Cinnamon (stick)** | A sweet, warm base |
| **Green cardamom** | The signature chai aroma, fresh and floral |
| **Fresh ginger** | Gentle heat and warmth |
| **Cloves** | Deep, spicy notes (go easy, they dominate) |
| **Black peppercorns** | A light tingle in the background |
| **Star anise** | A faint licorice note (optional) |

Lightly **crushing** the whole spices (in a mortar or with the back of a knife) releases more aroma.

## Chai concentrate (makes 4 drinks)

**Ingredients:**

- 2 cups (500 ml) water
- 2 cinnamon sticks
- 6–8 green cardamom pods, lightly crushed
- 4 cloves
- 5–6 black peppercorns
- 1 star anise (optional)
- 1–1.5 inches (3–4 cm) fresh ginger, thinly sliced (or 1 tsp ground ginger)
- 3 tbsp loose black tea (about 12–15 g) or 4–5 tea bags
- 2–3 tbsp honey or brown sugar (to taste)

**Method:**

1. Put the water and **all the spices** in a small pan and bring to a boil.
2. Simmer gently for **10 minutes**; your kitchen will smell wonderful.
3. Take it off the heat, **add the tea** and steep for **4–5 minutes**. Any longer and it turns bitter.
4. **Strain** and stir in the honey or sugar while warm.
5. Once cool, pour into a bottle with a lid; it keeps in the fridge for **up to 5 days**.

**Which tea?** The classic choice is **Assam**: dark and malty, it holds up even with milk. A strong English breakfast tea works too. For more spiced winter drinks, see [winter coffee drinks](/en/winter-coffee-drinks/).

## Chai latte (1 glass)

1. Warm **½ cup (120 ml) chai concentrate** (don't boil it).
2. Heat **⅔–¾ cup (150–180 ml) milk** to 140–150 °F (60–65 °C) and **froth** it: with a steam wand, an electric frother or by shaking it in a jar. See our [milk frother guide](/en/milk-frother-guide/) and [how to froth milk without a frother](/en/how-to-froth-milk-without-a-frother/).
3. Pour the concentrate into a glass, **add the milk** and spoon the foam on top.
4. Dust with a pinch of **cinnamon**.

## Quick version: tea bag, 5 minutes

No time for concentrate?

1. Add **1 black tea bag**, **¼ tsp cinnamon** and a pinch each of **ground cardamom** and **ginger** to ⅓ cup (100 ml) boiling water.
2. Steep for **4 minutes**, remove the bag and sweeten.
3. Add **⅔ cup (150 ml) hot frothed milk**.

## Traditional masala chai (on the stove, with milk)

The Indian way: simmer ¾ cup (200 ml) water with the spices for 5 minutes, add **¾ cup (200 ml) milk** and **2 tsp tea**, let it rise and fall a few times (watch out, it boils over easily!), strain and drink sweetened. Richer and creamier, with no foam.

## Variations

- **Dirty chai:** A chai latte with **1 shot of espresso**. The best meeting of tea and coffee. For the coffee side, see [espresso drinks explained](/en/espresso-drinks-explained/).
- **Iced chai latte:** Fill a glass with ice, add **½ cup (120 ml) cold concentrate** and **⅔ cup (150 ml) cold milk**. Even better with cold foam. See our [iced latte recipe](/en/iced-latte-recipe/).
- **Vegan chai:** **Barista oat milk** goes beautifully with the spices and foams well. See [oat milk for coffee](/en/oat-milk-for-coffee/).
- **Chai syrup:** Boil the concentrate down with **sugar** (1:1) and you have a **chai syrup** that works in coffee too. See our [coffee syrup guide](/en/coffee-syrup-guide/).

## How much caffeine?

A chai latte has about **25–50 mg** of caffeine depending on the tea, less than half a cup of drip coffee. A **dirty chai** with espresso comes to around **100 mg**.

## A note for cafés

Chai latte is a **high-margin** winter drink: with homemade concentrate, the ingredient cost per glass usually stays around **€0.40–0.70**. For pricing, see [coffee shop menu pricing](/en/coffee-shop-menu-pricing/).

## FAQ

**Store-bought powder or homemade?** Powders are handy but usually **very sweet** with weak flavor. Homemade concentrate tastes better and you control the sugar.

**Is there coffee in a chai latte?** No, a classic chai latte is **tea-based**. If you want coffee, make a **dirty chai**.

**How long do the spices keep?** Whole spices keep their aroma for **1–2 years** in a closed jar, ground spices **6–12 months**.

**My concentrate is cloudy. Is that normal?** Yes, it's spice and tea sediment. Pour it through a fine sieve or a paper filter and it'll be clear.
