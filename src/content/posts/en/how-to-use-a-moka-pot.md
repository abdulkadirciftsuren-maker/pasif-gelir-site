The eight-sided moka pot is a fixture in Italian kitchens. Without an espresso machine it makes **strong, full-bodied, espresso-like coffee**. Many people know moka coffee as bitter and burnt – but that's down to a few small mistakes, not the method. Here's how to make it smooth and slightly sweet.

> **Quick recipe:** Hot water in the bottom chamber (just below the safety valve), medium-fine coffee in the basket (level, not tamped), medium-low heat. As soon as the coffee turns lighter and starts sputtering, take it off and cool the base.

## How a moka pot works

It has three parts: the **bottom chamber** (water), the **funnel basket** (coffee) and the **top chamber** where the brewed coffee collects. As the water heats, steam pressure pushes hot water up through the coffee. The pressure is much lower than in an espresso machine, so moka coffee isn't true espresso – but it's the closest you'll get at home.

## What you need

- A moka pot (1-, 3- and 6-cup sizes are common)
- Medium-fine ground coffee: finer than filter, a little coarser than espresso – like fine sand
- Hot water from a kettle
- A towel or oven glove for the hot parts

The basket sets the dose: **just fill it level.** The ratio is roughly 1:7 to 1:10. For other sizes, see our [coffee ratio calculator](/en/coffee-ratio-calculator/).

## Step by step

1. **Heat the water separately.** Bring it nearly to a boil in a kettle. Starting with cold water leaves the pot on the stove for a long time, "cooking" the grounds – the number one cause of bitterness.
2. **Fill the bottom chamber** with hot water to **just below the safety valve**. Never cover the valve.
3. **Fill the basket.** Add coffee, level it with your finger and wipe off the excess. **Don't tamp** – unlike espresso, moka coffee isn't pressed.
4. **Screw it together.** Hold the hot base with a towel and screw on the top firmly. Wipe grounds off the rim first or it will leak.
5. **Medium-low heat.** Leave the lid open so you can watch. The flame shouldn't reach past the base or the handle will get hot.
6. **Watch the flow.** First it comes out dark and syrupy. When it turns lighter, shows yellowish bubbles and starts sputtering, **take it off the heat immediately.**
7. **Cool the base** under cold water or on a damp towel. This stops extraction so the last bitter drops don't end up in your cup.
8. **Stir and serve.** The coffee in the top chamber varies in strength, so stir before pouring.

## Bitter or sour? Dial it in

| Taste | Likely cause | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bitter, burnt | Started with cold water, heat too high or taken off too late | Hot water, lower heat, take off before sputtering |
| Bitter and harsh | Ground too fine | Grind a little coarser |
| Sour, watery | Too coarse, or basket not full | Grind finer, fill the basket level |
| Metallic | New or freshly washed aluminium pot | Throw away the first 2–3 brews; rinse with water only |
| Coffee won't come up | Tamped or too fine | Don't tamp, grind coarser |

## 3 safety rules

- **Never fill water above the safety valve.**
- **Don't tamp the coffee or grind it too fine** – if water can't pass, pressure builds.
- **Check the gasket.** Replace it when hard or cracked – usually once a year.

## Care

- Take it apart after each use, **rinse with warm water** and let it dry fully before reassembling.
- **No dishwasher** for aluminium – it darkens and makes coffee taste metallic.
- With hard water, descale the bottom chamber now and then with citric acid.

## FAQ

**Is moka pot coffee real espresso?** No. Espresso machines use much higher pressure. Moka coffee is strong and espresso-like, with less crema.

**Can I use Turkish coffee in a moka pot?** Not recommended – the powder-fine grind clogs the filter. For Turkish coffee, use a [cezve](/en/how-to-make-turkish-coffee/).

**What size should I buy?** A moka pot works best when full. One person: 1 or 3 cups; two people: 3 or 6 cups. A "cup" here is a small espresso cup.
