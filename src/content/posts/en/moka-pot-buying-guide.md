A moka pot is the cheapest way to make espresso-style coffee at home. But there are dozens of models – aluminum, stainless steel, induction-ready, in many sizes. Which one suits you? Here's everything to check before you buy. For how to brew with it, see our [moka pot guide](/en/how-to-use-a-moka-pot/).

> **Short answer:** On a **gas or electric stove**, a classic **aluminum** moka pot (like the Bialetti Moka Express) is excellent. For **induction**, you need **stainless steel** or a model with an induction-ready base. Choose the size by **the number of cups you make most often** – a moka pot doesn't work half-full.

## Aluminum or stainless steel?

| | Aluminum | Stainless steel |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Induction | No (works with an adapter plate) | Usually yes |
| Heating | Fast and even | A bit slower |
| Weight | Light | Heavier |
| Care | Hand wash, no soap; not dishwasher-safe | Easier; some are dishwasher-safe |
| Durability | Good, may darken over time | Very good |
| Price | Low | Higher |
| Taste | Classic moka taste | Same method, very similar taste |

**About aluminum:** Aluminum is the classic moka pot material. Don't scrub the inside or use soap; a thin coffee film building up over time is normal. The dishwasher will darken aluminum.

## If you have an induction hob

- **Aluminum moka pots don't work on induction.**
- Your options: a **stainless steel moka pot**, "induction" models with a steel base, or an **induction adapter plate**.
- Test: if a magnet sticks to the base, it works on induction.

## Choosing the right size

Moka pots are sized in **"cups"** – meaning small espresso cups (about 50–60 ml / 2 oz). A moka pot works with a **full basket**, so you can't make 2 cups in a 6-cup pot.

| You usually make… | Suggested size |
| --- | --- |
| 1 person, one espresso | 1- or 2-cup |
| 1–2 people, milk drinks | 3-cup (the most popular size) |
| 2–3 people | 4- to 6-cup |
| A big family | 6- to 9-cup |

Tip: if you drink milk coffee, go one size up – you'll use more coffee once you add milk.

## What else to check

1. **Safety valve:** The small valve on the bottom chamber – the water must stay below it.
2. **Gasket and filter plate:** Choose a brand with easy-to-find spare parts. Replace the gasket yearly or when it starts leaking.
3. **Handle:** Heat-resistant and sturdy.
4. **Lid knob:** Heat-resistant.
5. **"Crema" models:** Some have an extra pressure valve for more foam on top.

## FAQ

**Can I make Turkish coffee in a moka pot?** No. Turkish coffee is ground as fine as flour and cooked in a cezve; in a moka pot it clogs the filter – see [how to choose a cezve](/en/how-to-choose-a-cezve/).

**Which coffee?** Medium to dark roast, ground between espresso and drip (slightly finer than table salt) – see [coffee roast levels](/en/coffee-roast-levels/).

**Why is my moka coffee bitter?** Usually the heat is too high, or the pot stays on the stove while it's still sputtering. More in [why is my coffee bitter or sour?](/en/why-is-my-coffee-bitter/).
