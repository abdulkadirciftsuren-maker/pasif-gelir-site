If you want coffee at the push of a button, you have two options: a **capsule (pod) machine** or a **super-automatic** (bean-to-cup) machine that grinds its own beans. The capsule machine is cheap to buy; the super-automatic is cheap **in the long run**. This guide compares the **real cost**, **taste**, **cleaning** and **which one fits you**.

> **Short answer:** If you drink **one cup a day** or less, have little space and want zero fuss, a **capsule machine** makes sense. At **two or more cups a day**, especially with milk drinks, a **super-automatic** pays for itself in roughly **1.5–3 years** and makes better coffee.

## At a glance

| | Capsule machine | Super-automatic |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Machine price (approx.)** | $70–250 | $400–1,200 |
| **Cost per cup** | $0.35–0.90 | $0.15–0.25 |
| **Taste** | Consistent, but pre-ground | Freshly ground beans; livelier flavor |
| **Coffee choice** | Limited to the system's pods | Any beans you like |
| **Milk drinks** | Separate frother or milk pods | Steam wand or automatic milk system |
| **Cleaning** | Almost none; descale now and then | Daily rinse, weekly brew unit, descaling |
| **Size** | Small | Large and heavy |
| **Waste** | One pod per cup | Just grounds (compostable) |
| **Lifespan** | Usually a few years | 5–10 years with care |

## The real cost: when does a super-automatic pay off?

**Cost per cup:**

- **Capsules:** Brand-name pods cost roughly $0.70–0.90 (Nespresso Original, Vertuo), compatible pods and K-Cups $0.35–0.60.
- **Super-automatic:** A 2 lb (about 900 g) bag of beans costs around $18–25; at 8–10 g per shot that's **about $0.15–0.25 per cup**. Add roughly **$40–60 a year** for descaler, cleaning tablets and water filters.

**Example: two cups a day (730 cups a year)**

| | Capsules ($0.75) | Super-automatic ($0.20) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Machine** | $150 | $600 |
| **Coffee per year** | ~$548 | ~$146 |
| **Upkeep per year** | ~$10 | ~$50 |
| **Total after 1 year** | ~$708 | ~$796 |
| **Total after 2 years** | ~$1,266 | ~$992 |
| **Total after 3 years** | ~$1,824 | ~$1,188 |

So at two cups a day, the super-automatic pays for itself in **a little over a year** with brand-name pods, or **about 3 years** if you buy cheap compatible pods. After that it saves you roughly **$150–350 a year**. At one cup a day, the gap closes very slowly.

**Note:** Pod prices vary a lot by brand and deal, so run the numbers with your own prices.

## Taste: which makes better coffee?

- **Capsules:** The coffee is ground and sealed at the factory, so it's **consistent** but lacks the brightness of freshly ground beans. There are lots of flavors to choose from.
- **Super-automatic:** Grinds **fresh for every cup**, and you choose the beans, even specialty coffee from a local roaster. See [where to buy specialty coffee](/en/where-to-buy-specialty-coffee/).
- **But:** The best espresso still comes from a manual **espresso machine**. See [home espresso machine guide](/en/home-espresso-machine-guide/).

## Milk drinks

- **Capsules:** You'll need a separate **milk frother** (e.g. Aeroccino) or milk pods (Dolce Gusto). See [milk frother guide](/en/milk-frother-guide/).
- **Super-automatic:** Either a **steam wand** (froth it yourself) or an **automatic milk system** (one-touch latte and cappuccino). If you drink a lot of milk drinks, the super-automatic is far more convenient.

## Cleaning and maintenance

- **Capsules:** Empty the pod bin, rinse the tank, descale every 2–3 months. Done.
- **Super-automatic:** Empty the grounds bin and drip tray daily, rinse the brew unit weekly, rinse the milk system after each use, and descale and run cleaning tablets regularly. See [how to clean a coffee maker](/en/how-to-clean-a-coffee-maker/) and [how to descale a coffee maker](/en/how-to-descale-a-coffee-maker/).

## Waste and the environment

A capsule machine creates one pod of waste per cup. **Aluminum** pods are recyclable but need to be collected separately (Nespresso runs its own recycling program); compostable pods exist too. A super-automatic leaves only **coffee grounds**, which make great garden compost.

## Which one is right for you?

**Choose a capsule machine if:**

- you drink **one cup a day** or less,
- your kitchen is small, or it's for a dorm room or office corner,
- you don't want to deal with cleaning,
- everyone at home likes a different flavor.

**Choose a super-automatic if:**

- you drink **two or more cups a day** (or there are several coffee drinkers at home),
- you want **fresh beans** and your own choice of coffee,
- you often drink **lattes and cappuccinos**,
- you can spend more upfront.

## FAQ

**Can a super-automatic use pods?** No. It uses whole beans (some models also take pre-ground coffee).

**Do compatible pods damage the machine?** Good compatible pods usually work fine. Try a small pack first.

**Is super-automatic coffee stronger?** Strength is adjustable: most machines let you set the grind and the dose. It usually tastes **fresher and livelier**.

**Which is better for an office?** Pods for a few people and cups; from about five people up, a super-automatic is cheaper. See [office coffee machine](/en/office-coffee-machine/).
