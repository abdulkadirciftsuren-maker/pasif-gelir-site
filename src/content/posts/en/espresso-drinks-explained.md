Confused by the café menu? The difference between a **ristretto** and a **lungo**, a **macchiato** and a **latte macchiato**, or a **cortado** and a **flat white** is actually simple: **how much water**, **how much milk** and **in what order**. This guide explains espresso drinks **with real measurements**.

> **Short answer:** They all start with **espresso**. A **ristretto** is a shorter, more concentrated shot, a **lungo** a longer, lighter one. An **Americano** is espresso + hot water. A **macchiato** is espresso + a spoonful of foam, a **cortado** espresso + about the same amount of milk. Add more milk and you get a **flat white**, **cappuccino** and **latte**.

For an overview of all coffee drinks, see [types of coffee](/en/types-of-coffee/). The bigger milk drinks are covered in detail in [latte vs cappuccino vs flat white](/en/latte-vs-cappuccino-vs-flat-white/).

## 1. Shot length: ristretto, espresso, lungo

The name depends on **how much liquid** comes out of the same dose of coffee. Example with 18 g of coffee:

| Drink | Ratio (coffee : drink) | From 18 g coffee | Time | Taste |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Ristretto** | 1:1 – 1:1.5 | 18–27 g | 15–20 s | Sweet, dense, syrupy |
| **Espresso** | 1:2 | ~36 g | 25–30 s | Balanced: sweetness, acidity, bitterness |
| **Lungo** | 1:3 – 1:4 | 54–72 g | 35–45 s | Lighter, but can be more bitter |

- **Single / double:** A single shot uses about 7–9 g of coffee, a **doppio** 14–20 g. Most cafés now use a double shot by default.
- **Lungo ≠ Americano:** In a lungo, all the water runs **through the coffee**, so it gets more bitter. In an Americano, water is added **afterwards**.

## 2. With water: Americano, long black, red eye

| Drink | How it's made | Taste |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Americano** | Espresso + 3–7 oz (100–200 ml) hot water on top | Close to drip coffee, long and black |
| **Long black** | Hot water first, **then** espresso on top | Like an Americano, but keeps the crema and tastes a bit stronger |
| **Red eye** | Drip coffee + a shot of espresso | Very strong, lots of caffeine |

**Tip:** Don't use boiling water for an Americano; at **195–200 °F (90–94 °C)** it won't turn bitter.

## 3. A little milk: macchiato, cortado, piccolo

| Drink | Recipe | Glass/cup | Taste |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Macchiato** | Espresso + 1–2 spoonfuls of milk foam | 2–3 oz | Espresso, just "stained" (macchiato = marked) |
| **Cortado** | Espresso + about equal warm milk, little foam | 4–5 oz glass | Balanced, coffee-forward |
| **Piccolo** | Ristretto + 2–3 oz silky milk | 3–4 oz glass | Like a tiny latte, but stronger |
| **Flat white** | Double espresso + ~4 oz finely textured milk | 5–6 oz | Velvety, coffee up front |

**Macchiato or latte macchiato?** Two very different drinks! A **macchiato** is a small espresso with a little foam. A **latte macchiato** is a tall glass of **lots of hot milk** with espresso poured in and foam on top: nicely layered and close to a latte. (The chain-store "caramel macchiato" is really a sweetened latte macchiato.)

## 4. Special and sweet espresso drinks

- **Espresso con panna:** Espresso with a dollop of **whipped cream**. Halfway to dessert.
- **Affogato:** Hot espresso poured over a scoop of **vanilla ice cream**. Perfect in summer.
- **Mocha:** Espresso + chocolate + milk. The chocolate sauce from our [hot chocolate recipe](/en/hot-chocolate-recipe/) works well.
- **Espresso romano:** Espresso with a strip of **lemon peel**.
- **Espresso tonic:** **Tonic water** over ice with espresso poured slowly on top. Fresh and slightly bitter, a summer favorite.
- **Shakerato:** Espresso, ice and a little sugar shaken until frothy, served cold.
- **Corretto:** In Italy, espresso with a splash of **grappa** or liqueur (contains alcohol).

## How to make them at home

- **With an espresso machine:** You can make them all; milk drinks need a steam wand. See our [home espresso machine guide](/en/home-espresso-machine-guide/) and [how to do latte art](/en/how-to-do-latte-art/).
- **With a moka pot:** Not true espresso, but **strong and espresso-like**; good enough for home versions of an Americano, cortado or macchiato. See [how to use a moka pot](/en/how-to-use-a-moka-pot/).
- **With an AeroPress:** A little water makes a strong "espresso-style" coffee. See [how to use an AeroPress](/en/how-to-use-an-aeropress/).

## Ordering in Italy

- **"Un caffè"** = espresso. You don't need to say "espresso" in Italy.
- Ask for **"un latte"** and you'll probably get **a glass of milk**! For a latte, say **"caffè latte"**.
- **Cappuccino** is mostly a **morning** drink; ordering one after lunch marks you as a tourist (but nobody minds).
- Drinking standing at the bar is **cheaper** than sitting at a table.

## How much caffeine?

A single espresso has about **60–80 mg** of caffeine. A ristretto has a little less, a lungo a little more, but the difference is small. In an Americano, cortado or latte, the caffeine depends on the **number of shots**, not the water or milk.

## FAQ

**Which espresso drink is the strongest?** In taste, the **ristretto**. In caffeine, the **red eye** (drip coffee + espresso).

**What's the difference between a cortado and a flat white?** A cortado is smaller, the milk is **barely foamed** and the ratio is about **1:1**. A flat white is a bit bigger, with a **double shot** and silky microfoam.

**Americano or drip coffee?** They taste similar, but an **Americano** is made from espresso and has more body. Drip coffee tastes **cleaner and more aromatic**.

**Does a macchiato have syrup?** A classic macchiato doesn't. To make your own syrup for other drinks, see our [coffee syrup guide](/en/coffee-syrup-guide/).
