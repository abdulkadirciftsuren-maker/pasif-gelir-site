You don't need an expensive machine – or even a milk frother – for a latte or cappuccino at home. With a **jar**, a **French press** or a **whisk** you probably already own, you can make good milk foam in a few minutes. Here are 3 easy ways.

> **Short answer:** The easiest is a **jar**: fill it a third of the way with cold milk, screw on the lid and **shake hard for 30–60 seconds**. Then microwave it without the lid for about **30 seconds**. For the finest foam, use a **French press**: add warm milk and pump the plunger quickly **15–20 times**.

## First: the right milk and temperature

- **Whole milk** gives the creamiest, most stable foam. Lower-fat milk foams more but collapses faster.
- With **plant milks**, choose a **barista** version – see [oat milk for coffee](/en/oat-milk-for-coffee/).
- **Temperature:** about **60–65 °C (140–150 °F)** – hot enough that the container is uncomfortable to hold, but not boiling. Boiled milk loses its sweetness and the foam falls apart.

## Method 1: A jar

**You need:** A microwave-safe jar with a lid.

1. Fill the jar **no more than a third** with **cold milk** – the foam needs room.
2. Screw the lid on tightly and **shake hard for 30–60 seconds**. The milk roughly doubles.
3. **Remove the lid** (metal can't go in the microwave) and heat for about **30 seconds**. The heat stabilizes the foam and it rises to the top.
4. Pour the warm milk into your coffee first, then spoon the foam on top.

⚠️ **Don't shake hot milk in a closed jar** – pressure can build up. Shake it cold, then heat it.

## Method 2: French press (finest foam)

**You need:** A clean French press.

1. Heat the milk on the stove or in the microwave to **60–65 °C (140–150 °F)**.
2. Fill the French press **no more than a third** with milk.
3. Put the lid on and pump the plunger **15–20 times with quick, short strokes** – keep it just below the surface at first.
4. Stop when the milk has roughly doubled. **Tap the pot on the counter** and **swirl** – big bubbles pop and the foam turns glossy and smooth.

This fine foam is even good enough for simple latte art. For brewing coffee in it, see our [French press guide](/en/how-to-use-a-french-press/).

## Method 3: A whisk and small pan

**You need:** A small pan and a whisk.

1. Heat the milk over low heat to **60–65 °C**.
2. Take it off the heat, tilt the pan slightly and either spin the whisk quickly between your palms or whisk hard back and forth for **30–60 seconds**.
3. Once there's foam, add it to your coffee.

It takes more arm work and the foam is coarser, but it works in any kitchen.

## Which method for which drink?

| Method | Foam | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Jar | Medium, airy | Cappuccino, iced latte (unheated) |
| French press | Fine, glossy | Latte, flat white |
| Whisk | Coarse | Cappuccino, hot chocolate |

The differences are explained in [latte vs cappuccino vs flat white](/en/latte-vs-cappuccino-vs-flat-white/).

## The coffee: without an espresso machine

Milk drinks need strong coffee:

- **[Moka pot](/en/how-to-use-a-moka-pot/):** the closest thing to espresso.
- **[AeroPress](/en/how-to-use-an-aeropress/):** a concentrate from 18 g coffee and 90 g water.
- A **strong French press** or **[capsule coffee](/en/capsule-coffee-machine-guide/)** works too.

A pump of [coffee syrup](/en/coffee-syrup-guide/) gets you close to café flavor.

## FAQ

**Can I make cold foam?** Yes. Shake cold milk in the jar and spoon it onto iced coffee without heating.

**Why does my foam collapse straight away?** Usually the milk got too hot, or it was a plant milk without a barista formula. Start colder and try whole milk.

**I drink milk coffee every day – what should I buy?** Then a milk frother will save time – see the [milk frother guide](/en/milk-frother-guide/).
