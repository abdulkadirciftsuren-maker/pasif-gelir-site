Cold brew is made by **steeping coffee in cold water for hours**. It tastes **smoother, sweeter and less bitter** than hot-brewed coffee. It's perfect for summer iced coffee – and very easy: coffee, water and a little patience.

> **Short answer:** Mix **100 g of coarsely ground coffee with 1 liter of cold water** (about 1 cup of coffee to 4 cups of water), steep **12–18 hours in the fridge**, then strain. That's a **concentrate**: mix it roughly 1:1 with water or milk over ice. It keeps **about a week** in the fridge.

## Is cold brew the same as iced coffee?

No:

- **Iced coffee:** Hot-brewed coffee that's cooled down. Quick, but it can turn bitter as it cools.
- **Cold brew:** Never heated; steeps cold for hours. Smoother, sweeter, less bitter.
- **Japanese-style iced coffee:** Hot coffee brewed straight onto ice – fast and aromatic. Iced coffee recipes are in [iced latte and 5 cold coffee drinks](/en/iced-latte-recipe/).

## What you need

- **Coffee:** Medium or medium-dark roast – chocolatey, nutty coffees are ideal. See [coffee roast levels](/en/coffee-roast-levels/).
- **Coarse grind:** Like for a French press (coarse sea salt). A fine grind makes cold brew cloudy and bitter.
- **Cold, good water:** See [best water for coffee](/en/best-water-for-coffee/).
- **A container:** A cold brew pitcher, a large jar or a [French press](/en/how-to-use-a-french-press/).
- **A filter:** Paper filter, fine cloth or the pitcher's own filter.

## Step by step (concentrate)

1. **Grind:** Grind 100 g of coffee coarsely.
2. **Mix:** Put it in the container, add **1 liter of cold water** and stir gently until all the grounds are wet.
3. **Cover and wait:** Cover and steep **12–18 hours in the fridge**. (About 12 hours at room temperature also works, then move it to the fridge.)
4. **Strain:** First through a coarse strainer, then through a **paper filter** for a clean result. With a French press, press the plunger slowly, then filter through paper.
5. **Store:** In a sealed bottle in the fridge for about **a week**.

## How to serve it

| Drink | Recipe |
| --- | --- |
| **Classic cold brew** | 1 part concentrate + 1 part cold water + ice |
| **Cold brew with milk** | 1 part concentrate + 1 part milk + ice |
| **Sweet** | A pump of [vanilla or caramel syrup](/en/coffee-syrup-guide/) |
| **With foam** | Cold milk foam – see [how to froth milk without a frother](/en/how-to-froth-milk-without-a-frother/) |
| **Ready to drink** | Brew with 60–70 g coffee per liter from the start and drink undiluted |

The concentrate is strong – adjust water or milk to taste.

## Common mistakes

| Problem | Cause | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bitter, harsh | Too fine, or steeped over 24 hours | Grind coarser, strain at 12–18 hours |
| Watery | Too little coffee or too short | More coffee, longer steep |
| Cloudy, gritty | Too fine, strained only once | Strain a second time through paper |
| Stale taste | Old coffee | Use freshly roasted beans – see [how to tell if coffee is fresh](/en/how-to-tell-if-coffee-is-fresh/) |

## FAQ

**Does cold brew have more caffeine?** The concentrate is very strong, but diluted it's similar to regular coffee. It depends on how much coffee you use. You can also make it with [decaf beans](/en/decaf-coffee-guide/).

**Can I use Turkish coffee for cold brew?** Not recommended – it's so fine it's hard to strain, and the result is cloudy and bitter.

**Can I use older beans?** Yes, cold brew hides staleness better than hot brewing. Fresh still tastes better.
