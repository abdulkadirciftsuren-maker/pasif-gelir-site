The drip coffee maker is a kitchen classic: add water and coffee, wait a few minutes, and the pot is ready. But not every machine makes good coffee. The difference between a cheap machine and a good one usually comes down to **water temperature** and **how the coffee is kept warm**.

> **Short answer:** Choose a machine that heats the water to **92–96 °C (198–205 °F)**. If your coffee will sit for a while, get a **thermal carafe** – a hot plate makes it bitter. Freshly ground coffee and the right ratio (about **55–65 g per liter**) noticeably improve the taste with any machine.

## 7 things to check before you buy

1. **Brewing temperature:** Ideally around 92–96 °C (198–205 °F). Some cheap machines don't get there – the coffee then tastes sour and flat. Look for temperature specs or an independent certification such as the SCA (Specialty Coffee Association) home brewer certification.
2. **Thermal or glass carafe?**
   - **Glass carafe with hot plate:** keeps coffee warm but slowly "cooks" it. After 20–30 minutes it turns bitter.
   - **Thermal carafe:** keeps coffee warm without reheating and preserves the aroma for hours. The better choice if your coffee sits for a while.
3. **Size:** For 1–2 people, a small 4–6 cup machine is enough. For a family or office, 8–12 cups. Machines usually brew worse when making less than half their capacity.
4. **Pause-and-serve (drip stop):** Stops the flow if you pull the carafe out mid-brew.
5. **Timer:** Handy for coffee right when you wake up – but pre-ground coffee goes stale overnight.
6. **Built-in grinder:** Grinds the beans just before brewing – fresher, but more expensive, bigger and more work to clean.
7. **Permanent or paper filter?** A permanent filter saves paper but lets more oils and fines through. Paper gives a cleaner cup.

## Which machine for whom?

| Situation | Recommended type |
| --- | --- |
| 1–2 people, occasional coffee | Small 4–6 cup machine |
| Family, office | 8–12 cups with thermal carafe |
| Coffee sits for a long time | Model with thermal carafe |
| Best taste | Machine with reliable brewing temperature |
| Effortless mornings | With timer or built-in grinder |
| Tight budget | Simple machine + freshly ground coffee |

## 6 tips for better coffee from any machine

1. **Right ratio:** about 55–65 g of coffee per liter of water. The [coffee ratio calculator](/en/coffee-ratio-calculator/) helps for your number of cups.
2. **Grind fresh:** medium. Which grinder fits is covered in [Best coffee grinder](/en/best-coffee-grinder/).
3. **Rinse the filter:** Briefly wet the paper filter with hot water and pour the water away – this removes the papery taste.
4. **Good water:** In hard-water areas, filtered water is worth it.
5. **Don't leave it on the hot plate:** Transfer it to a thermal carafe or drink it within 20 minutes.
6. **Descale regularly:** Scale lowers the brewing temperature and makes coffee flat. Guide: [How to descale a coffee maker](/en/how-to-descale-a-coffee-maker/).

## Drip coffee maker or pour-over?

**Machine:** convenient and consistent, many cups at once. **Pour-over (V60):** full control and clearer flavors, but you have to stand by it. More in our [V60 pour-over guide](/en/v60-pour-over-guide/).

## FAQ

**What coffee for a drip machine?** Medium-ground drip coffee. Not Turkish coffee – it's too fine, clogs the filter and turns bitter. Which beans fit is covered in [Arabica vs. Robusta](/en/arabica-vs-robusta/).

**How long does drip coffee keep?** It's best in the first 30 minutes. In a thermal carafe, a few hours. By the next day it's stale.

**How much electricity does a drip coffee maker use?** Brewing only takes a few minutes. But if the hot plate stays on for hours, consumption rises – another point in favor of the thermal carafe.
