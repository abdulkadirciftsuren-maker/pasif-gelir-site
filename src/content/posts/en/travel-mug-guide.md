Do you take your coffee with you in the morning? A good **travel mug** keeps it hot for hours, doesn't leak in your bag and saves a lot of paper cups. This guide covers **which type** suits you, **what to look for** and **how to keep your coffee hot the longest**.

> **Short answer:** For daily use, pick a **vacuum-insulated stainless steel** mug, **12–16 oz (350–470 ml)**, with a **locking leakproof lid** that **comes apart easily** for cleaning. If you drive, make sure it **fits your cup holder**. For a whole day out or camping, a **thermos flask** is better.

## Which type suits you?

| Type | Keeps hot (approx.) | Who it's for | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Travel mug (vacuum-insulated steel)** | 3–6 hours | Commuters, driving, walking | The best choice for most people |
| **Ceramic-coated travel mug** | 3–6 hours | Anyone who cares about taste | No metallic taste, resists odors; pricier |
| **Thermos flask** | 12–24 hours | Camping, travel, all day | Big and heavy, lid doubles as a cup |
| **Reusable cup (glass/plastic)** | Short (no insulation) | Café takeaway | Light and easy to fill at a café |

*Heat retention times are approximate; every time you open the lid, they drop.*

## 8 things to check before you buy

1. **Vacuum insulation:** "Double-wall" alone isn't enough; it should be **vacuum-insulated**. With a real vacuum, the outside **stays cool** even with hot coffee inside.
2. **Leakproof:** If it goes in your bag, you need a **locking, leakproof** lid. "Spill-resistant" isn't the same as "leakproof".
3. **Lid type:** Push button (one-handed), slider or screw top. For one-handed sipping, a push-button lid is easiest.
4. **Cleaning:** Does the lid **come apart**? Do the gaskets come out? Coffee oils and milk residue build up in the lid.
5. **Size:** **12–16 oz (350–470 ml)** for daily use. For the car, the base shouldn't be wider than about **2.75–3 in (7–7.5 cm)**.
6. **Café refills:** If you buy takeaway, choose a **wide mouth** and a mug **short enough** to fit under the espresso machine.
7. **Taste:** Some people notice a slight metallic taste with steel. A **ceramic-coated** interior prevents it.
8. **Weight:** 9–14 oz (250–400 g) empty is normal. Since you'll carry it daily, lighter models are nicer.

## How to keep coffee hot the longest

1. **Preheat the mug:** Fill it with hot water for 1–2 minutes, pour it out, then add your coffee. Cold steel steals the first heat.
2. **Fill it up:** An air gap at the top makes heat escape faster.
3. **Open it less:** Every time you open it, the temperature drops.
4. **Don't pour it boiling hot:** You can't drink it that hot anyway; around 175–185 °F (80–85 °C) is ideal.
5. **Careful with milk drinks:** Drink milky coffee **within a few hours**; sitting lukewarm for long spoils the taste.

## Cleaning: no coffee smell

- **Daily:** Rinse with hot water and dish soap, **take the lid apart** and wash the gaskets.
- **Weekly:** Fill with a spoonful of **baking soda** and hot water and leave it for a few hours; coffee stains and smells come right out.
- **Dishwasher:** Many mugs have a dishwasher-safe **lid**, but **hand-washing the body** is safer; check the label.
- **Drying:** Dry the lid separately and the mug open; closed while damp, it soon smells.

## FAQ

**Can a travel mug go in the microwave?** No. Steel mugs must **never** go in the microwave.

**Why does my coffee taste metallic?** Some people notice it with steel; often, though, it's old coffee oil in the lid. Clean with baking soda or choose a **ceramic-coated** mug.

**My mug leaks. What should I do?** Take out the gasket and refit it properly. If it's worn, many brands sell **replacement gaskets**.

**Travel mug or thermos flask?** On foot or in the car during the day: a **mug**. For a whole day, camping or several people: a **flask**.
