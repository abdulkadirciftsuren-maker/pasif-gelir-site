Great coffee yesterday, bitter or sour coffee today – from the same beans? That's rarely bad luck. It's usually one of a few simple variables. The good news: you can tell what went wrong by taste and fix it step by step.

> **Short answer:** **Bitter** coffee is usually **over-extracted**: ground too fine, water too hot or brewed too long. **Sour** coffee is usually **under-extracted**: ground too coarse, water not hot enough or brewed too short. **Watery** coffee simply has too little coffee. Change **only one thing** at a time.

## What do you taste?

| Taste | What happened? | First fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bitter, dry, astringent | Over-extraction | Grind a little coarser or brew shorter |
| Sour, sharp, salty | Under-extraction | Grind a little finer or use hotter water |
| Watery, flat | Too little coffee | Use more coffee per cup |
| Bitter and sour at once | Uneven grind or channeling | Better grinder, level the coffee bed |
| Burnt, smoky | Very dark roast or hot plate | Try a lighter roast, don't keep it on the plate |
| Stale, cardboard-like | Old or open coffee | Fresh beans with a roast date |

**What is extraction?** Water dissolves flavor compounds from the grounds. Sour and fruity notes come out first, then sweet and balanced ones, and bitter, harsh ones last. Too little: sour. Too much: bitter. You're aiming for the middle.

## 7 reasons your coffee is bitter

1. **Grind too fine:** The water stays in contact too long.
2. **Water too hot:** Boiling water is usually too hot. Aim for about 92–96 °C (198–205 °F) – wait 30–60 seconds after boiling.
3. **Brewed too long:** A French press left longer than 4 minutes, or an espresso shot that runs too long.
4. **Very dark roast or Robusta:** Some beans are naturally more bitter – see [Arabica vs Robusta](/en/arabica-vs-robusta/).
5. **The hot plate:** It keeps "cooking" the coffee. A thermal carafe is better – see the [drip coffee maker guide](/en/drip-coffee-maker-guide/).
6. **A dirty machine:** Old coffee oils taste bitter. Clean the brew group and baskets regularly.
7. **Boiling Turkish coffee:** In the cezve it loses its foam and turns bitter.

## 6 reasons your coffee is sour

1. **Grind too coarse:** Water runs through too fast.
2. **Water not hot enough:** Common with cheap drip machines.
3. **Brew time too short:** An espresso shot under 20 seconds usually tastes sour.
4. **Very strong but short brews:** These can also taste sharp.
5. **Very light roast:** Naturally brighter – not a mistake, but if you don't like it, choose a medium roast.
6. **Scale:** A scaled-up machine can't reach brewing temperature – see [how to descale a coffee maker](/en/how-to-descale-a-coffee-maker/).

## Quick fixes by brew method

| Method | If bitter | If sour |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Turkish coffee](/en/how-to-make-turkish-coffee/) | Don't boil, low heat, fresh coffee | More coffee, slightly more heat |
| [V60](/en/v60-pour-over-guide/) | Grind coarser, finish by 3:30 | Grind finer, water 94–96 °C |
| [French press](/en/how-to-use-a-french-press/) | Pour off at 4 min, don't leave it in the pot | Grind finer, full 4 minutes |
| [Moka pot](/en/how-to-use-a-moka-pot/) | Start with hot water, lower the heat | Fill the basket fully, not too coarse |
| [Espresso machine](/en/home-espresso-machine-guide/) | Grind coarser, not over 30 s | Grind finer, aim for 25–30 s |
| [Drip coffee maker](/en/drip-coffee-maker-guide/) | Don't leave on the hot plate | 55–65 g per liter, descale |
| [Super-automatic](/en/super-automatic-espresso-machine-guide/) | Grind coarser, less water | Grind finer, raise the temperature |

## 5 rules for any brew method

1. **One change at a time:** Otherwise you won't know what helped.
2. **Weigh, don't scoop:** See the [coffee scale guide](/en/coffee-scale-guide/).
3. **Right ratio:** Drip coffee 55–65 g per liter (about 2 tablespoons per 6 oz) – the [coffee ratio calculator](/en/coffee-ratio-calculator/) helps.
4. **Fresh beans:** With a roast date, ground just before brewing – see [how to buy coffee beans](/en/how-to-buy-coffee-beans/) and the [grinder guide](/en/best-coffee-grinder/).
5. **Good water:** Coffee is mostly water. If your tap water is hard or tastes of chlorine, a filter helps.

## FAQ

**Do sugar or milk fix bitterness?** They mask it but don't fix the cause. Adjust grind and time first. For milk drinks, a [milk frother](/en/milk-frother-guide/) and a pump of [syrup](/en/coffee-syrup-guide/) soften the taste.

**Does a pinch of salt help?** A tiny amount of salt can reduce perceived bitterness a little. The real fix is still the brew.

**Why is my capsule coffee bitter?** Usually too much water. Don't run espresso capsules as a large cup – use lungo capsules instead. See the [capsule coffee machine guide](/en/capsule-coffee-machine-guide/).
