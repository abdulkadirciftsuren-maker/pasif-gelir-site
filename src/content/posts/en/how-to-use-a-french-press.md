The French press is the easiest and most forgiving way to make coffee at home. No special pouring technique: just combine coffee and water and wait. Still, a few small details decide whether you get a "muddy, bitter" cup or a "full, sweet" one.

> **Quick recipe:** 30 g coffee, 500 g water (1:16), coarse grind, 4 minutes steeping + 5 minutes resting.

## What you need

- A French press (350 ml or larger)
- 30 g coarsely ground coffee
- 500 g freshly boiled water
- A spoon and a scale

## Step by step

1. **Add coffee and water.** Put the grounds in the press and pour in all 500 g of hot water.
2. **Wait 4 minutes.** Put the lid on but don't press the plunger.
3. **Break the crust.** At 4 minutes, gently stir the layer of grounds on the surface – most of it sinks.
4. **Skim.** Spoon off the remaining foam and particles on top.
5. **Wait another 5 minutes.** This lets the fine dust settle to the bottom. This is the real trick.
6. **Press the plunger only to the surface** and pour slowly. Pressing all the way down stirs the sediment back up.

## Why this method works better

The classic "wait 4 minutes, press, pour" method carries lots of fine particles into the cup. Waiting a little longer and using the plunger just as a strainer makes the coffee noticeably cleaner and sweeter. The slight cooling even helps you taste the flavours.

## Common mistakes

- **Grinding too fine:** Makes it bitter and cloudy. Grind coarse – like sea salt.
- **Leaving coffee in the press:** It keeps extracting and turns bitter. Pour it all or move it to a thermos.
- **Blade grinder:** Creates lots of dust. Why we recommend a burr grinder: [How to choose a coffee grinder](/en/best-coffee-grinder/).
