A milk frother turns a simple coffee into a cappuccino or latte – no expensive espresso machine needed. There's a huge range: from small wands that cost next to nothing to electric jugs with a heating function. Which one suits you? That mainly depends on **how often** you drink milky coffee and **what kind of foam** you want.

> **Short answer:** Only now and then? A **handheld frother** is enough. A cappuccino or latte every day? An **electric frother with heating** saves time and gives consistent foam. No power and fine foam: a **manual pump frother**.

## The three types compared

| | Handheld frother | Electric with heating | Manual pump frother |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Price | Very low | Medium to high | Low to medium |
| Heating milk | Separately | Automatic | Separately (or on the stove) |
| Foam quality | Coarse, airy | Even, fine | Fine and stable |
| Cold foam | Yes | On many models | Yes |
| Effort | Low | Very low | A little muscle |
| Cleaning | Very easy | Needs to be thorough | Easy |
| Space needed | Minimal | Sits on the countertop | Small |

## Handheld frother (wand)

A small, battery-powered wand with a spiral whisk head. You heat the milk in a pan or the microwave, then froth it in a few seconds.

- **Pros:** extremely cheap, tiny, clean in an instant.
- **Cons:** the foam is fairly coarse. Fine for a classic cappuccino, but hardly suitable for silky flat white foam or latte art.
- **Tip:** Tilt the container and keep the whisk just below the surface – that way it pulls air into the milk.

## Electric milk frother with heating

A jug on a base: pour in milk, press the button, done. It heats and froths at the same time and switches off automatically. Many models use a **magnetic whisk**, and some use induction.

- **Pros:** consistent results, no boiling over, and many models also make **cold foam** for iced coffee.
- **Cons:** more expensive, takes up space and needs cleaning after every use.
- **What to look for:** capacity (foam needs a lot of room – a 300 ml jug often makes only about 150 ml of foam), a removable jug, a non-stick interior, and hot/cold programs.

## Manual pump frother

A jug with a mesh plunger that you pump up and down quickly several times. With stainless steel models you can heat the milk directly on the stove.

- **Pros:** no power, durable, surprisingly fine and stable foam.
- **Cons:** takes a little effort, and the milk has to be heated first.
- **No gadget at all?** Use the plunger of a **French press** the same way – it works on the same principle. See [How to use a French press](/en/how-to-use-a-french-press/). More options in [how to froth milk without a frother](/en/how-to-froth-milk-without-a-frother/).

## Which milk froths best?

| Milk | Foam | Taste |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Whole milk** | Creamy, stable | Full-bodied, slightly sweet |
| **Reduced-fat milk (2% or 1%)** | Lots of airy foam, collapses faster | Lighter |
| **Lactose-free milk** | Usually froths well | A little sweeter |
| **Barista oat milk** | Very good, stable | Slightly grainy |
| **Regular almond milk** | Froths, but collapses quickly | Nutty |
| **Soy milk** | Froths well, can curdle in hot coffee | Beany |

**Three rules for good foam:**

1. **Use cold milk.** Straight from the fridge it froths best.
2. **Not too hot.** About **60–65 °C (140–150 °F)** is ideal – hot enough that you can barely hold the jug. Above 70 °C (160 °F) the milk loses its natural sweetness and the foam becomes unstable.
3. **Choose plant milks labeled "barista".** They're made specifically for frothing. More in [Oat milk for coffee](/en/oat-milk-for-coffee/).

## Cappuccino, latte or flat white?

The foam decides the drink:

- **Cappuccino:** lots of airy foam.
- **Latte:** plenty of hot milk with only a thin layer of foam.
- **Flat white:** very fine, glossy microfoam and little milk.

Our guide [Latte vs cappuccino vs flat white](/en/latte-vs-cappuccino-vs-flat-white/) explains the differences with a graphic. For a coffee base without an espresso machine, a [moka pot](/en/how-to-use-a-moka-pot/) works well.

## Cleaning

- **Rinse right after use.** Dried-on milk protein is hard to remove and can smell musty.
- Electric jugs: **never submerge the base** in water; only wash the jug (if removable). Don't use scouring pads on the non-stick coating.
- Handheld frother: run the whisk briefly in warm water with a drop of dish soap, rinse, done.

## FAQ

**Can I make hot chocolate in a milk frother?** With many electric models, yes – look for a dedicated function. Add cocoa powder to the hot milk so it doesn't stick to the bottom.

**Why does my foam collapse right away?** Usually the milk got too hot, or it was a plant milk without a barista formula. Start colder and stop earlier.

**Do I need an espresso machine?** No. With a moka pot or very strongly brewed coffee and a milk frother, you can make cappuccinos and lattes at home – see [Types of coffee](/en/types-of-coffee/).
