Turkish coffee needs few ingredients but a little patience. The secret of a cup with thick foam is not expensive coffee but three simple rules: **cold water, low heat, and no stirring once it is on the stove.** This guide shows you step by step how to get it right every time – for one cup or for guests.

> **Quick recipe (1 cup):** 1 heaped teaspoon (6–7 g) Turkish coffee, 1 small coffee cup (about 70 ml) of cold water, sugar to taste. 3–4 minutes on low heat – never let it boil.

## What you need

- **Cezve:** Its size should match the number of cups. Use a small one for 1–2 cups – in a large cezve the foam spreads out. Which one to buy is covered in our [cezve buying guide](/en/how-to-choose-a-cezve/).
- **Fresh Turkish coffee:** Ground as fine as flour, and ideally used within 2–3 weeks of opening. Regular filter grind will not work.
- **Cold drinking water:** If your tap water tastes of chlorine or is very hard, use filtered water.
- **A small coffee cup, a teaspoon and a glass of water** for serving.

## Amounts and sugar levels

Use the cup itself as your measure: as many cups of coffee as you are making, that many cups of water go into the cezve.

| Coffee | Sugar (per cup) | In Turkish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Plain | No sugar | *sade* |
| Slightly sweet | ½ teaspoon | *az şekerli* |
| Medium sweet | 1 teaspoon | *orta şekerli* |
| Sweet | 2 teaspoons | *şekerli* |

Sugar always goes in **before heating**. Added later, it makes the foam collapse.

1. **Add the ingredients.** First the cold water measured with the cup, then the coffee and sugar.
2. **Stir before heating.** Stir until the coffee is fully mixed in and there are no lumps. **Don't stir again once it is on the stove** – that is the most common reason the foam collapses.
3. **Heat slowly.** Don't rush. The slower it warms, the denser and longer-lasting the foam. This takes about 3–4 minutes.
4. **Catch the foam.** When foam gathers on the surface and is about to rise over the rim, take the cezve off the heat. **Don't let it boil** – boiling destroys the foam.
5. **Share the foam.** Spoon the foam evenly into the cups.
6. **Warm it briefly again.** Put the cezve back on the heat for a few seconds until the coffee starts to rise once more.
7. **Pour slowly.** Pour the rest gently down the side of the cup so the foam stays intact. Wait a minute for the grounds to settle, then serve.

## Why is there no foam?

| Problem | Likely cause | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No foam at all | Stale coffee | Buy small packs, store airtight |
| Foam collapses quickly | The coffee boiled | Take it off as soon as the foam rises |
| Thin, coarse foam | Heat too high | Use the lowest setting, be patient |
| Foam spreads out | Cezve too big | Match the cezve to the number of cups |
| Foam destroyed | Stirred on the stove | Only stir before heating |
| Coffee too weak | Too little coffee | One heaped teaspoon per cup |

## Serving

Turkish coffee is traditionally served with **a glass of water**, drunk before the coffee to cleanse the palate. A piece of Turkish delight or a small chocolate on the side is a lovely tradition. Because the coffee is served with its grounds, you don't drink the last sips – the grounds stay at the bottom of the cup.

## Cezve or machine?

A **cezve** gives you full control and the traditional taste, but you have to stay by the stove. A **Turkish coffee machine** stops it from boiling over and gives the same result every time – handy for guests or busy mornings. ## FAQ

**Can I use regular ground coffee?** Only if it is ground as fine as powder. Normal filter grind is too coarse: it won't foam and won't settle.

**How do you make Turkish coffee with milk?** Use the same amount of cold milk instead of water. Milk boils over faster, so use very low heat and keep watching.

**Is Turkish coffee the same as espresso?** No. Both are finely ground, but Turkish coffee is cooked in a cezve and served with its grounds; espresso is brewed under pressure. More in our guide to [coffee types](/en/types-of-coffee/).
