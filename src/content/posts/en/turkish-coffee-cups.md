With Turkish coffee, the **serving** matters almost as much as the taste: a delicate cup, a small saucer, a glass of water and a piece of Turkish delight on the side. But there are lots of choices: porcelain, ceramic, glass, cups with metal holders, gold trim… This guide explains **what makes a good Turkish coffee cup**, **what set size to buy** and **what to look for in a gift**.

> **Short answer:** For everyday use, get a **set of 6 in thin, dishwasher-safe porcelain**. The cup should hold **2.5–3 oz (70–90 ml)** so coffee and foam fit without spilling. As a gift, **cups with zarf holders** or a **set for 2 with a Turkish delight dish** are always welcome. **Hand-wash** anything with gold or silver trim.

## What makes a good Turkish coffee cup?

- **Size:** One cup of Turkish coffee uses about **65–70 ml (2.2–2.4 oz)** of water. A cup with **70–90 ml** total volume leaves room for the foam. Espresso cups (demitasse) are similar in size but usually thicker and wider.
- **Shape:** A **narrow, tall** cup holds the foam better and keeps the coffee hot longer. The grounds also settle more neatly.
- **Thin rim:** Fine porcelain feels nicer on the lips.
- **Handle:** Your finger should fit through comfortably. Very small handles can get hot.
- **Saucer:** It's part of the presentation and needed if you flip the cup for fortune telling. See [Turkish coffee fortune telling](/en/turkish-coffee-fortune-telling/).

## Materials: porcelain, ceramic, glass, zarf

**What's a zarf?** In the Ottoman era, coffee cups had no handles. The hot cup sat in an ornate metal holder of silver, brass or copper called a **zarf**. Today, zarf sets are mostly bought as gifts. More on that: [history of coffee](/en/history-of-coffee/).

## Gold trim and the dishwasher

- **Gold or silver trim** tarnishes and fades in the dishwasher. **Hand-wash** these cups with a soft sponge.
- **Plain white porcelain** is usually dishwasher safe, but check the product description.
- **Microwave:** Cups with metal decoration or a zarf should **never** go in the microwave.
- **Coffee stains:** Rub brown rings gently with a little baking soda and water.

## How to serve Turkish coffee

1. **Warm the cups** with hot water first so the coffee stays hot longer.
2. Brew the coffee and spoon the foam into the cups first. Recipe: [how to make Turkish coffee](/en/how-to-make-turkish-coffee/).
3. Serve the cup **on its saucer** with a **glass of water** and a piece of **Turkish delight** or chocolate.
4. Drink the water **before** the coffee: it cleanses the palate.

## FAQ

**What size is a Turkish coffee cup?** Ideally **70–90 ml (2.5–3 oz)** total, holding about 65–70 ml of coffee plus foam.

**Can I use espresso cups for Turkish coffee?** Yes, they're a similar size. But espresso cups are usually thicker and wider, so the foam breaks up faster.

**Which cup keeps coffee hot longest?** Thin, quality **porcelain**, warmed beforehand. Glass cups hold less heat.

**What makes a good gift?** For the wow factor, a **zarf set**; for everyday use, a **porcelain set for 6**. As a small gift, a **set for 2 with a Turkish delight dish**.
