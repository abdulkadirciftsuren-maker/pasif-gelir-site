Salt in coffee? It sounds odd, but in many parts of the world it's a real tradition. This article covers **where people drink coffee with salt**, **why salt works in coffee** and **4 easy recipes** to make at home.

> **Short answer:** Yes, salt coffee is a thing: **"cà phê muối" in Vietnam** (coffee with salted cream), **sea salt coffee in Taiwan**, coffee with salt in **parts of Ethiopia**, a **Turkish marriage-proposal tradition** and the café favorite **salted caramel latte**. A pinch of salt **softens bitterness**. But the amount has to be tiny: the coffee should taste **smoother**, not salty.

## Salt coffee around the world

| Where | Drink | How? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Turkey** | The proposal coffee | The bride-to-be salts the groom's coffee as a prank |
| **Vietnam (Huế)** | Cà phê muối | Strong coffee + condensed milk + lightly salted cream on top |
| **Taiwan** | Sea salt coffee | Salted milk foam on iced coffee |
| **Ethiopia (some regions)** | Coffee with salt | A pinch of salt instead of sugar, sometimes with butter |
| **Scandinavia (old style)** | Boiled coffee | A pinch of salt to soften bitterness |
| **Cafés worldwide** | Salted caramel latte | Caramel syrup + a little salt |

### Turkey: the salty proposal coffee

One of Anatolia's most playful customs: at the "kız isteme", when the groom's family formally asks for the bride's hand, the bride-to-be makes Turkish coffee and puts **salt** (sometimes pepper) in the groom's cup. If he drinks it without making a face, he's said to be **patient and good-natured**. It's a **test and a joke**, not a treat. Turkish coffee is normally served without salt; here's how to make it right: [how to make Turkish coffee](/en/how-to-make-turkish-coffee/).

### Vietnam: cà phê muối

It comes from **Huế**, the old imperial capital, and has become popular across Vietnam and beyond. Dark-roasted coffee drips through a small metal filter called a **phin** onto **sweetened condensed milk**, and **lightly salted whipped cream** goes on top. Sweet, salty and bitter in one sip.

### Taiwan: sea salt coffee

A Taiwanese café chain made it famous: **salted milk foam** on iced coffee, sipped through the foam without stirring.

## Why does salt work in coffee?

- **It suppresses bitterness:** A small amount of sodium reduces how strongly your tongue picks up bitter flavors. That's why over-roasted or cheap coffee tastes smoother.
- **It brings out sweetness:** Sweet and salty together taste rounder (think salted caramel).
- **The dose is everything:** A **tiny pinch** per cup is enough. If you can taste the salt, it's too much.

**But:** Salt doesn't fix badly brewed coffee; it only masks it. The real causes of bitterness: [why is my coffee bitter or sour?](/en/why-is-my-coffee-bitter/)

## Recipe 1: drip coffee with a pinch of salt

The simplest way, for coffee that tastes too bitter.

1. Brew coffee as usual: drip, [French press](/en/how-to-use-a-french-press/) or moka pot.
2. Add a tiny pinch of salt **to the grounds** (before brewing) or to the **brewed coffee**: **about 0.1 g per 8 oz (250 ml)**, as much as fits between two fingertips.
3. Stir and taste. If you can taste salt, use less next time.

Note: **Never put salt in the bean hopper** of a super-automatic; it can damage the grinder and machine.

## Recipe 2: Vietnamese salt coffee (cà phê muối)

**For 1 glass:**

- 15–20 g dark-roast coffee (medium-coarse grind)
- 60–80 ml (2–2.5 oz) hot water
- 1–2 tsp sweetened condensed milk
- **Salted cream:** 50 ml (about 3 tbsp) heavy cream, 1 tsp condensed milk, **a very small pinch of salt** (about 0.5 g)

**Method:**

1. Put the **condensed milk** in the glass.
2. Set the **phin** on the glass, add the coffee, pour a little hot water over it, let it bloom for 30 seconds, then add the rest of the water and put the lid on. It drips through in 4–5 minutes.
3. **Whip the cream:** Whisk the cream, condensed milk and salt with a [milk frother](/en/milk-frother-guide/) or whisk until **thick but still pourable**.
4. Stir the coffee and **slowly pour the cream on top**. Add a few salt flakes if you like.
5. Drink it hot, or serve over ice.

**No phin?** A [moka pot](/en/how-to-use-a-moka-pot/), [AeroPress](/en/how-to-use-an-aeropress/) or a shot of espresso works too; you just want strong, dark coffee.

## Recipe 3: Taiwanese-style sea salt iced coffee

1. Fill a glass with ice and pour in **150 ml (5 oz) cold coffee** (cooled drip coffee, or espresso + cold water).
2. **Salted cold foam:** Froth 60 ml (2 oz) cold milk, 30 ml (1 oz) cream, 1 tsp sugar and **a small pinch of salt** with a handheld frother for 20–30 seconds.
3. Spoon the foam gently onto the coffee. Don't stir; sip through the foam.

More iced coffee ideas: [iced latte recipe](/en/iced-latte-recipe/).

## Recipe 4: salted caramel latte

1. Add **15 ml (½ oz) caramel syrup** and **a tiny pinch of salt** to the mug.
2. Pour in a shot of espresso (or 50 ml strong coffee) and stir.
3. Top with 200 ml (7 oz) hot frothed milk. No frother? [How to froth milk without a frother](/en/how-to-froth-milk-without-a-frother/).

Choosing a syrup and making caramel syrup at home: [coffee syrup guide](/en/coffee-syrup-guide/).

## Tips

- **Which salt?** Fine sea salt or table salt dissolves easily in coffee. **Flaky salt** is nice for sprinkling on top.
- **Which coffee?** Dark and medium-dark roasts pair best with salt. See [coffee roast levels](/en/coffee-roast-levels/).
- **Start small:** Adding salt is easy; taking it out is impossible.
- **Health:** The amount in one cup is very small. If your doctor has told you to limit salt, check with them anyway.

## FAQ

**Do you put salt in Turkish coffee?** Traditionally no, only in the proposal prank. Turkish coffee is served with or without sugar.

**Does salt make coffee unhealthy?** A pinch is a tiny fraction of daily salt intake. If you need to limit salt, still be careful.

**What does salt coffee taste like?** Done right, not salty but **smoother, less bitter and slightly sweet**. The Vietnamese version is sweet and salty at once, like salted caramel.
